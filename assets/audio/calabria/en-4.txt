Tropea and the Costa degli Dei.

A town on a cliff, a monastery on a rock, a strait with two monsters in it, and a Byzantine chapel the size of a room. The coast Calabria is known for and the mountains behind it that almost nobody sees.

Calabria's tourism is concentrated on about fifty kilometres of the Tyrrhenian coast between Pizzo and Nicotera, marketed since the 1950s as the Costa degli Dei, the coast of the gods. At its centre is Tropea, a town built along the top of a sandstone cliff with the sea directly below it, and in front of the cliff, on a rock of its own, the monastery church of Santa Maria dell'Isola. It is the most photographed thing in the region and it deserves to be.

Tropea.

The rock was an island until silt joined it to the shore, which is why the church still carries the name. There has been a religious house on it since the Byzantine period; the present building is largely medieval with later repairs, reached by a long stair cut up the rock and surrounded by a garden. The town above is a grid of narrow streets and worn palazzi with balconies, ending abruptly at viewpoints over the water, and on a clear day Stromboli is visible on the horizon, smoking. Tropea was named the most beautiful village in Italy in 2021 and belongs to the Borghi più belli d'Italia association, which in August you will feel.

Key facts.

Santa Maria dell'Isola, Tropea: a former Benedictine house on a rock that was once an island, reached by a stair from the beach.

The Strait of Messina narrows to 3.1 km between Torre Faro and Villa San Giovanni. Its currents and a whirlpool at the northern end are the origin of Scylla and Charybdis.

Cattolica di Stilo: a ninth-century Byzantine church on a Greek-cross plan with five domes, brick-built, on the slope of Monte Consolino.

Sila National Park (1997) covers 737 km² of granite plateau and forest, rising to Botte Donato at 1,928 m; Aspromonte National Park covers the mountains of the far south.

Scylla and Charybdis.

North of Reggio the coast reaches its narrowest point opposite Sicily. The Strait of Messina is 3.1 kilometres across at the neck, and the tides of two seas meet in it, running hard in opposite directions and throwing up standing waves and a whirlpool. Homer put two monsters here: Scylla on the Calabrian rock, six-headed and taking sailors off the deck, and Charybdis opposite, swallowing the sea three times a day. The rock is still called Scilla, with a Ruffo castle on top of it, and below the castle the fishermen's quarter of Chianalea is built with its feet in the water. From these waters comes the swordfish, and the boats that hunt it, with a mast for the spotter and a walkway thirty metres out over the bow for the harpooner, are a direct descendant of a fishery the Greeks described.

The Byzantine centuries.

Calabria was Byzantine long after the rest of Italy was not, and it left buildings. The Cattolica di Stilo, on the hillside above the town of Stilo, is a small ninth-century church of brick with five domes on a Greek-cross plan, four columns inside dividing a space you could cross in a few paces. Its frescoes are Byzantine and it carries Arabic inscriptions as well, which suggests it may have served both communities. Not far away, Gerace sits on a rock above the Ionian plain with the largest Norman cathedral in Calabria, and in the Aspromonte villages the Grecanico dialect is still spoken by the old.

The mountains nobody visits.

Behind the beaches the region is high and empty. The Sila is a granite plateau of pine and beech forest, lakes and cattle pasture at around 1,300 metres, cold and snowy in winter, and its national park protects 737 square kilometres of it; the tallest trees, the so-called giants of the Sila, are larch pines of great age in a fenced grove. Further south the Aspromonte rises straight out of the sea to nearly 2,000 metres in a series of terraces, with beech and fir on top and prickly pear at the bottom, and it is one of the emptiest places in Italy. Both are an hour from the coast and almost nobody on the beach goes up.

Before you move on.

The Cattolica di Stilo and Gerace are the Byzantine and Norman inheritance; the Sila and Aspromonte are high, forested and empty.
