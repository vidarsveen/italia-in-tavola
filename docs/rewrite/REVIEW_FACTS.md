# Fact review brief (one reading, fresh context)

You are checking that a rewritten reading has not lost, changed or invented facts. Style is not your
concern; another reviewer covers it. You do not rewrite anything.

You are given three things: the **old text** of the reading (the source of every fact that existed
before), the **fact ledger** the writer kept (each old fact marked kept / moved to a box / cut with a
reason, plus every new fact with its source), and the **draft**.

## Report

Three lists, each item quoting the relevant words:

1. **Lost**: a fact in the old text that is not in the draft's prose or boxes and is not in the ledger as
   cut. Quote the old sentence.
2. **Changed**: a fact that appears in both but differs (a date, a number, a name, a rule, a direction, a
   cause). Quote both.
3. **Unsupported**: a fact, name, quotation, anecdote or causal claim in the draft that is neither in the
   old text nor in the ledger with a source. Quote the draft sentence. Include softer claims: "the oldest",
   "the first", "no other", "always", "named after".

Then one line on whether the ledger's **cut** reasons are sound, naming any cut that removed something a
reader of the old text would miss.

If everything checks, say so. Return empty lists rather than manufactured doubt.

## Rules

- A fact moved from prose to a Key facts box, a tasting table or a recap counts as kept.
- A fact reworded but preserved counts as kept. Do not report rewordings.
- Do not verify facts against the outside world; you check the draft against the old text and the
  ledger. If a ledger source looks inadequate for the size of the claim (a producer's own website for
  "the oldest in the region"), say so in one line, but that is a note, not a finding.
- Do not comment on style, rhythm, structure or length.
