# Reader review brief (one reading, fresh context)

You are reviewing one reading of a narrated course on regional food and wine (Italy) or regional Indian
cooking. It will be listened to as much as read, in English and later in Norwegian. You have not seen any
other reading and you will not rewrite this one. Your job is to find what a careful, well-read listener
would object to, rank it, and propose the smallest fix for each.

You are given: the writing rules (`docs/prose-plan.md` §4, including 4d), the draft, and the opening
sentences of the two readings that precede it in the course.

## Report

A ranked list, most important first, **at most five items**, each with:

1. the sentence quoted exactly,
2. what is wrong, in one line, naming the rule it breaks if one applies,
3. a proposed replacement, or "cut".

Then one line: **anything you would praise that must not be lost in a second pass.**

If the draft holds, say so and return zero items. You are not obliged to find anything; a reviewer who
invents objections to justify the review makes the reading worse.

## Look for, in this order

- **Rhythm that has become a mannerism**: runs of clipped declaratives chained with "and", three sentences
  of the same shape, or the opposite, a run of long ones. Say where it starts.
- **Figurative agents and mannered phrases** where a literal phrase exists: a thing doing a human act
  ("the calendar did the work"), metaphors, brochure words. The owner dislikes these.
- **Overclaims**: "no other", "the only", "always", where a knowledgeable reader could name an exception.
- **Announcing instead of showing**: "worth noting", "here is the part", "this reading is about",
  "the point is", a paragraph that tells the reader the next thing is interesting.
- **A fact stated before the reader has what they need to understand it** (a name in a box before the
  prose has introduced the thing it belongs to; a number before its context).
- **Inventory paragraphs**: names as a list rather than as evidence inside a sentence with a verb.
- **The opening and the closing**: does the first sentence make you want the second; does the last
  sentence land on an image or a callback rather than a summary or advice.
- **Anything said twice**, including once in prose and once in a box.
- **Sentences over about 35 words**, and anything a listener could not follow without seeing the page.

## Do not

- Rewrite the reading, or propose changes to its structure, boxes, photographs or facts.
- Comment on the facts as facts; another reviewer checks those.
- Suggest making it "more engaging" in the abstract. Every item must quote a sentence.
- Pad the list to five.
