# Abruzzo readings 3 and 4 — research and fact-check

Research pass for the rewrite of `content/abruzzo.js` lesson 3 ("Arrosticini and the shepherd's
kitchen") and lesson 4 ("Gran Sasso and the hill fortresses"). Verdicts are CONFIRMED / CORRECTED /
UNVERIFIABLE. Every finding carries a URL and, where it matters, the source's own words.

Labels used: [producer] = a firm selling the thing; [promotional] = tourism or trade body;
[calculated] = arithmetic done here, not quoted from a source.

Note on method: the session's web-search budget ran out part way through, so the later findings were
gathered by fetching named pages directly. Gaps that this caused are listed in section D.

---

## A. Reading 3 — the shepherd's kitchen

### F1. Arrosticini are sheep or castrato, not lamb
**Claim (old text):** "cubes of sheep meat, traditionally *castrato* or older mutton rather than lamb".
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
> "carne di pecora giovane o di castrato" … "montone sottoposto a castrazione e con età compresa tra
> i sei mesi ed i due anni oppure di femmina giovane che non abbia ancora partorito"

(sheep meat, young sheep or castrated ram; a ram castrated and between six months and two years old,
or a young female that has not yet lambed)
https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini

### F2. The cube size
**Claim (old text):** not stated; the hero caption says "cubes of mutton and fat".
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and usable detail.** Cubes of about 1 cm a side, lean alternated with fat.
> "cubetti di carne di circa 1 cm di lato"

https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini
Corroborated: "ogni cubetto non superi la dimensione di 1 cm" and "alternare pezzi di carne grassa
con pezzi di carne magra" — https://www.saporinews.com/2020/08/arrosticini-di-pecora-abruzzesi-curiosita-e-ricetta/

### F3. The wooden skewer
**Verdict: CONFIRMED with a better detail than the old text has.** About 20 cm long, dialect *li
cippe* / *li cippitill*; today birch or bamboo, but the origin story specifies *vingh* (a willow-like
shrub growing along the banks of the Pescara).
> "tipicamente di betulla o bambù" … "lungo circa 20 cm"

https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini
> "inseriti su bastoncini di legno di 'vingh'" — a plant that grows wild along the Pescara river banks

https://dileonardoarrosticini.it/storia-degli-arrosticini.html [producer]
One source gives 20–30 cm skewers with meat on only the first 15 cm:
"20/30cm" … "i primi 15 cm dello spedino" — https://www.saporinews.com/2020/08/arrosticini-di-pecora-abruzzesi-curiosita-e-ricetta/

### F4. The *fornacella* / *canala*, and why it is that shape
**Claim (old text):** "a long, narrow brazier shaped like a gutter, the **fornacella**, which is
barely wider than the meat so the wooden ends stay clear of the heat".
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, including the reason.** The names are regional: *fornacella*, *furnacella*,
*rustillire*, *canala*, *canalina*.
> "forma allungata a canalina" — the elongated channel shape concentrates the heat where the meat
> sits and keeps the skewer ends cool, so they do not burn the hand or the mouth

https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini
English Wikipedia: "brazier with a typically elongated shape, called furnacella, which resembles a
gutter" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini

### F5. Eaten by pulling the meat off with the teeth
**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — and the Italian is precise enough to rewrite from.
> "sfilando uno ad uno i pezzettini di carne tenendoli stretti tra i denti e tirando verso l'esterno
> il legno dello spiedino"

(sliding the pieces off one by one, gripping them between the teeth and pulling the wooden skewer
outwards) https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini

### F6. "Bundles of twenty-five or fifty standing in a jar"; "the traditional count is fifty"
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE as written.** No source found states a traditional count of fifty, or the jar.
What is documented is a minimum serving:
> "gli arrosticini si consegnano al cliente in un cartoccio di carta stagnola e con almeno dieci
> arrosticini" (served in a foil wrap, at least ten at a time)

https://www.saporinews.com/2020/08/arrosticini-di-pecora-abruzzesi-curiosita-e-ricetta/
Recommendation: write "never fewer than ten" (sourced) or "by the dozen" (safe, and matches the brief's
own framing), and drop the fifty and the jar unless the owner has a first-hand memory of it.
The bread held under the skewer to catch the fat is likewise not in any source I could reach —
treat as folk practice, not a documented fact.

### F7. The origin story — the old text's date is WRONG
**Claim (old text):** "two shepherds in the hills between L'Aquila, Teramo and Pescara in about **1830**".
**Verdict: CORRECTED — the traditional date is the 1930s, not the 1830s.** A century out.
> "si narra infatti che essi furono inventati negli anni trenta da due pastori del Voltigno"

(it is told that they were invented in the nineteen-thirties by two shepherds of the Voltigno)
https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini
The place is the Voltigno plateau, between Civitella Casanova, Villa Celiera and Carpineto della Nora,
on the south-eastern side of the Gran Sasso where the provinces of L'Aquila, Teramo and Pescara meet.
https://dileonardoarrosticini.it/storia-degli-arrosticini.html [producer]
**Caution:** English Wikipedia carries the date **1830** — "legend claims that in 1830, two shepherds
cut up sheep meat into small pieces" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini). That is almost
certainly where the old reading's 1830 came from, and it contradicts every Italian source found,
including the regional and producer accounts, which say *anni trenta* of the twentieth century.
**Documentary basis: none.** Every source frames it as *si narra* / *la leggenda*. No names of the two
shepherds appear in any source I reached. Write it as a story the region tells, not as history.
Arrosticini are on the national list of *prodotti agroalimentari tradizionali* for Abruzzo (entry 9),
which is a real registration and can be stated as such. https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrosticini

### F8. Transhumance: the tratturi and the 111 metres — the old text understates it
**Claim (old text):** "broad grass droving roads up to a hundred metres wide".
**Verdict: CORRECTED.** The legal width was fixed at 60 Neapolitan paces = 111.6 m, not "up to a hundred".
Treccani:
> "la loro larghezza … corrisponde generalmente a 111,6 m, equivalenti a 60 passi napoletani"

https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/tratturo/
> "la misura della larghezza della sede del tracciato viario è di 111 metri, corrispondenti a sessanta
> passi napoletani" — https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tratturo

Stone *cippi* marked the edges, and violating them carried the death penalty (per the it.wikipedia
Tratturo article and https://www.movio.beniculturali.it/ascb/leeccellenzedelmolise/it/27/il-registro-dei-tratturi).

### F9. The main routes and their lengths
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**, with figures.
- L'Aquila–Foggia, the *Tratturo Magno* or *Tratturo del Re*, **243 km** — the longest
- Celano–Foggia, **207 km**
- Pescasseroli–Candela, **211 km**
- Castel di Sangro–Lucera; Centurelle–Montesecco
> "L'Aquila – Foggia (Tratturo del Re, il più lungo, 243 km)"

https://www.fidaf.it/transumanza-e-tratturi-patrimonio-dellumanita/
Network total: "circa 3.100 chilometri" (it.wikipedia Tratturo); "superava i 3000km"
(https://www.cicloamici.it/wp/2020/11/11/i-tratturi-vie-erbose/).

### F10. The Dogana della Mena delle Pecore
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Founded 1447 at Lucera by Alfonso I of Aragon; seat later moved to Foggia by
his son Ferrante; first *doganiere* Francesco Montluber.
> "Fu istituita nella città di Lucera nel 1447 da re Alfonso I"

https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogana_delle_pecore
What it taxed: the *fida*, a grazing rent on wintering flocks.
> shepherds paid **8 ducati per 100 pecore** for winter grazing; the king held the pastures from
> 9 November to 29 May (*vernotico*), the owners from 9 May to 29 September (*statonica*); the Dogana
> covered **312,800 versure**, about 385,900 ha, and could hold up to two million head

https://manganofoggia.it/gli-aragonesi-e-la-dogana-delle-pecore/

### F11. The peak numbers — a figure with a year
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
> "Nel 1592 furono censite nei pascoli del Tavoliere **4.471.496 pecore** e **9.600 animali grossi**,
> che fruttarono all'erario **622.173 ducati e 7 carlini**"

https://www.casadalena.it/La%20Dogana%20e%20i%20locati%20di%20Capracotta.htm
(same figure at https://www.letteraturacapracottese.com/post/dogana-foggia-maggiori-locati-capracotta)
Early 1600s, at the maximum expansion of pastoralism, "oltre 5 milioni di pecore" were *locate* at the
Foggia Dogana (same sources). Use the 1592 figure: it has a year, a census and a revenue attached.

### F12. When the system ended
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** The Dogana was suppressed under the French occupation of the Kingdom of Naples.
> "Fu soppressa durante l'occupazione francese del Regno di Napoli con una legge promulgata da
> Giuseppe Bonaparte il **21 maggio 1806**"

https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogana_delle_pecore
The reforms of Giuseppe Bonaparte and Gioacchino Murat, 1806–1814, ended the regime
(https://www.fidaf.it/transumanza-e-tratturi-patrimonio-dellumanita/). Under Fascism the protected
width of the tratturi was cut from 60 to 30 Neapolitan paces (it.wikipedia Tratturo). Walking
transhumance itself died out with the railways and lorries in the twentieth century; Calascio's own
account dates the local collapse to "la crisi della pastorizia transumante" of the late nineteenth
century (https://www.roccacalasciolucedabruzzo.it/aree/calascio/).
**UNVERIFIABLE here:** the 1865 law dividing the Tavoliere into farmland — I could not reach a source
for it within budget. Do not assert it.

### F13. UNESCO 2019 — correct, but there is a 2023 change to know about
**Verdict: CONFIRMED with a necessary refinement.** Transhumance was inscribed on the Representative
List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in **2019** (14.COM, Bogotá, 11 December 2019),
on a joint nomination by **Italy, Austria and Greece**. In **2023** (18.COM) the element was **extended**
to Albania, Andorra, Croatia, France, Luxembourg, Romania and Spain — which is why the UNESCO page now
shows "2023" at the top and lists ten countries.
Official title: **"Transhumance, the seasonal droving of livestock"**.
> "Previous: 14.COM 10.b.2" / "Inscription: 18.COM 8.b.14"

https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/transhumance-the-seasonal-droving-of-livestock-01470
> "La transumanza è stata inserita nel 2019 dall'UNESCO nella Lista del Patrimonio Culturale
> Immateriale" … "una candidatura transnazionale presentata da Italia, Austria e Grecia" … "Nel 2023
> l'elemento è stato esteso ad Albania, Andorra, Croazia, Francia, Lussemburgo, Romania e Spagna"

https://www.unesco.it/it/iniziative-dellunesco/patrimonio-culturale-immateriale/la-transumanza/
Date and place of the 2019 decision: "Il Comitato … riunito l'11 dicembre 2019 a Bogotà"
https://onuitalia.com/2019/12/11/transumanza/ ; also
https://www.ansa.it/europa/notizie/agri_ue/dai_territori/2019/12/23/la-transumanza-entra-nel-patrimonio-immateriale-unesco_4a3cc57b-e787-4905-877e-910005225ba2.html
Safe wording: "inscribed by UNESCO in 2019, and extended in 2023 to ten countries."

### F14. "The tratturi were Roman in origin"
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE as stated.** Treccani describes them only as tracks worn by the flocks
themselves — "vie … a fondo naturale, terroso o roccioso, formatesi per il passaggio periodico delle
greggi" (https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/tratturo/). The *legal* system is Aragonese, from 1447
(F10). Pre-Roman and Roman use of the same corridors is widely asserted but I found no archaeological
citation for it. Write "older than any record of them" or attribute the claim, and put the documented
weight on 1447.

### F15. Spaghetti alla chitarra — the tool and its first record
**Claim (old text):** "A sheet of egg pasta is laid over a wooden box strung with steel wires… the box
beneath resonates, which is why it is called a guitar."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED on the mechanism; the resonance explanation is one of two, and the second is
better sourced.** The frame is beech strung with parallel steel wires; the name is also explained
simply by the wires looking like guitar strings.
> "a rectangular beech wood frame fitted with parallel steel wires" … "The strings laid on the frame
> resemble the strings of a guitar, another reason behind the name 'chitarra'"

https://www.gamberorossointernational.com/news/spaghetti-alla-chitarra-history-and-recipe-of-the-abruzzo-dish/
**First documentation — a precise, usable fact the old text lacks:** the chitarra appears in a
notary's inventory of a bride's dowry at **Teramo in 1871**.
> "the tool was first recorded by a notary as part of a bride's dowry in 1871"

Meryl Feinstein, Pasta Social Club, 5 September 2025 —
https://pastasocialclub.substack.com/p/spaghetti-alla-chitarra-abruzzos
The claim "first documented in the early nineteenth century" (in the brief) is therefore **CORRECTED**
to 1871, late nineteenth century, with the tool certainly older in use. Its predecessor was *lu
rentrocelo* / *rintrocilo*, a grooved rolling pin from the Pescara and L'Aquila areas (same source).

### F16. The dough
**Verdict: CORRECTED in emphasis.** Always egg; the flour varies — soft wheat "00", durum semola
rimacinata, or both. The old text's "egg pasta" is right; a claim that it is specifically egg-and-durum
is only sometimes true.
> "made equally with soft wheat ('00', all-purpose) and hard wheat (semolina, semola rimacinata)
> flours—sometimes one, sometimes the other, and sometimes both"

https://pastasocialclub.substack.com/p/spaghetti-alla-chitarra-abruzzos
The square section and the rough surface that holds sauce are confirmed in the same source.

### F17. *Pallottine*, and whether the dish is specifically from Teramo
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Teramo, and it is the one traditional Italian pasta-and-meatballs.
> "In Teramo, one of the most popular preparations is spaghetti alla chitarra con pallottine, Italy's
> only traditional iteration of spaghetti and meatballs" … "mini meatballs no bigger than a hazelnut,
> made with bread, cheese, and mixed meats"

https://pastasocialclub.substack.com/p/spaghetti-alla-chitarra-abruzzos and
https://experiencebellavita.com/abruzzo-recipe-chitarra-teramana-con-pallottine/
Meat: beef or veal, sometimes mixed, bound with bread, cheese, egg, nutmeg. Hazelnut size confirmed.

### F18. Scrippelle 'mbusse — name, broth, cheese
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** *'Mbusse* means soaked/wet; thin crêpes rolled with grated cheese and covered
with hot chicken or capon broth flavoured with celery, onion, clove and cinnamon. Pecorino is
traditional; some cooks use Parmigiano.
https://www.ilgiornaledelcibo.it/scrippelle-mbusse/
Etymology offered for *scrippelle*: from the French "crêpe s'appelle" adapted into dialect — note this
is folk etymology, presented as such by the sources.

### F19. The Napoleonic origin story
**Claim (brief/old text):** a French soldier dropping crêpes into broth during the Napoleonic occupation.
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE — explicitly a legend, and the person named is not a soldier.** The story names
**Enrico Castorani**, a Teramano working as assistant to the cook of the French officers garrisoned in
Teramo during the French decade in the Kingdom of Naples (first decade of the nineteenth century), who
dropped a tray of crêpes into the broth and served it anyway.
> the source introduces it as "la leggenda, infatti, narra che" and frames the origin as legendary
> rather than documented

https://www.ilgiornaledelcibo.it/scrippelle-mbusse/
The only documented context is the "asse culturale tra Napoli e Parigi" of the Bourbon and French
years. Write it as a story Teramo tells, and name Castorani rather than an anonymous soldier.

### F20. Brodetto alla vastese — the pot, the rule, and the boundary with Marche
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, from what the sources call the municipal recipe.** Vasto's tourism department
codified the recipe in 2011 and registered it with the Chieti chamber of commerce.
- The vessel: "la bassa e larghissima teglia (la tijèlle) di tradizionale terracotta" — a low, very
  wide traditional terracotta pan.
- Everything starts cold: water, oil, strips of sweet green pepper, parsley, garlic, salt; then
  tomatoes; then the fish in order of how well each stands up to cooking.
- The peppers: "un grosso peperone verde dolce e fresco"; the tomatoes "pomodori 'mezzitempi'".
- The fish: "gallinella, assieme alla cicala di mare, a tràcina, scorfano, triglia, merluzzo, piccola
  seppia, sogliola e infine alla razzetta".
- The rule: "La cottura, a coperchio fisso, si protrae per altri 15/20 minuti, **senza toccare affatto**
  il delizioso intingolo ed i suoi contenuti" — lid on, and nothing touched at all.
https://cucinaregionaleitaliana.it/portfolio-items/brodetto-di-pesce-alla-vastese-ricetta-tradizionale/

**What is different from the Marche brodetti** (this matters because the Marche reading already covers
them in detail):
| | tomato | vinegar | peppers | other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fano | tomato concentrate | yes | no | no blue fish, clams or mussels |
| Ancona | "moltissimo pomodoro", the densest | yes | no | at least thirteen fish |
| Porto Recanati | none | yes | no | **saffron**; the oldest style |
| San Benedetto del Tronto | none ("bianco") | "tanto aceto" | **green peppers** + unripe green tomato | |
| **Vasto** | **yes, ripe *mezzitempi*** | **no** | **sweet green pepper** | pasta cooked in the leftover sauce afterwards |
Source for the Marche four: https://www.ilgiornaledelcibo.it/brodetto-marchigiano/
So the boundary runs at the Tronto: **the Marche brodetti are all vinegar dishes; the Vastese is not.**
San Benedetto, the last Marche port before the border, shares the green pepper but is white and sharp
with vinegar; Vasto keeps the pepper, adds ripe tomato and leaves the vinegar out. The second Vastese
marker: "il brodetto alla vastese è l'unico posto che prevede anche l'aggiunta della pasta… spesso i
sottilissimi capelli d'angelo" — the leftover sauce goes back to the kitchen and pasta is cooked in it.
Ancona's difference is stated directly: "Il brodetto all'anconetana differisce da quello vastese
soprattutto perché sfumato con aceto."
https://www.scattidigusto.it/brodetto-di-pesce-alla-vastese-ricetta-originale

### F21. Pecorino di Farindola — the pig's rennet
**Claim (old text):** "one of the very few cheeses in the world set with pig's rennet".
**Verdict: CONFIRMED as phrased (the hedge is correct); the stronger claim in the brief — "the only
known cheese in Europe made with pork rennet" — is NOT supported by the Slow Food source and should
not be written flat.**
- Slow Food Foundation says only that it is made with pig's rennet and that the practice is ancient;
  **no claim of uniqueness anywhere in their text**: "made with pig's rennet, which gives it a
  distinctive smell and flavor"; "Vestini cheese, made with pig's rennet, already known in Roman times".
  https://www.fondazioneslowfood.com/en/slow-food-presidia/farindola-pecorino/
- Regional tourism: the rennet "rende unico questo formaggio in Italia e forse nel mondo" — unique in
  Italy and perhaps in the world. (Regione Abruzzo page; quoted via search result, see D2.)
- Italian Wikipedia goes furthest: "probabilmente l'unico formaggio al mondo prodotto con l'aggiunta
  del caglio suino". https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Farindola
- Consumer press: "l'unico con caglio suino" [promotional] —
  https://www.ilcuocoincamicia.com/pecorino-di-farindola/
**Recommendation:** "one of the very few cheeses anywhere set with pig's rennet, and as far as anyone
has established the only one in Italy" is defensible. "The only in Europe" is not.

### F22. Farindola — how the rennet is made, and the women
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with the best detail of the whole reading.** The women make the rennet themselves
at the winter pig-killing:
> "in occasione della macellazione del suino nel periodo invernale, prelevano la parte interna dello
> stomaco che viene lavata e messa ad essiccare per poi essere conservata con sale, aceto e vino bianco
> per circa quaranta giorni prima di essere filtrata"

(at the winter killing they take the lining of the stomach, wash it, dry it, then keep it in salt,
vinegar and white wine for about forty days before straining it)
https://www.gazzettadelgusto.it/cibo/pecorino-di-farindola-abruzzese/
Why the women: "prodotto da sempre in casa dalle massaie mentre gli uomini erano impegnati nei pascoli
con il bestiame" — made at home by the women of the house while the men were out at pasture with the
animals. Hence "il pecorino delle donne". Same source.
Slow Food: "is produced by women, who hand down the recipe from generation to generation."
Ageing 3 to 12 months, rubbed daily and washed with oil and vinegar (Gazzetta del Gusto).

### F23. Farindola — status and figures
**Verdict: partly CONFIRMED, partly UNVERIFIABLE.**
- Status: **PAT** (regional traditional product) and **Slow Food Presidium**. https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_di_Farindola
  It is **not DOP**. Do not write DOP.
- Producers in the Presidium: **seven**. https://www.fondazioneslowfood.com/en/slow-food-presidia/farindola-pecorino/
- Area: Farindola and neighbouring comuni of Pescara and Teramo provinces (the eastern slope of the
  Gran Sasso, largely inside the national park); one source narrows it to "tra Farindola ed Arsita".
  https://www.storienogastronomiche.it/il-pecorino-di-farindola-delizia-di-origini-romane-oggi-presidio-slow-food-dallabruzzo/
- **Annual production figures: UNVERIFIABLE.** No source I reached gives wheels or kilograms. Slow Food
  says only that production "almost disappeared completely in the 1990s"; others say "quantità
  limitatissime". Write "a few hundred wheels" ONLY if the owner has a figure; otherwise say the
  Presidium has seven producers, which is itself the scale.
- **Presidium start date: UNVERIFIABLE.** Not given on the Slow Food page or anywhere I could reach.

### F24. Ventricina
**Verdict: CONFIRMED and sharper than the old text.** Vastese style: about 80% lean pork and the rest
fat, cut with the point of a knife into irregular cubes of **3–4 cm**, seasoned with dried peppers,
fennel flower or seed, salt and pepper, stuffed into bladder or gut, aged at least **100 days** below
13 °C. PAT and Slow Food Presidium.
> "tagli nobili (magri) di carne suina per circa l'80%" … "tagliata a punta di coltello a formare
> cubetti irregolari di circa 3–4 cm"

https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventricina
Do not confuse with **ventricina teramana**, which is spreadable, 60–70% fat, flavoured with orange
peel, garlic, pepper paste and rosemary, and aged only weeks — same source. The old text's comparison
with 'nduja is a judgement, not a fact; if kept, note that the *teramana* is the spreadable one, so the
'nduja comparison actually fits Teramo better than Vasto.

### F25. Navelli saffron — the friar and the century (old text is off, and the sources disagree)
**Claim (old text):** "a Dominican friar brought the crocus back from Spain in the **fourteenth** century".
**Verdict: CORRECTED — the usual date is the thirteenth century, and the sources disagree among
themselves.** Whatever is written must be hedged as tradition.
- Italian Wikipedia: a Dominican of the **Santucci** family of Navelli brought it from Spain in the
  **13th century** (XIII secolo). https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafferano_dell%27Aquila
- Il Giornale del Cibo: the monk brought it "**around 1230**", after the Council of Toledo.
  https://www.ilgiornaledelcibo.it/zafferano-laquila/
- WineNews (reporting the consortium's own twentieth-anniversary event): "**12th century**".
  https://winenews.it/it/quando-una-dop-e-volano-del-territorio-laquila-ripercorre-la-storia-dello-zafferano_567148/
- Slow Food Abruzzo: "secondo la tradizione, sembra sia stata introdotta a Navelli … da un monaco di
  ritorno dalla Spagna, Santucci" — explicitly tradition, no century.
  https://www.slowfoodabruzzo.it/mappa-prodotti-e-comunita-abruzzo/zafferano-dellaquila/
**Recommendation:** "a Dominican of the Santucci family is said to have brought the bulbs back from
Spain in the thirteenth century" — attributed, thirteenth not fourteenth.

### F26. Saffron — flowers per kilo. The old text's figure is low
**Claim (old text):** "roughly 150,000 flowers to make a kilo".
**Verdict: CORRECTED.** The figures in the sources are higher.
- Italian Wikipedia: "approximately **200,000** flowers are required to produce one kilogram".
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafferano_dell%27Aquila
- The consortium's own anniversary reporting: "**250,000**" flowers per kilo.
  https://winenews.it/it/quando-una-dop-e-volano-del-territorio-laquila-ripercorre-la-storia-dello-zafferano_567148/
Write 200,000, or "between two and two and a half hundred thousand".
Picking before the flowers open, by hand, in the dark, is confirmed: "attorno alla seconda quindicina
di ottobre prima della loro schiusa all'alba" (it.wikipedia), and Slow Food: collection "exclusively
manual", "in the early morning hours, before the sun opens [the flowers]". Three red stigmas per
flower, separated by hand, then toasted over almond or oak embers (it.wikipedia) — the toasting is a
detail the old text does not have and is worth using.

### F27. Saffron — DOP, area, and what is produced now

### F41. Rocca Calascio — the old text's earthquake is the wrong one
**Claim (old text):** "began as a single stone watchtower in the tenth century, was given walls and
corner towers in the thirteenth, and stood at 1,460 metres… An earthquake in 1461 destroyed much of
it… the castle was left as it fell."
**Verdict: CORRECTED. Two earthquakes, and the order matters.**
- **1461** damaged the settlement and the fortress, and it was **rebuilt afterwards**: "il terremoto del
  1461" half-destroyed the urban nucleus, after which **Antonio Piccolomini** built the fortified
  circuit. https://www.istitutoitalianocastelli.it/castelli-ditalia/rocca-calascio/
- **Antonio Todeschini Piccolomini**, granted the fief by King Ferdinand (**1463/64**), "rafforzò la
  fortificazione dotandola di una cerchia muraria in ciottolame e quattro torri di forma cilindrica" —
  the four round corner towers are **fifteenth-century**, not thirteenth. https://www.roccacalascio.info/informazioni-rocca-calascio/la-storia/
  It.wikipedia: in 1463 Ferdinand I granted it to Antonio Todeschini Piccolomini, who "commissionò
  l'adattamento della fortificazione alle armi da fuoco" — adapting it for firearms.
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocca_Calascio
- **1703** is the earthquake that finished it: "Nel 1703 un altro terremoto distrusse il castello e il
  paese sottostante" (Istituto Italiano Castelli); "il terremoto tornò a colpire nel 1703, devastando il
  castello" (https://www.vivicalascio.com/la-rocca/). The **2 February 1703** shock, Candlemas day,
  epicentre a few kilometres from L'Aquila, destroyed the upper village and gutted the fortress. After
  it "buona parte della popolazione si trasferì nel vicino paese di Calascio"
  (https://www.roccacalascio.info/informazioni-rocca-calascio/la-storia/).
- So: tenth-century watchtower (traditional date; first document 1380), fifteenth-century Piccolomini
  rebuilding after 1461, ruined by 1703. **The old text's "wrecked by the 1461 earthquake and never
  rebuilt", in both the prose and the facts box, is wrong.**

### F43. Rocca Calascio — the abandonment, and what the village lived on [section B material]
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and this is the human detail the brief asked for.**
- **1474: the barony held more than 90,000 sheep.** "Nel 1474, la baronia possedeva oltre 90.000
  pecore" and "la lana di Calascio era rinomata anche fuori regione" — Calascio's wool was known well
  beyond the region. https://www.roccacalasciolucedabruzzo.it/aree/calascio/
- The fortress sat on the sheep road and housed a **customs post for the flocks going down to Puglia**:
  "Renaissance phase: a customs station was established for sheep herding to Puglia"
  (https://www.vivicalascio.com/la-rocca/). The castle was not only a watchtower; it was a toll gate on
  the wool economy.
- The Piccolomini took the barony in **1464**, the Medici bought it in **1579**, opening "un periodo di
  massimo sviluppo economico e culturale" (same source).
- The end: the 1703 earthquake sent most people down to Calascio; the 1915 (Avezzano) earthquake
  "ne aveva ormai segnato il declino inesorabile e il paese venne lentamente abbandonato, tanto che nel
  **1957** non contava più neppure un abitante" — by 1957 not one inhabitant left. Istituto Italiano
  Castelli; it.wikipedia gives the same year: "completamente disabitato nel 1957".
- The underlying cause, stated plainly by the comune's own site: "la crisi della pastorizia
  transumante" from the late nineteenth century, then emigration to North America and the industrial
  cities from the second half of the 1800s.
**So the answer to "when did the last inhabitants leave" is 1957**, and the chain is: wool → Dogana →
earthquake → emigration → empty.

  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_nazionale_del_Gran_Sasso_e_Monti_della_Laga
- **Maiella:** identified by the same 1991 law, authority constituted by **DPR 5 giugno 1995**.
  https://www.parcomajella.it/DPR-Istituzione-Ente-Parco-Nazionale-della-Majella.htm
The old text's "created in 1991" for Gran Sasso is right as far as it goes.

### F50. The Gran Sasso tunnel and the INFN laboratory
**Verdict: CONFIRMED; not currently in the reading, and worth having.**
- Built **1982–85**, fully operational **1987** with the MACRO experiment; sited to exploit "excavations
  already underway for the construction of the motorway tunnel under the Gran Sasso massif"; about
  **1,400 m of rock** overhead, some 3,400 metres water equivalent of shielding; 120 km from Rome;
  OPERA and ICARUS received a neutrino beam from CERN 730 km away.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratori_Nazionali_del_Gran_Sasso
- Why underground: the rock filters out cosmic rays, so the detectors can see neutrinos and look for
  dark matter. (same)
- **The 2002 incident is well documented, and the chemical is not trichloroethane.** On **16 August
  2002**, errors in Hall C released **50 litres of trimethylbenzene (pseudocumene)** from the BOREXINO
  experiment into the environment. In **October 2002** the Teramo prosecutor placed the detector under
  sequestration; the drainage of the lab and of the motorway tunnel was found not to be leak-tight,
  raising fears for the aquifer that supplies drinking water; INFN suspended all fluid handling on
  **5 June 2003**; the government declared a state of environmental emergency for the whole Gran Sasso
  facility on **27 June 2003**; by September 2003 the court allowed work to resume.
  https://cerncourier.com/a/gran-sasso-puts-its-troubles-in-order/
  and https://www.science.org/content/article/chemical-spills-put-italy-s-underground-physics-lab-jeopardy
  The mountain that feeds the flocks also holds the drinking water and a particle physics lab: that is a
  real, sourced tension if the reading wants it.

### F51. L'Aquila — the ninety-nine
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with the legend flagged as a legend.**
- Foundation: "Il 20 maggio **1254** l'imperatore **Corrado IV** di Svevia promulgò un atto concedendo
  agli abitanti il permesso di costruire una nuova grande città di nome 'Aquila'"; destroyed by Manfredi
  in 1259, rebuilt from **1266** under Charles I of Anjou. https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Aquila
- The ninety-nine castles founded it; each was to contribute a church, a piazza and a fountain; the
  civic bell tower strikes **99 times** each evening. (same)
- **Fontana delle 99 Cannelle:** built **1272**, inscribed "ANNO DOMINI MCCLXXII MAGISTER TANCREDUS DE
  PENTOMA DE VALVA FECIT HOC OPUS"; **93 carved mask-heads plus six further spouts**; the water source
  was kept secret so no castle could claim it, and legend says the architect was killed to keep it so.
  **Scholars suspect the legend runs backwards** — the number of castles may have been fixed by the
  number of spouts, not the other way round. Reopened after restoration **16 December 2010**.
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontana_delle_99_cannelle
So: write "ninety-nine" as the city's organising myth, and note that the fountain has ninety-three
faces and six spouts.

### F52. Collemaggio, Celestine V and the Perdonanza
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Celestine V was crowned in **Santa Maria di Collemaggio on 29 August 1294**;

the title **"Celestinian forgiveness celebration"**.
> "the tradition originated when Pope Celestine V issued a historical 'Bull' as an act of partnership
> among local populations" (1294); the celebration includes a candlelit procession, the "Fire of
> Morrone", tripods lit across twenty-three villages, and about 1,000 people in procession

https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/celestinian-forgiveness-celebration-01276
https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perdonanza_Celestiniana
Both the transhumance and the Perdonanza were inscribed at the same 2019 committee session — a neat,
true pairing for a reading that runs from the sheep roads to the city.

### F53. The 2009 earthquake

## C. Material for the rewrite (section B of the brief)

### F54. A named drover with documented numbers — **Mattia Pizzella of Capracotta**
The Dogana's registers name individual *locati* year by year. One is worked through in detail:
> **Mattia Pizzella**, aged **52 in 1743**, registered "**2.640 pecore e 178 animali grossi**"; the
> family's holding grew until "i Pizzella possedevano **10.000 capi**" around **1750**, then fell back
> to where it started by **1780**.

https://www.letteraturacapracottese.com/post/dogana-foggia-maggiori-locati-capracotta
(also https://www.casadalena.it/La%20Dogana%20e%20i%20locati%20di%20Capracotta.htm and
https://www.amicidicapracotta.com/2014/02/27/i-maggiori-locati-capracottesi-della-dogana-di-foggia-dal-1600-al-1800/)
A man of fifty-two, a name, a number of sheep, a rise and a fall inside forty years — everything the
opening needs, and it comes from the tax registers rather than from folklore.
Other named holders in the same registers, for scale:
- **Cappella della Madonna di Loreto**, 21,210 head in **1700**, 17,980 in **1750** — a chapel, running
  twenty thousand sheep.
- **dr. Giacomo Antonio del Baccaro**, 16,500–16,900 head in 1700.
- **the Duke of Capracotta**, 14,900 in 1700.
The registers exist because the Dogana taxed them: the sheep were counted because they were taxed.
Capracotta is in Molise, on the L'Aquila–Foggia tratturo, so this is on the road the reading describes.

### F55. What the walk actually was
- **7 to 10 shepherds per 1,000 sheep**, plus cheesemakers, muleteers and dog handlers.
  "Ogni 1000 pecore si ritenevano necessari dai 7 ai 10 pastori"
  https://www.cicloamici.it/wp/2020/11/11/i-tratturi-vie-erbose/
- **About two weeks** on the road: the migration "durava in media 2 settimane" (same source).
- Season: down from October, back in May — "da ottobre a maggio"; rests beside water, up to three
  nights in one place; flocks had to number more than twenty head to be registered as *locati*.
  https://www.fidaf.it/transumanza-e-tratturi-patrimonio-dellumanita/
- Treccani: the flocks went down in September and returned to the mountains in June.
  https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/transumanza/
- Millions of animals moved on the tratturi "senza sconfinamenti nei terreni adiacenti" — without
  straying onto the fields either side, which is what the 111 metres were for.
  https://www.fidaf.it/transumanza-e-tratturi-patrimonio-dellumanita/
**A cheesemaker walked with the flock.** That is the documented link between the road and the dairy,
and it is the honest way into the cheese paragraph.

### F56. What the shepherds cooked — *pecora alla callara*
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and it is exactly the food-on-the-road material the brief asked for.**
- The name is the pot: brass or copper cauldrons hung from a tripod over the fire — "paioli di ottone
  sorretti da un treppiede ed un gancio sopra il fuoco, chiamati appunto 'cottore'".
  https://www.parchilazio.it/cammino_naturale_dei_parchi-schede-6963-ricette_in_cammino_pecora_alla_callara
- What went in it: the animals that died or went lame on the road. The dish was born "quando, lungo il
  percorso dagli Abruzzi al Tavoliere delle Puglie, i pastori consumavano le pecore morte per la fatica
  o quelle azzoppate o ferite, cuocendole negli appositi paioli di rame" — sheep that died of
  exhaustion, or went lame, cooked in copper pots on the road from Abruzzo to the Tavoliere, on the
  Regio Tratturo from L'Aquila to Foggia.
  https://www.cibotoday.it/video/video-ricetta-pecora-alla-callara-abruzzo.html and
  https://www.chietitoday.it/cucina/pecora-alla-callara-ricetta-originale-abruzzese.html
- The cooking: **four to six hours**, because old sheep is tough — an hour and a half of first boiling,
  then about three hours more. Water, tomato, potato, onion, celery, carrot, bay, rosemary, thyme,
  chilli, wine. (parchilazio, as above)
This is the mirror image of arrosticini and makes the pair of dishes legible: the callara is the animal
that could not go on, cooked all day; arrosticini are the scraps, cooked in three minutes. Both are the
same economy — nothing of a sheep is wasted, and the meat you eat is the meat you could not sell.

### F57. The wool economy, end to end
The chain the two readings share, all sourced above:
1. **Summer**: the flocks on Campo Imperatore, at Castel del Monte, Calascio, Santo Stefano (F38).
2. **The barony's scale**: over **90,000 sheep** in 1474 (F43); Calascio wool known outside the region.
3. **The prize product**: *lana carfagna* from Santo Stefano di Sessanio, worked in Florence and sold
   across Europe under the Medici, 1579–1743 (F46). The Medici bought the barony for 106,000 scudi.
4. **The road**: 111.6 m wide, 243 km from L'Aquila to Foggia, about two weeks' walk (F8, F9, F55).
5. **The toll**: the Dogana at Foggia from 1447, 8 ducats per hundred sheep; in 1592, 4,471,496 sheep
   counted and 622,173 ducats collected (F10, F11).
6. **The end**: the Dogana abolished 21 May 1806; the crisis of transhumant pastoralism from the late
   nineteenth century; emigration to America and the northern cities; Rocca Calascio empty by 1957
   (F12, F43, F46).
**What I could NOT source:** a price for Abruzzo wool, the names of the Florentine buyers, and a date
for the collapse of the wool price itself. See D5. Do not invent a figure for the wool.

### F58. Candidate openings, ranked
1. **Mattia Pizzella, 1743** (F54): a named man of fifty-two, 2,640 sheep and 178 cattle entered in the
   Foggia register; his family at 10,000 head by 1750 and back to 2,640 by 1780. Opens on a person, in a
   document, and the number itself carries the whole economy.
2. **The callara on the tratturo** (F56): a copper pot on a tripod, a sheep that could not finish the
   walk, four hours of fire. Opens on something happening, and lands directly on the table.
3. **The 1592 count** (F11): four and a half million sheep written down in one year on the Tavoliere,
   because each one was taxed at eight ducats the hundred. The scene is the counting, not the sheep.
4. **The women of Farindola at the winter pig-killing** (F22): taking the lining of the stomach, washing
   it, drying it, keeping it forty days in salt, vinegar and white wine — and that is why the cheese
   tastes as it does, and why it is the women's cheese.
5. **The corridor under the snow at Campo Imperatore** (F39): a cable car built 1931–34 and a tunnel
   from its upper station into the hotel, so that in winter nobody crossed the open mountain to eat.
   For reading 4, and it carries Mussolini's imprisonment without making him the subject.
6. **Nuremberg's merchants opening an office in L'Aquila about 1513** to buy saffron without going
   through Venice (F28) — for the saffron paragraph, and it explains the price better than any adjective.
