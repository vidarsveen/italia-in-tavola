# Abruzzo — research pass for the two wine readings (1: Montepulciano; 2: Cerasuolo)

Research done 2026-09-16 for the rewrite of `content/abruzzo.js` readings 1 and 2. Claims extracted from the
existing text; verdicts CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / UNVERIFIABLE. Labels: **[producer]** the estate's or importer's
own account, **[promotional]** consortium/marketing, **[calculated]** arithmetic of mine.

## Part A — verification

### W1. Montepulciano the grape vs Vino Nobile di Montepulciano the wine
**Claim (reading 1, lead):** two Italian wines are called Montepulciano and have nothing in common; Vino Nobile
comes from a Tuscan hill town and is Sangiovese; the Abruzzo grape is not grown around that town; "nobody has
explained the name."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**, and the "nobody has explained" line can be sharpened.
The Italian reference works state the disconnection flatly: «il vitigno Montepulciano non ha nessun legame con
l'omonima città toscana» ("the Montepulciano grape has no connection with the Tuscan town of the same name"),
and the Tuscan wine's name is traced to Sante Lacerio, Pope Paul III's *bottigliere*, in the sixteenth century,
for a wine of Sangiovese.
URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montepulciano_(vitigno) — *Wikipedia is a lead, not a source; but the
substance is corroborated by the official disciplinare's own history (W2), which places the grape in Abruzzo and
says nothing of Tuscany.*
The confusion is explained there as ampelographic: «similitudine di alcune caratteristiche ampelografiche»
(similarity of some ampelographic characteristics) between what was grown in the two places.
**What cannot be known:** no source found this session offers a documented etymology of the grape's name. Write
it as unexplained, not as "named after the town."

### W2. The first documented mention of the grape — Michele Torcia, 1792
**Claim:** implied by the brief; the old text does not carry it.
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**, and it is in the appellation's own legal text, which is the best possible place for it.
Article 9 / the historical section of the Montepulciano d'Abruzzo DOC disciplinare:
> «la prima notizia storica sulla presenza del vitigno Montepulciano in Abruzzo è contenuta nell'opera di
> Michele Torcia dal titolo *Saggio Itinerario Nazionale pel Paese dei Peligni* fatto nel 1792»
("the first historical notice of the presence of the Montepulciano vine in Abruzzo is contained in the work of
Michele Torcia, *Saggio Itinerario Nazionale pel Paese dei Peligni*, made in 1792").
The same section then tracks the grape through Panfilo Serafini (1854) — «Le viti più comuni sono il
montepulciano, sia primaticcio, sia cordisco o tardivo» ("the commonest vines are the montepulciano, both the
early-ripening and the *cordisco* or late one") — Andrea Vivenza's *Brevi norme per fare il vino* (1867), and
Ottavi & Marescalchi's *Vade-Mecum del commerciante di uve e di vini in Italia* (first edition 1897).
URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/montepulciano-dabruzzo-doc-proposta-modifica-disciplinare.html
The same Torcia 1792 reference appears in the Colline Teramane DOCG disciplinare's historical notes, which adds
the nineteenth-century expansion toward the Pescara valley by the Mezzana and Tabassi families.
URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/montepulciano-dabruzzo-colline-teramane-docg.html
**Useful for the rewrite:** Serafini's 1854 line is the earliest *Abruzzese* voice quoted, and it already
distinguishes two Montepulcianos, *primaticcio* (early) and *cordisco* (late) — a real detail, in period words.

### W3. Is Montepulciano one of Italy's most planted red grapes?
**Claim:** "one of Italy's most planted."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance; the exact figure is disputed between sources, so give a range or a rank.**
- Second among **red** varieties nationally, «circa 34.800 ha», «circa 5,3%» of the Italian vineyard, on 2010
  census data. URL: https://www.viviilvino.it/articoli/vitigni-rossi-italiani-piu-coltivati-classifica-caratteristiche-e-diffusione/
- Fourth among **all** varieties (behind Sangiovese ~10%, Trebbiano ~9.6%, Catarratto ~8.7%), 2015 data.
  URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/articoli/superficie-vitata-in-italia-suddivisa-per-vitigno/
- 30,700 ha, concentrated in Abruzzo (56%) and Puglia (19%), authorised in Abruzzo, Marche, Molise and Puglia;
  no year given. URL: https://italianwinecentral.com/variety/montepulciano/
- In Abruzzo itself the consortium says Montepulciano «rappresenta più della metà della base ampelografica
  regionale» ("represents more than half the regional ampelographic base"), about 17,000 ha, with over 70% of
  new plantings. URL: https://www.vinidabruzzo.it/territorio/ **[promotional]**
**Safe wording:** "second only to Sangiovese among Italy's red grapes" (2010 census), or "more than half of
Abruzzo's vineyard." Do not print a single national hectare figure as current: the two credible ones differ by
4,000 ha and neither is newer than 2015.
**Flagged disagreement:** 34,800 ha (2010) vs 30,700 ha (undated) vs "fourth overall" (2015).

### W4. Montepulciano d'Abruzzo DOC — date, zone, percentage, alcohol, Riserva
**Claim:** "DOC (1968): at least 85% Montepulciano, minimum 12% alcohol. Riserva needs 12.5% and two years of
ageing, at least nine months of it in wood."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, every element.**
- Decree: «Approvata DOC con D.P.R. 24.05.1968 G.U. 178 – 15.07.1968»; latest amendment D.M. 19.01.2023,
  G.U. 30 del 06.02.2023. URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/montepulciano-d-abruzzo-doc/
- Grapes: «deve essere ottenuto dalle uve provenienti da vigneti che nell'ambito aziendale risultano composti
  dal vitigno Montepulciano per almeno all'85%», balance up to 15% other non-aromatic black grapes.
- Zone: all four provinces — Chieti, L'Aquila, Pescara, Teramo.
- Alcohol: base «titolo alcolometrico volumico totale minimo: 12,00% vol»; Riserva «12,50% vol».
- Riserva ageing: «deve essere sottoposto a un periodo di Invecchiamento di almeno 24 mesi di cui almeno 9 in
  recipienti di legno» (at least 24 months, of which at least 9 in wooden vessels).
URLs: https://www.disciplinare.it/montepulciano-dabruzzo-doc-proposta-modifica-disciplinare.html and
https://www.assovini.it/italia/abruzzo/item/102-montepulciano-d-abruzzo-doc
- Maximum yield: 14–15 t/ha of grapes for the base DOC, «la resa massima dell'uva in vino non dovrà essere
  superiore al 70%»; the Casauria subzone is cut to 9.5 t/ha and 13% vol.
  URL: https://www.greatitalianfoodtrade.it/vino-italiano/montepulciano-dabruzzo/
- Altitude limit and the geography, from the disciplinare's own link-to-place section: «L'altitudine dei
  terreni coltivati a vite può raggiungere i 700 metri s.l.m., con pendenze ed esposizioni variabili» and the
  climate is attributed to «alla presenza del mare Adriatico ad est e dalla vicinanza dei massicci del Gran
  Sasso e della Maiella ad ovest». Permitted training: «pergola abruzzese e spalliera semplice o doppia».

### W5. Colline Teramane DOCG — date, requirements, and whether it is the only DOCG
**Claim:** "Colline Teramane… was raised to DOCG with the 2003 vintage, the region's first."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED as to being the first — but the reading is now out of date: Abruzzo has THREE DOCGs.**
- Colline Teramane: «DM 20.02.2003 G.U. 54 – 06.04.2003», previously DOC under the 1968 DPR. Minimum 90%
  Montepulciano, maximum 10% Sangiovese; minimum 12.5% vol; base aged two years (at least one in oak or
  chestnut plus six months in bottle), Riserva three years; 28 comuni in the province of Teramo.
  URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montepulciano_d'Abruzzo_Colline_Teramane_DOCG (decree citation; treat as a
  lead but it matches the disciplinare mirror at
  https://www.disciplinare.it/montepulciano-dabruzzo-colline-teramane-docg.html on the 90%/10% and the ageing)
- **Terre Tollesi / Tullum DOCG**: created as a DOC in 2008, "promotion was approved by the Ministry of
  Agriculture on July 4, 2019", Italy's 75th DOCG; the zone is the single comune of Tollo (Chieti); reds at
  least 95% Montepulciano, whites at least 90% Passerina or Pecorino; first labelled vintage 2019, with 2018
  retroactive. URL: https://italianwinecentral.com/tullum-becomes-docg-75/
- **Casauria DOCG**: a subzone of Montepulciano d'Abruzzo since 2006, now elevated. Gambero Rosso, 11 November
  2025: 18 comuni in the province of Pescara, about 20 producers, over 35 ha, ~300,000 bottles in 2022, and the
  disciplinare «una volta scaduti i 90 giorni previsti per legge … dovrebbe essere pubblicato entro novembre
  sulla Gazzetta ufficiale europea» — i.e. the EU publication was expected in November 2025. Emanuele
  Imprudente, the region's agriculture assessor: «La nuova Docg – segna un pezzo di storia importante per
  l'Abruzzo». URL: https://www.gamberorosso.it/notizie/vino/nuova-docg-casauria-abruzzo/
  The consortium now lists all three as DOCG: https://www.vinidabruzzo.it/vini-tutelati/ and
  https://www.assovini.it/italia/abruzzo/itemlist/category/166-docg-abruzzo
**Caution:** I could not find the Gazzetta citation completing Casauria's recognition, only the consortium's
listing of it. Write "three DOCGs" if you rely on the consortium, or write Colline Teramane (2003) and Tullum
(2019) and call Casauria the newest, recognised 2025.

### W6. The 2021–2023 reorganisation of Abruzzo's appellations
**Claim:** not in the old text at all — the reading predates it. **This is the biggest gap in reading 1.**
**Verdict: CONFIRMED that a reform happened; here is what it did.**
Approved unanimously by the national wine committee (article dated 9 March 2022), described as the first
restructuring in over a decade and the first change to the denominations in fifty years:
- the eight IGTs were merged into a single **Terre d'Abruzzo IGT**;
- a **"Superiore"** mention was introduced across the DOPs, and «Il riconoscimento di Superiore permetterà
  anche di evidenziare in etichetta il riferimento a territori» ("the Superiore recognition will also allow the
  label to carry a reference to territories");
- four provincial appellations — Colline Teramane, Colline Pescaresi, Terre de L'Aquila, Terre di Chieti — may
  now carry Superiore and Riserva;
- the existing DOCs (Abruzzo, Montepulciano, Cerasuolo, Trebbiano, Controguerra, Villamagna, Ortona) and the
  older subzones were left standing, under one «identità d'Abruzzo».
URL: https://www.virtuquotidiane.it/enogastronomia/cantine-e-vini/cambiano-i-disciplinari-il-vino-regionale-tutto-sotto-il-marchio-dabruzzo-menzione-superiore-e-una-sola-igt.html
Subzones of Montepulciano d'Abruzzo as now listed: Casauria/Terre di Casauria, Terre dei Vestini, Alto Tirino,
Terre dei Peligni, Teate, Terre di Chieti, Terre Aquilane/Terre de L'Aquila, Colline Pescaresi, San Martino
sulla Marrucina. URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/montepulciano-dabruzzo-doc-proposta-modifica-disciplinare.html
Vinous, reporting the 2025 vintage, notes the reform's practical effect on the whites (85% minimum of the
Trebbiano varieties, yields cut by about 10%, blending capped at 15%) and that growers find the four subzones
too broad, with village-level UGAs expected but not scheduled.
URL: https://v1.vinous.com/articles/abruzzo-s-triumphant-2025-vintage-jun-2026
Also relevant: the umbrella **Abruzzo DOC** itself dates only from «D.M. 9.08.2010, G.U. 196 del 23.08.2010»;
its red is at least 80% Montepulciano, its white at least 50% Trebbiano Abruzzese, and it carries the varietal
wines (Pecorino, Passerina, Cococciola, Montonico).
URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/abruzzo-doc/

### W7. Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo — the DOC of 2010
**Claim:** "In 2010 it was given its own Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo DOC… at least 85% Montepulciano and reach 12%
alcohol, 12.5% for a Superiore." Also "for forty years the wine lived inside the red's appellation."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED on every number.**
- Decree: «Approvata con D.M. 5.10.2010, G.U. 242 del 15.10.2010»; last amended «D.M. 19.01.2023 – G.U. 30 del
  06.02.2023». URLs: https://www.quattrocalici.it/disciplinari/cerasuolo-dabruzzo-doc/ and
  https://www.assovini.it/italia/abruzzo/item/2632-cerasuolo-d-abruzzo-doc
- Grapes: «il vino "Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo" deve essere ottenuto dalle uve provenienti da vigneti che nell'ambito
  aziendale risultano composti dal vitigno Montepulciano almeno all'85%».
  URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/cerasuolo-dabruzzo-doc.html
- Alcohol: Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo 12.00% vol; Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo Superiore 12.50% vol.
  URL: https://www.agraria.org/vini/cerasuolo-d-abruzzo-doc.htm
- Before 2010 it was a *tipologia* inside the Montepulciano d'Abruzzo DOC of 24 May 1968 — so 1968 to 2010 is
  42 years. "For forty years" is fair. **[calculated]**
- No compulsory ageing is specified for either type; the Superiore may be wood-aged.

### W8. Cerasuolo — the colour rule and the maceration hours
**Claim:** "six to twenty-four hours of skin contact"; the colour is "cherry red to deep coral."
**Verdict: CORRECTED — the rule is narrower than the reading implies, and the legal colour is pink, not red.**
- The disciplinare's method: «una vinificazione in bianco oppure una breve macerazione a freddo per 8-12 ore,
  cui segue un periodo più o meno breve di affinamento» ("a white vinification, or a short cold maceration of
  8–12 hours, followed by a shorter or longer period of maturing").
  URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/cerasuolo-dabruzzo-doc.html
- The required colour: «rosa ciliegia più o meno carico» — *cherry PINK*, more or less deep — with the nose
  «gradevole, finemente vinoso, fruttato, fine e intenso» and the palate «secco, morbido, armonico, delicato
  con retrogusto gradevolmente mandorlato» (dry, soft, harmonious, delicate, with a pleasantly almondy
  aftertaste). URLs: https://www.agraria.org/vini/cerasuolo-d-abruzzo-doc.htm and
  https://www.assovini.it/italia/abruzzo/item/2632-cerasuolo-d-abruzzo-doc
- The "6 to 24 hours" figure is what the trade press says producers actually do, not what the rules say;
  8–12 h cold maceration is the text. If the reading keeps a range, attribute it to practice.
- **Note a discrepancy** worth not repeating: the Italian Wikipedia article gives the base type's colour as
  «rosso ciliegia più o meno carico» and the Superiore's as «rosa ciliegia …». Every disciplinare mirror I
  checked says *rosa* for both. Use *rosa ciliegia*.
- The almond finish is in the legal description — a free, specific, sourced tasting detail.

### W9. "The best rosé in Italy" / "Italy's only rosé with its own DOC"
**Verdict: the old text does not actually make either claim** (its summary says "Italy's darkest rosé"). The
claim that IS commonly made, and that a rewrite would be tempted by:
- "First Italian DOC dedicated solely and exclusively to a rosé wine" (2010): «la prima D.O.C. italiana
  dedicata solo ed esclusivamente ad un vino rosato».
  URL: https://www.wineshop.it/it/blog/il-cerasuolo-dabruzzo-una-doc-tutta-in-rosato.html — this is trade-blog
  sourcing repeated widely, **not** a ministry statement. Defensible as "the first Italian denomination given
  over entirely to a rosé", with care.
- It is **not** unique now and was never the only rosé appellation: **Castel del Monte Bombino Nero DOCG**
  (Puglia, recognised 2011) is exclusively rosé and outranks it, being a DOCG — "the only Italian rosé to have
  obtained the DOCG stamp."
  URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/castel-del-monte-bombino-nero-docg/
- Bardolino Chiaretto, Valtènesi Chiaretto and Salice Salentino Rosato are the other established rosé
  traditions Italian writing sets beside Cerasuolo.
  URL: https://www.civiltadelbere.com/cerasuolo-dabruzzo-e-rosati-di-puglia-confronto-fra-giganti/
- **"Italy's darkest rosé" is UNVERIFIABLE** and should not be written as fact.

### W10. Does the dark-rosé style predate the DOC? The "one-night wine"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED that the style is documented well before 2010; the "vino di una notte" phrase is trade
usage, not a legal or archival term.**
- Pre-DOC documentation: Ottavi and Marescalchi's 1903 work records that «in particolare nella provincia di
  Chieti, si producevano da tempo "vini cerasuoli"» ("particularly in the province of Chieti, 'cerasuoli' wines
  had long been produced"). URL: https://blog.wineowine.com/cerasuolo-dabruzzo/ — secondary, and the only
  source I found for it; the same authors' *Vade-Mecum* is cited in the DOC's own history for Montepulciano
  (W2), which makes the reference plausible, but **one source only: flag it or attribute it.**
- "Vino di una notte": «il vino è chiamato "vino di una notte" perché le bucce restano in macerazione per poche
  ore» — the wine is called the wine of one night because the skins macerate only a few hours. Trade-press
  usage; it appears in no disciplinare I could read.
- The peasant/field origin: «la storia dei rosati è legata spesso a vini per il consumo domestico, quotidiano»
  ("the history of rosés is often tied to wines for domestic, everyday consumption"), and Cerasuolo is
  described as the wine peasants took to the fields in summer. URL: https://www.jasci.it/cerasuolo-false-credenze-sul-rosato/
  — this is a producer's blog **[producer]** arguing against three myths (that Cerasuolo is a red-white blend,
  a compromise, or low quality) and insisting «Il segreto è tutto nel particolare metodo di vinificazione».
- **The practical reason a dark rosé exists here is sourced in the grape, not in folklore:** the disciplinare
  permits either a white vinification or a *short cold maceration*, i.e. the colour comes out of Montepulciano
  in hours because the skins are so pigmented — which is why a producer must stop early rather than press
  early. That mechanism is what the reading should carry.
- **UNVERIFIABLE:** any documented claim that Cerasuolo was specifically the shepherds' or the harvesters'
  wine. The "brought to the fields" line is trade writing, not an archival source.

### W11. Trebbiano d'Abruzzo — the DOC and the grape tangle
**Claim:** "Trebbiano d'Abruzzo DOC (1972)… the grape called Trebbiano d'Abruzzo is thought not to be a
Trebbiano at all but the southern variety Bombino Bianco, and vineyards often hold a field blend of both plus
Trebbiano Toscano."
**Verdict: DOC date CONFIRMED; the grape statement CORRECTED — it is subtler than "is really Bombino Bianco".**
- Decree: «Approvata con D.P.R. 28.06.1972 G.U. 221 del 25.08.1972»; last amended D.M. 19.01.2023.
  URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/trebbiano-d-abruzzo-doc/
- The rule names three varieties as interchangeable: «Trebbiano abruzzese e/o Bombino bianco e/o Trebbiano
  toscano almeno all'85%». URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/trebbiano-dabruzzo-doc.html
  So the DOC does not decide the question — it permits all three, which is exactly why a bottle can be
  anything from neutral to great.
- The 1972 text listed the varieties as "Trebbiano d'Abruzzo (Bombino bianco) and/or Trebbiano Toscano" — the
  identification was written into the original rules and later separated.
- State of play: **Trebbiano Abruzzese and Bombino Bianco have been listed separately in the national register
  since 1994, and no conclusive DNA study has settled whether they are the same.** One accession labelled
  "Trebbiano Abruzzese" held at the University of Foggia matched "Bombino Bianco di San Severo", which is
  evidence of mislabelled plantings rather than of variety identity. Ampelographers increasingly doubt they are
  the same.
- A 2001 DNA study found a genetic relationship between Trebbiano Abruzzese and Trebbiano Spoletino.
  URL: https://glossary.wein.plus/trebbiano-d-abruzzo-grape-variety (synonyms listed there include «Bombino
  Bianco di San Severo, Trebbiano Abruzzese, Trebbiano di Teramo»).
- I checked the one SSR paper that kept surfacing — Bergamini C, Perniola R, Cardone MF, et al., "The molecular
  characterization by SSRs reveals a new South Italian kinship and the origin of the cultivar Uva di Troia",
  *SpringerPlus* 2016;5(1):1562, doi:10.1186/s40064-016-3228-8
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5023643/) — it names Bombino bianco as a parent in a southern
  family but **does not mention Trebbiano Abruzzese at all**. So it cannot be cited for this question.
- Vinous, 2026: the genuine Trebbiano Abruzzese "clone" is verified by only a handful of estates, which is the
  honest modern framing. URL: https://v1.vinous.com/articles/abruzzo-s-triumphant-2025-vintage-jun-2026
**Safe wording:** the name covers three different grapes, the register separated two of them in 1994, and DNA
has not closed the case. Do not write "it is really Bombino Bianco."

### W12. Valentini
**Claim:** "Edoardo Valentini, in Loreto Aprutino, farmed as his family always had, sold off most of each
harvest and bottled only what he judged worthy; his wines are made without modern intervention."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance; several numbers available; one date disputed.**
- Wine Spectator's obituary: Valentini died in late April 2006 after a sudden illness, in a local nursing home;
  the estate "only vinifies around 20 percent of its crop, after a severe selection process in the vineyards.
  The rest of the grapes are sold locally." Estate 495 acres with nearly 150 acres of vineyard. Age given as
  **70**. URL: https://www.winespectator.com/articles/influential-italian-winemaker-dies-3083
- Wine Spectator's profile: "From the 170 acres of vineyards near the village of Loreto Aprutino, Valentini
  selects about 5 percent of the harvest to make wine"; wine is made "only when they feel everything is right",
  so "the white is made in about seven out of 10 vintages, the red even less. The remainder of the crop is
  sold"; no red in 2007 or 2008; the wines are "made in cement vats and aged in very large, very old Slovenian
  oak casks"; Edoardo made wine "from 1956 until he died in 2006".
  URL: https://www.winespectator.com/articles/azienda-agricola-valentini-an-enigma-in-abruzzo-42961
- Berry Bros & Rudd: a 60-hectare property at Loreto Aprutino, split between olives and vines; the family
  traces itself to 1650, and the estate was formally recognised for its wine in 1868 under Camillo Valentini;
  Edoardo was the first of the family to run it personally, until his death in 2006; Francesco Paolo took over,
  with his wife Elèna and their son Gabriele. **[producer/merchant]**
  URL: https://www.bbr.com/producer-3004-valentini-abruzzo
- The Rare Wine Co. **[merchant]**: fifty vintages; about 5% of the fruit bottled; replaced Trebbiano Toscano
  vines with an old Trebbiano Abruzzese selection; vines trained *tendone*; Francesco's first solo vintage
  2007; the Montepulciano "only bottled twice in the past decade"; a November 2013 blizzard damaged "half of
  the vines … unfortunately in the best zones". URL: https://www.rare.wine/producer/valentini/
**Flagged disagreement:** age at death, 70 (Wine Spectator) vs 72 (merchant sites); and the vineyard is given
as 150 acres, 170 acres, or 60 ha of mixed land. Use "a few hectares' worth of the crop bottled out of a large
estate", or cite the 5% and the 7-in-10 vintages, which two sources agree on.
**Note for reading 1:** the old text says Valentini is "in Loreto Aprutino" (correct, province of Pescara) —
but Berry Bros calls Loreto Aprutino part of the Colline Teramane DOCG zone, which is **wrong** (that DOCG is
the province of Teramo only, W5). Do not repeat the merchant's error.

### W13. Valentini's bulk wine — a documented, quotable line
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and this is the best Valentini quotation I found.** Gabriele Valentini (son of Francesco
Paolo), explaining why the estate still sells unbottled wine from the cellar door to customers who bring their
own containers:
> «Al momento della raccolta non sappiamo ancora cosa sarà venduto sfuso e cosa imbottigliato. Quindi poniamo
> la stessa cura per tutto.»
("At harvest we still don't know what will be sold loose and what will be bottled. So we take the same care
over all of it.")
He calls the practice «una tradizione antica» and «una scelta antieconomica» that keeps the wine «accessibile a
tutti». URL: https://www.intravino.com/primo-piano/tutto-quello-che-ce-da-sapere-sul-mitologico-vino-sfuso-di-valentini-partendo-da-un-bianco-2013/

### W27. How Montepulciano got out of the tankers
The documented account, from a 2018 Italian piece on the DOC's fiftieth anniversary:

### W28. Why a dark rosé exists here — the practical reason
Not folklore: the rules themselves offer the winemaker two roads, «una vinificazione in bianco oppure una breve
macerazione a freddo per 8-12 ore» (W8), because Montepulciano's skins give up so much colour that a few hours
is already a cherry-pink wine. The grape cannot make a pale Provençal rosé without being pressed off almost
immediately; the region legislated for what the grape does. Supporting, from the trade press: the wine is
called «vino di una notte» for that reason, and the peasant tradition behind it is the everyday wine of the
house and the field, not a by-product of red-making — «la storia dei rosati è legata spesso a vini per il
consumo domestico, quotidiano» (W10). The earliest documentation of the style is Ottavi and Marescalchi, 1903,
on the «vini cerasuoli» long made in the province of Chieti (single source — attribute it).
