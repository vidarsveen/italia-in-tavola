## 1. What the neighbouring regions have already spent

Basilicata was written immediately before this and is the sharpest collision, because the two regions share
a mountain range, a sea, a pasta technique and a poverty statistic.

**1. Basilicata 4 has just spent the Pollino.** It states that the Pollino is **Italy's largest national
park**, that the **loricate pine** grows wild in Italy only there and on the massif next door, and it carries
**Italus**, dated in 2018 to a first ring in AD 789. Calabria's old reading 4 claims the **Sila** park at
737 km² and the "giants of the Sila".

→ **Calabria may not re-tell the Pollino**, and its Sila paragraph must not repeat the largest-park claim,
which now belongs to Basilicata and would contradict it. The research is asked to establish Sila's real
ranking and area. Note the Pollino straddles both regions: Basilicata got there first in the rewrite order
and Calabria defers, which this ledger records as a deliberate division.

**2. Basilicata 3 has just spent the dried-pepper economy** — peppers threaded at 120°, dried in the dark
then in the sun, fried for two or three seconds, and a crop that nearly vanished when a dam flooded its
ground. Calabria's chilli is a different product (hot, not sweet; a seasoning, not a snack) but the *shape*
of the story is identical.

→ **Calabria's chilli must arrive through heat and preservation, not through drying on strings.** If the
research supports chilli as a genuine preservative in 'nduja, that mechanism is Calabria's own and Basilicata
has nothing like it.

**3. Basilicata 3 also owns ferricelli wound on an iron rod**, and explicitly notes the local Greek-origin
claim for the technique is unevidenced. Calabria's **fileja** is the same object under another name, and its
old text makes the same Greek claim.

→ **Fileja may not be explained as a rod-rolled shape.** What is left to it is the sauce it is cut for —
goat ragù and 'nduja — and, if the research supports it, the reed rather than the iron.

**4. Molise 3, Puglia 3 and Basilicata 3 have spent stretched-curd cheese hung in pairs**, and Caciocavallo
Silano DOP is named in all three. Calabria's old text names it again.

→ **One clause at most**, and only for what is specifically Calabrian. Pecorino crotonese is unspent and is
the better subject.

**5. Puglia 1 owns a Greek-speaking minority and the dispute about its origin** — about forty villages
between Otranto and Gallipoli, seven with speakers now, and the open question whether the language came with
Magna Graecia or with Byzantine settlers. Calabria's **Grecanici** are the same argument in another province.

→ Calabria may have the Grecanici, but **not the origin dispute staged a second time**. State it once,
attribute it, and move on. The Aspromonte's emptiness is the unspent part.

**6. Magna Graecia itself is half-spent.** Basilicata 1 has Venosa as a Latin colony and Horace born there;
Basilicata 2 has the Magna Graecia SNP study of southern Italian and Greek genotypes; Puglia has Pliny on
Manduria. Calabria's reading 1 is *titled* for Magna Graecia, so this is the reading most at risk.

→ The research is asked specifically whether "the oldest vineyard", the **Cirò-Olympics story** and
"Enotria" survive contact with primary sources. If they do not — and the Olympics story is very likely
modern marketing — **reading 1 has to be re-planned rather than corrected**, which is what happened to
Lazio 3 and Puglia 1 and 3.

**7. The poverty ranking is now settled against Calabria, in this course's own research.** Basilicata's pass
established from Istat and the 1951–54 parliamentary inquiry that **Calabria, not Basilicata, is Italy's
poorest region** on GDP per head in every series that can be dated, and Basilicata's reading says "among the
poorest" precisely because of it.

→ Calabria is the region entitled to that fact, but **it must be cited with figures and years, not asserted
as atmosphere**, and it may not become a *cucina povera* frame — Toscana owns that as a proud name, Puglia
owns the surplus-that-left inversion, and Basilicata refused it outright. Calabria's old reading 3 opens on
"the food of people with very little", which is spent three times over.

**8. Free ground, so far as the sweeps show:** bergamot; liquorice and the Amarelli firm; the **Riace
bronzes**; swordfish and the *passerella* boats; 'nduja and its French etymology; the Tropea onion; the
1783 and 1908 earthquakes; Edward Lear's and George Gissing's Calabrian journals.

## 3. Map and scene facts, stated up front

Figures marked ⚑ are for the research to confirm or correct.

- Calabria is the **toe** of the boot, with the **Tyrrhenian** on the west and the **Ionian** on the east,
  separated from Sicily by the **Strait of Messina** ⚑ (about 3 km at its narrowest). It borders **Basilicata**
  to the north and nothing else.
- Five provinces ⚑: **Catanzaro** (the capital), Cosenza, Crotone, Reggio Calabria, Vibo Valentia.
- Three massifs run down it: the **Pollino** on the Basilicata border (Basilicata's, see §1), the **Sila**
  granite plateau in the centre ⚑, and the **Aspromonte** in the far south, which rises from the sea to
  nearly 2,000 m ⚑.
- **Cirò** and **Cirò Marina** are on the **Ionian** coast in the province of Crotone; **Tropea** is on the
  **Tyrrhenian** in Vibo Valentia; **Bagnara** and **Scilla** are on the Tyrrhenian near the strait;
  **Rossano** (liquorice) is Ionian, in Cosenza; **Diamante** (the chilli festival) is Tyrrhenian, in Cosenza;
  **Spilinga** ('nduja) is inland from Tropea; **Riace**, where the bronzes were found, is Ionian.
  Directions must not be got backwards: **Cirò is east, Tropea is west.**
- Greek colonies on the Ionian side: **Sybaris**, **Kroton**, **Locri** ⚑. **Reggio** is Rhegion.
- The **bergamot** strip is the Ionian coast of Reggio Calabria province ⚑.
- Calabria is Italy's poorest region on GDP per head ⚑ (established in the Basilicata research: about 57.0%
  of the national average against Basilicata's 79.7% in 2015–2024).

## 4b. What the food and landmark research established (returned 2026-09-17)

`docs/rewrite/calabria-research-food.md`, F1–F40 and D1–D25. Outlines for readings 3 and 4 wait on the wine
pass, because §5.0 must allocate one opening shape across all four readings at once. These are the findings
that do not wait.

**Hard errors in the old text.**
- **A species error.** The "giants of the Sila" are **not larch**. They are Calabrian black pines, *Pinus
  nigra* subsp. *laricio* — *laricio* resembles "larch" and is a pine. Fifty-eight of them in a 5.44 ha
  reserve at Fallistro, tallest 43.6 m, some dated 1620–1650, FAI-run since June 2016 (D16).
- **A quotation misdated by seventeen years.** The "quality best appreciated in the United Kingdom is made
  in Calabria" line is **1911** Britannica, not 1928, and it is about British taste in liquorice *juice*
  sold as Solazzi and Corigliano — not a world ranking (F35).
- **Liquorice volumes wrong by two orders of magnitude.** "1,000 ha, 2,500 t a year" has no source;
  certified DOP production is **27–44 tonnes a year (2021–23) from 31 operators** (F33).
- **Costa degli Dei "marketed since the 1950s"** — regional reporting puts the coinage in the **1990s**,
  by a tourism agency, with the mythology retrofitted (D1).
- **Sila's distinction is forest cover, not size.** About 80%, the highest of any Italian national park.
  **Pollino is Italy's largest** — and Basilicata has just spent it, so any ranking sentence here must be
  about forest (D15, and §1.1 above).
- **Santa Maria dell'Isola was Byzantine before it was Benedictine** (Basilian monks; Benedictine from the
  11th century, Montecassino, Robert Guiscard c. 1060), and **the stair was built about 1810**, after the
  1783 earthquake — so the climb is not medieval (D5, D7).
- **The rock joined the shore by earthquake, not silt** (D6). The 1783 sequence is load-bearing for the
  whole landmark reading and absent from the old text (D21).
- **Chilli's Calabrian trail is 17th–19th century**, not a 16th-century love affair: Campanella c. 1635,
  Grimaldi 1770, "exceedingly rare" 1792, Columella 1804, Pugliese 1848 (F10).
- **"The chilli preserves it" is overstated.** Salt, falling water activity, fermentation, cold smoke and
  months of drying do the work; capsicum's antimicrobial effect is real but bounded, and the authors name
  the limits themselves (F12). Write "helps preserve" and name salt and drying first.

**The best material the old text lacks.** The **Riace bronzes**, found by Stefano Mariottini on 16 August
1972, 230 m off Riace Marina in 8–10 m of water, raised 21–22 August — absent entirely, and the most famous
objects in the region (D20). 'Nduja's **30% chilli, 300 g per kilo**, and its casing the ***orba***, the
blind gut (F2, F3). Its **legal position**: the IGP has been in process since 2008, and MASAF told the
consortium in February 2024 that other Calabrian producers may keep using the name — the fact behind every
jar sold abroad (F5). **Bergamot is a near-monopoly**, ~80% of world production in one province, and the DOP
protects the **oil, not the fruit** (F31, F32). **Sardella**'s derogation for fishing fry was withdrawn in
**2010**, so jars now often hold imported icefish (F36). The **Tropea onion** is a three-province IGP of
115 operators and ~18–23 million kg a year, and **the consortium hedges its own Phoenician story with
«sembra che»**, its first organised count being the *Enciclopedia agraria Reda*, 1936–39 (F15–F19) — which
is the house style already written by the people who would most like the legend to be true.

**Calabria is last in Italy on GDP per head**: €21,700 in 2024 against a Centre-North 1.75× higher (Istat,
published 22 December 2025), and €17,064 declared income per head for 2023 (MEF). This confirms the
Basilicata finding and is the region entitled to the fact — **with the year attached, not as atmosphere**,
and never as a *cucina povera* frame (F38, and §1.7).

**Do not assert these — the research could not reach the sources.** EUR-Lex (the onion's registering
regulation number); the MASAF Pecorino Crotonese disciplinare (image-only); Treccani on *'nduja*; **Rohlfs's
Calabrian dictionary**; Gissing beyond chapter VIII and Norman Douglas beyond chapter VII; **Edward Lear's
*Journals***; ICCAT's swordfish rules; the liquorice consortium (DNS failure); any peer-reviewed measurement
of Tropea onion pungency. Two candidate openings carry explicit verify-before-printing warnings: **Gissing's
doctor "Riccardo Sculco"** and the **1770 Grimaldi citation**, which names no first name or title.

**Also barred by the research itself:** no named grower, onion-plaiter or swordfish spotter exists in any
reachable source, so reading 3 cannot open on one and must not invent one. **Roghudi** duplicates Craco and
may be used only inside the Greko language story, never as a ruin scene. And the **ferretto's Greek origin**
gets no source — the same verdict this project reached for Basilicata's ferricelli — so it should not be
staged even to knock down, which would be the second time in two regions.

## 4c. What the wine research established (returned 2026-09-17)

`docs/rewrite/calabria-research-wine.md`, W1–W55. **The old text's wine spine traces to the English Wikipedia
article "Calabrian wine", which is wrong on at least three counts** — the number of DOCs, the share bottled
as DOC, and Milo drinking Cirò. Where the old text and that article agree in substance, assume copying.

**Reading 1's frame collapses.** The Krimisa-Olympics story is not merely unsourced, it is **disprovable**:
Olympic prizes were olive wreaths (no source names wine); the sanctuary excavated at Punta Alice by Paolo
Orsi, 1924–29, is **Apollo Aleus's, not Bacchus's**; the Krimisa record carries no wine and no Olympics; the
trade's own telling attributes it to unnamed "historians". The documented half is that **Cirò was the
official wine at Mexico City in 1968** and bottles were given at Athens in 2004 — a marketing act retrojected
two and a half millennia. Likewise "Milo of Croton drank Cirò": Athenaeus book 10 has three choes of wine in
a single gluttony anecdote **and names no wine**. And *Enotria* is not "the land of wine": Dionysius of
Halicarnassus gives an eponymous hero, Oenotrus son of Lycaon, with no grape in it, and Treccani places the
Oenotrians in **Lucania and Bruttium**, so Basilicata's claim on the name is as good as Calabria's.
→ **Reading 1 is re-planned, not corrected.** Its honest subject is how much of "Greek" Calabria is a name
rather than a plant or a place — and what the ancients actually praised on this coast was **Lagaritan and
Thurian wine from the plain of Sybaris** (Strabo 6.1.14), at the other end of the region from Cirò.

**Hard errors.** "Twelve DOCs" → **nine plus the DOCG**, because seven Cosenza denominations merged into
**Terre di Cosenza in 2011**. "Only 4% bottled as DOC" → UIV-ISTAT 2013 gives **DOP 43%, IGP 34.6%**. Greco
di Bianco's minimum is **17% total (or 14% actual)**, not 13%. Savuto's Gaglioppo minimum is **35%**, so it
is not a small Cirò. The Sybaris river-diversion story is contradicted by **core samples**, which found no
river deposits above the city — it lies about 6 m down, below the water table. "Old vines survived because
nobody had money to change anything" is contradicted by Treccani c.1930: **every Calabrian commune was
phylloxerated** and the vineyard was "tuttora in lenta ricostruzione".

**The best new material.** **Gaglioppo's second parent was identified in 2012–13: Sangiovese × Mantonico
bianco** (Gasparro et al., 52 SSR loci; confirmed by D'Onofrio et al. 2021 on 1,232 varieties) — and
Mantonico bianco is a **founder variety of southern Italy**, parent also of Nerello Mascalese and Catarratto,
of which about **ten hectares** survive and which Italy did not register until **2014**. **"Greco" names
three different plants**: Calabria's Greco bianco is genetically distinct from Campania's Greco, and the
grape in Greco di Bianco is **a Malvasia** sharing a profile with Malvasia di Lipari — which, set beside this
course's Campania finding that Greco and Asprinio are one grape, is a genuinely new paragraph. **The
pale-but-tannic paradox has a measured mechanism**: Calabrian researchers use Gaglioppo as the model
low-anthocyanin red, its colour hue was tracked rising across 120 months on 87 wines, and its grip is
largely **seed** tannin, removable without touching colour. The **DOCG is the end of an argument**: Cirò
Revolution, founded 2010 to oppose the 10% international varieties, won when the 2023–25 DOCG banned them
outright and raised Gaglioppo to 90%.

**Do not assert.** The ministry's variety catalogue was down all session and EUR-Lex returned empty, so
register entries and the Cirò Classico EU text come from aggregators. No Calabria-specific emigration figure
for 1880–1915 exists in any reachable source — **do not put a number on it**. The 1969 disciplinare's
original grape composition was unreachable, so drop the "came down to 80%" narrative and write the
DOC-versus-DOCG contrast instead. Cirò's hectarage is reported as four incompatible figures; the planted
classico heart is about **500 ha** and 9,000 or 20,000 must never be printed as "vineyard". Do not write
that **Librandi funded the DNA work** — the published genetics were funded by the Chamber of Commerce of
Cosenza and a MIUR project.

### Reading 1 — "Magna Graecia and the oldest vineyard" → retitle

**The title must change**: "the oldest vineyard" is unsupportable (unguarded 2). Proposed: **"Magna Graecia,
and what the Greeks left"**.

**Spine.** How much of Greek Calabria is a name rather than a thing. Lead on Orsi at Punta Alice (W5, O1) —
the temple everyone calls Bacchus's is Apollo's, and there is no wine in the record. Then the colonies as
fact (Sybaris c. 720, Kroton c. 710–708, Locri early 7th c., W1), Sybaris destroyed in 510 BC with the river
story disproved by core samples (W3), and **what the ancients actually praised**: Lagaritan and Thurian from
the plain of Sybaris, Consentia and Tempsa in Pliny — none of it Cirò (W10). Close on the 1968 Mexico City
service as the real, datable origin of the Olympic story.
**Box:** the colony dates; Enotria's eponymous hero and Treccani's Lucania-and-Bruttium; the Grecanici's
2,000 speakers and the Rohlfs-versus-Byzantine dispute (W9 — one sentence only; Puglia owns the staging).

### Reading 2 — "Gaglioppo and Cirò"

**Spine.** A grape that lost its Greek passport and gained a mechanism. Lead on the 52 markers (W21, O3).
Then: what the genetics actually kill (not the Greek link of the *population* — De Lorenzis 2019 supports
that — but the idea that Gaglioppo *itself* is an import; it is a local cross with a Tuscan parent, W22).
**Mantonico bianco**, founder variety, ten hectares, registered 2014 (W31). Then the mechanism section, which
must be the longest: low anthocyanins by measurement, hue rising over 120 months, **seed tannin removable
without touching colour** (W24–W26) — the cause of both the old austere style and the modern perfumed one,
and of a famous red appellation being **30% rosé** (W51). Then the rules as a live argument: DOC 80% with
10% internationals allowed; **Cirò Revolution, 2010**; the DOCG at 90%, internationals banned, 36 months with
6 in wood, EU-registered 25 July 2025 (W15, W16, W35). Close on the Piedmont tannin trade (W45, O10),
attributed.
**Box:** DOC 1969 decree and dates; the nine-DOCs-plus-one correction and Terre di Cosenza 2011 (W17);
Greco di Bianco's real numbers — 12 ha, ten days on racks, 35–50% weight loss, 17% (W19).
**Tasting card check:** four IT-78 cards exist; if reading 2's table changes, both language files move with
it, character for character.

### Reading 3 — "'Nduja, chilli and the Calabrian pantry"

**The reviewers caught what the lint cannot.** Twenty findings, all accepted. The two that matter most would
have passed every mechanical check:

- «Guden på stedet er Apollon, som Filoktetes skal ha viet buen til Herakles.» In Norwegian `viet buen til
  Herakles` parses as *dedicated the bow **to** Heracles*, so the dedicatee becomes Heracles and **Apollo drops
  out of the sentence** — in a lead whose whole point is that the god at Krimisa is Apollo and not Bacchus.
- «Noe vin på denne kysten roste **de**.» The antecedent of `de` sits in the `<h2>` above it, and the spoken
  script **drops headings**. A listener hears it straight after a sentence about people alive today, so the
  nearest antecedent is present-day Calabrians and the section's argument inverts. This is a defect that exists
  *only* in the audio, and no reader of the page would ever see it.

Also found: «å skjes opp» is not a verb (*å skje* means *to happen*), twice; «en pasta» used for a paste, twice,
in a reading that is genuinely about pasta two paragraphs earlier (§4b rule 8 already records this); «ved
dekket», where bare *dekket* is a ship's deck and the same word appears in its real sense earlier in the same
reading; «romantikken», which is the Romantic period and not “the romance”; and six invented compounds that
are grammatical and that nobody says — «kjernetannin» (carrying a gender disagreement with a following
«den»), «modelldrue», «fråtseanekdote», «vinry», «landfestet», «bygningsmassen». No fact drift was found
in any of the four, and the direction checks (Ionian vs Tyrrhenian) all passed.
