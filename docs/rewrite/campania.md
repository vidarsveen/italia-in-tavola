## 1. What the neighbouring regions have already spent

Written before the outlines, because Campania 4 is the third Roman-archaeology landmark reading in four
regions and the collision is not hypothetical.

**Lazio 4 (the Colosseum) owns, and Campania may not repeat:**

- The **thermopolia**. Lazio 4 already uses Pompeii's counters as its evidence for Roman fast food —
  "about eighty of them on the archaeological park's count, or a hundred and fifty-eight if every masonry
  counter is included" — *and* stages Steven Ellis's objection that the Latin label was fixed on these
  rooms by their nineteenth-century excavators. Campania 4 cannot open on, or build a section around,
  a counter with sunken jars. The 2020 Regio V thermopolium may appear only as a box line if at all.
- **Amphorae as evidence of trade**: Monte Testaccio, the Dressel 20s, the painted labels. So
  Campania's wine amphorae and their makers' marks are a spent shape; garum has to arrive some other way
  than "jars carried a maker's name".
- **Food remains recovered from a dig**: the drain's fig, grape, olive, peach, cherry and melon stones,
  the chicken and pig bones. The Herculaneum sewer study is the same *kind* of evidence. If it is used
  at all it must do something Lazio's drain did not — Lazio's point was that a drain takes whatever runs
  into it and so is nobody's lunch; Herculaneum's cesspit is the opposite, one block's household waste.
  That contrast is the only honest way to use it, and it must be stated as the reason.

**Abruzzo 3 and Molise 3 own the transhumance**: the tratturi, the Dogana, the 1592 sheep count, the
animal that could not finish the walk, the shepherd's dairy. Campania's buffalo plains are a different
animal economy and must be written as one — marsh, malaria, reclamation — with no drove roads in it.

**Abruzzo 4 owns the mountain-hotel/structure opening** and **Molise 4 owns the Samnite sanctuary**.
Campania's Samnite material (Nola, Nocera, the Oscan-speaking interior) is therefore nearly exhausted
as an *opening*, though it may appear inside a paragraph.

**Therefore barred for Campania 1–4:**

- a named producer or writer introduced with a date (Lazio 2, Abruzzo 1, Molise 2);
- an archaeological feature or structure described in the present tense (Lazio 4, Abruzzo 4, Molise 4) —
  this rules out the obvious Pompeii openings;
- a hypothetical person or animal ("a cellarman who…", "a sheep that…") (Abruzzo 2, Abruzzo 3);
- a fronted prepositional place phrase before the subject (Lazio 2, Lazio 3, and flagged again on

- a lawsuit or an institution going to court (Molise 1);
- **a lead that ends on a withheld name** — three in a row in Molise, and a reviewer warned after two.

What is left, and what the research agents were asked to supply openings for: a person with a want or a
deadline *doing* something (not writing it down), a crowd, a scene at a time of day, a measurement taken,
a prejudice overturned inside the sentence. Four different shapes, logged here before drafting.

### What the wine research changed, before any outline

1. **Reading 1's causal spine is wrong.** "Loose and sandy, which is why phylloxera could never move
   through it" credits *volcanic* soil with a property that belongs to *sand*. The mechanism is mechanical:
   below about 3% clay or above about 65–70% sand the louse's galleries collapse and it desiccates. The
   proof is that the same ungrafted vines survive at Colares in Portugal and in the Sable de Camargue,
   where there is no volcano at all (W12). This is not a correction to a sentence; it is the reading's
   argument, and it is a better one, because it has a test in it.
2. **Coda di Volpe is no longer the white of Vesuvius.** Caprettone was separated from it by DNA, entered
   the national register on **20 October 2014** and was given its own Vesuvio DOC types in **2020** — while
   the Lacryma Christi article of the same rulebook still reads «Coda di volpe (localmente detto
   Caprettone)» (W22). The old text was written before this and is simply out of date.
3. **Greco and Asprinio are the same grape** on the DNA, and distinct from Calabria's Greco Bianco (W19).
   That links the austere hill white of Tufo to the thin sharp wine picked fifteen metres up a poplar on
   the plain near Aversa (W42). Neither reading has it.
4. **Sulphur does not flavour Greco di Tufo.** The best source sets up "first the mines, now the wine" and
   pointedly declines to connect them; producer pages assert it (W20). Both readings currently state it as
   mechanism. The coincidence is worth telling; the loop must be left open.
5. **Taurasi Riserva needs eighteen months in wood, not one year** (W28/W30), in the prose and the box.
6. Smaller: forty vents → INGV recognises at least 24 structures (W9); Naples is not an 8th-century BC
   foundation, Cumae is (W3); *Oenotria* named the far south and the "land of trained vines" gloss is
   called groundless (W4); the 1944 eruption's 26 deaths came from **roofs collapsing under ash**, not lava,
   and "destroying vineyards" is unsourced (W8); Sciascinoso is missing from the Lacryma Christi red (W24);
   the Tufo mines did not close in 1982 (W35); Greco's problem is mildew, not rot (W36); Irpinia's soils are
   calcareous clay with superficial volcanic deposits, so reading 1 currently contradicts reading 2 (W11).

**Hedge budget.** Campania carries at least eight legends that must be staged rather than told (Christ's
tears, Aglianico from *Hellenic*, Greco from Thessaly, Fiano as *vitis apiana*, di Marzo's 1647 cuttings,
"the Greeks brought the vine", Falanghina as Falernian's daughter, sulphur in the tuff). Three regions
running have been flagged for the correction-and-debunk cadence. **Ceiling: two staged disputes in reading 1
and two in reading 2**, one of them told at length and the rest settled in a clause or moved to the box.
Counted before the reviews, not after.

### Reading 1 — Volcanic soils and ancient grapes

**Lead (B10, reworked): a meeting, a comparison, and a prejudice overturned inside the sentence.**
Bacoli, 22–24 January 2025: growers meet to compare notes on *viticoltura a piede franco*, vines nobody
ever grafted — and some of the people at the table farm sand with no volcano within reach of it. Opening
move: **a dated event with people in a room**, which is a shape none of the last twelve leads used. Story
arc: a comparison that dissolves the region's favourite explanation of itself. Closing beat: **sand**, a
substance, not a withheld name (three of Molise's four leads withheld a name).

1. The mechanism, in McGee's order (rule 3): the louse moves through soil between roots; in loose sand its
   galleries collapse and it dries out; so below about 3% clay the vine is safe. Colares and the Camargue
   as the control case. No date for phylloxera in Campania — none exists (U18) — and the European dates
   corrected: France in the 1860s, Italy's first confirmed outbreak 1879 (W12).
2. What the volcano *does* do, stated narrowly: drains fast, holds heat. The potassium-and-stress claim is
   unsourced lore (U4) and is either dropped or attributed as what growers say. Ungrafted vines attributed,
   not asserted: Casa Setaro's 12 ha at 200–350 m; the Campi Flegrei's centenarian vines; the oldest claims
   of all are at **Tramonti on the Amalfi coast**, 300–500 years, on the estate's own say-so (W13).
3. The three volcanic systems, corrected. Vesuvius **18 March 1944**, and the detail that replaces
   "destroying vineyards": the 26 dead were killed by **roofs collapsing under the weight of ash** at
   Terzigno and Pagani, which is also what red zone 2 of today's plan is drawn against — 25 comuni, about
   700,000 people, 72 hours (W8). Campi Flegrei: at least 24 structures, 12–13 km across, last eruption
   **Monte Nuovo, 29 September 1538** (W9). Irpinia: calcareous clay with superficial volcanic deposits,
   not volcanic ground (W11) — the sentence that currently contradicts reading 2.
4. The grapes, as evidence inside sentences with verbs, never a list (rule 6). **Aglianico**: the name is
   unrecorded before the 15th century and first printed as «Aglianiche» in **1520**, which points at a
   Spanish-era coinage for a vine everyone calls Greek, and the DNA does not support the Greek origin
   (W16) — *this is the reading's one long staged dispute*. Ripens into November (W17). **Caprettone**:
   registered 20 October 2014, its own DOC type in 2020, the old name still fossilised in the neighbouring
   article of the same rulebook (W22). **Piedirosso**: the dialect *per' e palummo* is the old name; the
   Italian "Piedirosso" was coined by the ampelographer **Michele Carlucci in 1909** (W25). **Sciascinoso**
   restored to the Lacryma Christi red, 50/30/20 with Piedirosso and Aglianico (W24).
5. **Lacryma Christi**: two competing legends in one sentence — Lucifer's stolen piece of Paradise, and the
   hermit's water turned to wine — then the honest line, that no document dates the name, and the Capuchins
   of Torre del Greco are the earliest custodians anyone can attribute it to (W23). Second staged dispute;
   the budget is now spent.
6. **Ending** on the rulebook that corrects itself in one article and preserves the mistake in the next
   (W22): an image and a limit, not a summary.

**Moved to the Key facts box:** the four DOCG names; Vesuvio DOC 13 January 1983 and its blend rules; the
red-zone numbers; Ischia DOC 3 March 1966 kept as "one of Italy's first" and **not** upgraded (W26);
Falernian on Monte Massico. **Cut with reason:** "the oldest wine country in Italy" (unsupported; Italy's
oldest wine residue is 4th-millennium BC Sicily, W1); "by 1970… anonymous bulk" (no 1970 figure; replaced by
the present-day "most of it still leaves in bulk", W5); *Oenotria* as "the land of trained vines" applied to
the peninsula (W4); "Aglianico is one of Italy's three great red grapes" (opinion, U8); "most of southern
Italy makes soft low-acid whites" (unsourced generalisation about everywhere else, U9).

### Reading 2 — Taurasi, Fiano and Greco

**Title is an inventory** (§2 permits improving such titles; the count and order stay). Decide at draft
time; a title naming the hills rather than three wines would match the reading's actual argument.

**Lead (B1): 1866, Tufo — a man riding sees a peasant woman burning a stone.** It is sulphur. Opening move:
**narrative past, a person noticing something**, which the last twelve leads have not used (Molise 2's
person was *writing a document*, a different act). Closing beat: the substance, not a name. Chain, and every
link is sourced (W35, W34, W43): a burning stone → a mine district between Tufo and Altavilla, 1,300 men
underground → a village with money and a railway station → Greco shipped by rail to France by Cantine di
Marzo → a French idea about bottle fermentation → metodo classico Greco di Tufo now. **The loop the reader
expects stays open**: the vineyards sit on top of the abandoned galleries, tasters reach for sulphur and
smoke when they describe the wine, and nobody has shown the two are connected (W20). That refusal is the
paragraph, and it is the reading's one long staged dispute.

1. The mines' end, in the comune's own framing rather than a wrong year: about 118 years, extraction
   stopping around 1972, the works closing about 1983 with **seven men** left, killed by American sulphur
   and then by sulphur scraped out of oil refineries (W35). Never "1982".
2. **Greco is Asprinio** (W19), which opens the door to the alberata (W42): vines married to poplars,
   fifteen metres up and twenty in places, one plant covering 200 m² and carrying 220 kg, every operation
   done from a one-man ladder called a *scalillo*; about **16,000 hectares in the 1960s, under two hundred
   today**, attributed because two press pieces share a phrasing (W42). The same grape, on a hill and up a
   tree. This pairing is the reading's spine and replaces the old "rest of the map" list.
3. **Taurasi**: DOCG 1993 (DOC since 1970), "the first red in southern Italy" attributed, never asserted
   (U13); 85% Aglianico; **three years, one in wood — and four years with eighteen months in wood for the
   Riserva**, the clock starting 1 December of the harvest year (W28). The scale is the surprise worth
   printing: **472 ha and about 6,370 hl**, small for the reputation (W28).
4. **Mastroberardino, with dates and without the unsourced sentence.** Founded 1878 by Angelo; for much of
   the post-war period the house made **more than half of all Campania's DOC wine**, which is why one family
   name carries the region; the **1968 Riserva** was made with his brother Walter and named one of Wine
   Spectator's twelve wines of the century in 1999; Antonio died **28 January 2014**, so the old text's
   present tense goes. The **1994 split** — Antonio kept the name and the winery, Walter took much of the
   vineyard and founded Terredora — is the honest ending of that arc (W29). **The 1980 earthquake sentence
   is cut**: the quake is documented to the minute (23 November 1980, 19:34, Mw 6.9, 2,914 dead, 688 comuni)
   but *no source* describes its effect on cellars or vineyards, or any replanting decision (W29/U10). What
   is documented is depopulation; the vineyards enter as what was rebuilt afterwards.
5. **Fiano**: DOCG 2003, 26 communes, and the legal curiosity that does the work the *vitis apiana*
   hypothesis cannot — **«Apianum» is permitted on the label**, at no more than half the height of the
   denomination (W18). A hypothesis with a legal existence. The decline is attributed, not asserted: the
   "two hectares left in the 1970s" figure rests on one weak source (W32) and is either attributed or
   dropped; the Riserva's minimum year replaces "improves for a decade" (W44/U12).
6. **Greco di Tufo** closes the circle to the lead: DOCG 2003, eight communes, **and materially bigger than
   either Taurasi or Fiano** — 636–666 ha, 35,550 hl (W33) — which the old reading's running order implies
   the opposite of. Thick-skinned yes; **mildew and shot berries, not rot** (W36). The spumante ageing
   number is **not printed**: the sources disagree, 18 vs 36 months (W34).
7. **Ending**: B8's compromise, in short — two traditions claimed the Roman name Falerno, one built on
   Aglianico and one on Primitivo, and the 1989 decree did not choose between them but wrote both into the
   rules. A label that is a legal settlement about antiquity. Ends on the limit of the claim (rule 10).

**Moved to the box:** the DOCG dates and rules for all four; the Costa d'Amalfi's **69 ha** (not "a handful
of hectares", W40); Cilento 1989 and 59 comuni; Aglianico del Taburno 2011 with its **200–650 m** altitude
band replacing the unsourced "softer and earlier-drinking" (W41). **Cut with reason:** "steep green hills
between 400 and 700 metres" → the consortium's 300–600 m and its own phrase, *viticoltura di tipo montano*
(W27); "one of very few Italian whites outside Friuli and Alto Adige…" (U12); "thick-skinned and prone to
rot" (W36); the Taurasi commune list (if ever printed, the disciplinare's — Wikipedia's substitutes a Fiano
commune, W28).

### Readings 3 and 4 — leads chosen, and the shapes ruled out

The food research (`campania-research-food.md`, F1–F36 and D1–D39, with eleven ranked openings in its
section E) proposes its own best leads. Two of them are barred by §2 of this ledger and the substitutes are
recorded here with the reasoning, because a reviewer who cannot see the reasoning will propose them again.

**Reading 3 — the lead is E1, Serao 1884.** A Neapolitan businessman opens a proper pizzeria outside the
city, copper pans gleaming, the oven always lit, every kind available, and it fails: *"la pizza, tolta al
suo ambiente napoletano, pareva una stonatura e rappresentava una indigestione."* Opening move: **a named
primary source describing a business that did not work** — a shape none of the last sixteen leads used.
Story arc: a failure, not a triumph. Closing beat: the dish would not travel. The chain is the reading's
whole argument — the thing would not travel, so what travelled was the method, which is why UNESCO in 2017
listed **the art of the pizzaiuolo and not the pizza**. E6, the boy carrying wedges on a tin tray at two
centesimi for schoolchildren, is the same source and goes **inside** the reading, not at the top.

**Reading 4 — the research's first choice is barred, and the substitute keeps its substance.** E3 (the
pomegranates and the date argument) and E5 (the stamped loaf) and E4 (the insulted bar wall) are all, in
shape, *an archaeological find described in the present tense*, which Lazio 4, Abruzzo 4 and Molise 4 have
used in succession; E5 and E4 additionally open on **an inscription with a name in it**, which is Molise 4
exactly. So reading 4 opens instead on **the household evidence as activity**: the braziers lit, the heavy
wool, the chestnuts and dried figs and pomegranates put by, and the wine jars at Boscoreale left open ten
days before they were sealed. People doing autumn things, in a season, with no find and no inscription in
the first sentence. The date argument follows from it and keeps E3's honesty: the manuscripts say 24
August, the kitchens say autumn, the 2018 charcoal scrawl was re-read by the Parco's own palaeographer
three days later, and current volcanology declines to name a day. **Write "in the autumn of AD 79".**

**Reading 4 loses the thermopolia altogether.** The research calls the 2020 Regio V counter "the best food
scene in the entire site" (D14) and confirms Osanna's eighty (D13) — but Lazio 4 has already spent the
counters, the count *and* Ellis's objection to the name (§1 of this ledger). Repeating them would make two
regions read as one. Reading 4 reaches the table by the routes Lazio did not take: the **pistrina** and the
fact that the poor had no oven at all (D15, which also corrects "communal ovens"), the **loaf stamped with
the name of the man who owned the man who baked it** (D16), the **2023 Regio IX bakery with barred windows
and grooves cut for blindfolded donkeys** (D17), and the **Herculaneum sewer** (D19) — which is admissible
only because it is the *opposite* of Lazio's drain and must say so: one block's household waste, not
whatever ran in off the street. **Quiz 4.3 asks what a thermopolium was and must be replaced**, since its
answer will no longer be in the reading.

**Hedge budget, and it is the tightest of any region so far.** Campania's food chapters carry at least
fourteen undocumented stories (Margherita in every part, Port'Alba, marinara as the older, the 8,000
pizzerias, pizza a otto, the portafoglio's origin, tomatoes "thought poisonous", the buffalo's arrival and
the monks of Capua, Gragnano's sea breeze, the genovese's name, Santa Rosa's sfogliatella, the babà's
route, the pastiera's Partenope, the caffè sospeso as unbroken) and reading 4 carries a dozen more. The
correction-and-debunk cadence has now been flagged in three regions running, so: **one dispute staged at
length per reading, one more in a clause, and everything else simply written correctly without announcing
that it has been corrected.** Reading 3's long one is the Margherita letter, where the old text's framing
must be **inverted** — it says the letter exists and historians doubt the rest, whereas the letter's
authenticity is precisely what is disputed (signature, form, the surname, the silence of the 1889 press),
while every ingredient of a margherita was already on Neapolitan pizzas in 1858. Reading 4's long one is the
date. Nothing else gets a paragraph of doubt, and **the count is made before the reviews, not after**.

**The two-thirds rule for reading 3's technical claims.** Almost every number in the old text belongs to the
wrong rulebook: the eight-hour rise, the 485 °C and the San Marzano requirement are **AVPN** rules, not the
**STG**, and the STG names no flour type because Germany objected and the clause was deleted in 2009. The
draft must say which document it is quoting each time, or it will be wrong twice over. The Germany
objection (E2) is too good to lose and is not a legend: it goes inside the reading, at the point where the
rules are introduced.

### The finding that matters, and it is the same in all four reports

**The correction-and-debunk cadence took the region over.** Counts by the reviewers: reading 1, about a
dozen instances against a budget of two, with six paragraphs *ending* on the move; reading 2, seven;
reading 3, twelve outside the two licensed ones; reading 4, nine. Reading 3's reviewer went further and
identified it as **syntactic**: the frame *"X is A rather than/not B"* fires seven times, and the reading's
last clause is "the fourteenth *actually*".

This is the fourth region running to be flagged for it (Abruzzo, Molise, Lazio, now Campania), and the new
information is uncomfortable: **§6 of this ledger set an explicit budget of two staged disputes per reading,
named which two they were, and said "counted before the reviews, not after" — and the drafts still came in
at five times the budget.** A budget written into the ledger does not survive contact with research returns

### The four fact reviews, and what the second pass did

All four landed after the reader reviews. **Nothing in them was rejected outright**; three findings were
accepted in a narrowed form, and those are marked.

**Reading 1.** The serious one: the lead **turned a comparison into an attendance list**. W13 documents a
meeting of growers at Bacoli, 22–24 January 2025; W12 separately names Colares and the Camargue as sandy
non-volcanic parallels. The draft put people from those places in the room, and the whole lead rested on
it. Rewritten so the meeting is the meeting and the parallels stay parallels. Also fixed: Monte Nuovo "on
ground that had been a lake" (no source; now its 130 metres), Ischia's ungrafted vines built into a prose
sentence where W15 says explicitly *"do not build a sentence on it"* (now only the box list), "Verdeca
permitted up to nearly half" where W24 says *"do not print percentages for the white"*, "under Spanish rule
for a generation" (the draft's own arithmetic, about sixteen years), and "later than anything else in the
region" (W17 permits the window, not the ranking). Three losses restored: **the mountain is monitored
constantly** (the one line telling a reader the volcano is watched), **the meaning of Coda di Volpe** — the
draft had left the gloss in Latin only, so an English listener was never told it means fox's tail — and
**Amalfi's pergolas**.

**Reading 2.** The draft **told a legend straight that this ledger's own §6 lists among the eight that must
be staged**: the di Marzo family "growing a white grape on the hill above since the seventeenth century" is
the house's 1647 story, and the documentary date is an 1833 registration. Rewritten to the 1830s, with the
legend dropped rather than hedged, which also saves hedge budget. The **France-to-metodo-classico chain**
was asserted in the course's own voice where W34 labels it a house account: now "the house says". The
compass bearing "north-east of Avellino" was unsourced geography of exactly the kind §3 of this ledger
exists to prevent; cut. "A countryside that emptied twice" counted one documented emptying twice; now one.
Three losses restored: **Irpinia's climate** (the cold winters and the day-to-night swing, which had
vanished from the file entirely), **three of the four DOCGs coming off these hills**, and **the name
Sannio**, which is the word on the label.

**Reading 3.** Thirteen losses, the most of any reading in the rewrite so far, and the cause is
instructive: this reading was **rebuilt from the research rather than edited from the old text**, so it
kept every corrected claim and dropped the plain definitions around them. A reader was no longer told what
is on a marinara, what a babà is, that the DOP cheese is called Mozzarella di Bufala Campana, that pizza is
eaten folded into a *portafoglio*, or that Queen Margherita of Savoy had a name. All restored. Also fixed:
"an idea in 1884" (1884 is Serao's publication date, not the event's), "found nobody practising it" (the
2008 article found it unobserved *in the bars it visited*), "water buffalo are in southern Italy" followed
immediately by an example from Lazio, "the first records that actually count herds" (a superlative the
source does not make), and the Gragnano workshop counts, which are now attributed as tradition because no
census stands behind them. The reviewer's one borderline call — Rocco's toppings reading as a trio on one
pizza — was fixed by wording it as the research prescribes.

**Reading 4.** Every over-reach ran **in the direction of the better sentence**: "ten months unaltered"
became "a year", "the poorer citizens" became "most people", "read for a century" became "described in
every guidebook", "the first and second day of the eruption" became "the first morning", and an observed

they do the work of explaining that the wave was not the cause, and Roger II's 1131 is dropped.

### Two procedural lessons, for `docs/prose-plan.md`

1. **Where a reader review and a fact review collide, the fact wins and the fix is compression, not
   deletion.** The reader wanted the attribution tails cut from the alberata figures and from "the first red
   of the Italian south"; the fact reviewer required both attributions kept. Both were kept and shortened —
   "The figures come from two press accounts that share a phrasing", "It is usually called the first red of
   the Italian south to be promoted" — which satisfies both and is shorter than either draft.
2. **A reading rebuilt from research returns loses definitions, not claims.** Reading 3 is the proof: the
   corrected facts all survived and the ordinary explanatory sentences did not. Before review, diff the old
   text for every sentence that *defines* something (what a dish contains, what a word means, what a thing is
   called) and check each one against the draft. That is a different sweep from the fact ledger, which
   tracks claims.

## 9. The Norwegian edition

Written 2026-09-16 from the outline, the ledger and the facts — not translated from the English sentence by
sentence — then linted (`prose_lint.py campania --lang no`, clean at 30/35/35/32 words longest) and put
through four fresh-context reader reviews with the eleven §4b rules **inlined in the prompt**, one lesson
per agent. That inlining is the fix recorded in the Molise pass, where four agents made to read CLAUDE.md
first all stalled at 600 seconds with nothing to show; all four returned here, in two to five minutes each.

### What the reviews found, by category rather than by reading

**Four false friends that change a fact and pass every linter.** This is the category the Molise ledger
predicted, and it produced more here than there:
- «holder på formen av en gang» — *en gang* is heard as **once**, not as *a tunnel*, so the phylloxera
  mechanism collapsed in the one sentence that has to carry it. Recast as «en jord der gangene den graver,
  holder seg åpne».
- «Fiorelli fikk det til å virke» — *virke* is read first as **seem**, so it said he made the method look
  plausible, the opposite of the point. Now «var den som fikk metoden til å fungere».
- «Myren som tømte sletten» — reads as the marsh **draining** the plain, when the English means emptied
  **of people**, and the whole Paestum ending turns on it. Now «tømte sletten for folk».
- «under sytti hektar av dem» — *under* is taken as the preposition of place, so the terraces were
  **beneath** seventy hectares instead of amounting to fewer than seventy. Now «som til sammen er mindre
  enn sytti hektar».

**A hedge turned into an assertion, contradicting its own paragraph.** «Nowak har **vist** at …» means *has
demonstrated*, where the English says "has argued"; two sentences earlier the same paragraph says the
dispute is open. Now «har argumentert for at». **This is the single most dangerous class of error in the
Norwegian edition**, because it is invisible in the target language and no checker can see it.

**The heading-antecedent failure, for the third region running.** «Disse åsene» took its subject from an
`<h2>` two headings back, and since headings are not narrated, a listener attached it to the flat land at
Aversa — which the previous paragraph had just described. Worse than Molise's «Den eldste er Biferno»,
because it does not merely dangle, it contradicts. Readings 3 and 4 were checked and are clean.
**The sweep has to run on the draft before the reviews, not be left to the reviewers**, and it has to run
on both languages: the English reader review found the same defect in reading 4 («The column …»).

**Five invented compounds.** «nedfallsaske», «kvartalsblokk», «utbruddssøylen», «markssitronene» and
«blikkskjold» are all grammatical and none is a word anybody says — exactly the trap in the standing memory
note. Replaced with «asken som falt ned» / «askelaget», «bygård», «askesøylen», «frilandssitronene» and
«blikkbrett». «bronsetrekking» went too, partly because it collided aloud with «en trekking så langsom …»
later in the same reading. Verdicts worth keeping: «deigkule», «mesterpizzaioloer», «basaltgulvet» and
«terrassemur» are real and stay.

**Two rule-8 vocabulary breaches, one of them inside a tasting table.** «lang salt avslutning» for "long
savoury finish" and «salt fyll» for "savoury filling". The first mattered twice over, because a tasting
table and its card in `content/tasting.no.js` must move together (§13): the Taurasi card carried the same
wording, and the Lacryma Christi card had dropped "savoury" altogether. Both cards corrected;
`tastingcheck.py` passes at 89 wines and 89 cards a language.

**Number style split across one screen.** The prose used the pre-1951 counting order — «femogseksti»,
«seksogtyve», «femogtyve», «fireogtyve» — while the facts box two paragraphs down wrote the same facts as
«25 kommuner» and «700 000». Digits now throughout for these. Read aloud, the old order also sounds like a
different century, which is the reason it matters in a narrated course and not only on the page.

**Grammar proper:** a predicative in object case («kalte … de vanlige pizzaene **dem** med hvitløk»), a
participle chain opening the lead of reading 3 (rule 5, in the most exposed sentence there is), a verbless
second conjunct carried over from an English cleft, a bare plural subject with a «mens»-clause wedged
before its verb, and **«pizzeria» given neuter gender three times** with a pronoun following it into the
error. All fixed.

### What the reviewers asked to keep

«Det var sanden.» landing after «Alle går ut fra at vulkanen sørget for det» — the argument in four words,
with the cleft correctly refused. «Metoden reiste dit retten ikke kunne.» «Forklaringen på osten ligger i
melken.» «Der bærer pastaen det genovesiske navnet, ikke sausen.» «Mat med den prisen spises stående, og
mat som spises stående, må kunne brettes.» And «Smykker var lest som kvinnelighet og nærhet som familie, og
ingen av slutningene holdt», which reading 4's reviewer called the best sentence in that reading.
