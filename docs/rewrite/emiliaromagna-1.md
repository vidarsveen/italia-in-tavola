  Proposed: **Lambrusco, the wine that was two wines.**
- Summary now: *The frothy red of Emilia was Italy's most exported wine and its most mocked. What the grapes

## Paragraph by paragraph

Target 900–1,000 words of prose. Headings are labels for the eye and are not spoken, so each paragraph has to
arrive at the next on its own.

1. **Lead — a number with a consequence, then the pivot.** In 1985 one brand, Riunite, sold eleven and a half
   million cases in the United States, most of it sweet, poured over ice, sold with a jingle. It had been the
   best-selling imported wine in America for a decade, and it made the word a joke that sommeliers still wince
   at. Then the pivot: the same year, in a bar in Modena, what came out of the fridge was dry, dark purple,
   foaming violet, in a tumbler beside a piece of fried dough. Same name. Two wines. The reading is about how
   the gap opened and how it is closing.
   *Ledger: "thirteen million cases" corrected to 11.5 million and attributed to Riunite (Reuters 2012 via
   Wikipedia); "designed for its food" dropped, unprovable.*

2. **Where the bubbles came from — the farmer's calendar** (label: *Nobody invented the fizz*). Actors, event,
   result, because: a farmer pressed in October; November cold stopped the yeast before it finished; in spring
   he bottled what he had, sugar still in it; May warmed the cellar, the yeast woke and had nowhere to put the
   gas. By June every farmhouse on the plain had a cloudy, dry, sparkling red, and nobody had meant to make
   one. It was drunk inside the year because it would not keep. (This is PLAN §10b's worked example, kept.)

3. **The tank, and what it bought and cost** (label: *The tank*). Therefore-chain: the Martinotti tank, second
   fermentation in sealed steel, filtered and bottled under pressure, made the wine clean, cheap and the same
   every time; therefore it could be made sweet and shipped by the million; therefore Riunite; therefore the
   joke. Most Lambrusco is still made this way and, made dry, it is good. The tank is not the villain; the
   sugar was. *DOC dates (1970), the four sweetness words and frizzante/spumante go to the facts box.*

4. **Three grapes, as a walk, not a list** (label: *North of Modena, south of Modena*). Drive north out of
   Modena onto the sandy plain between the Secchia and the Panaro and the vine is Sorbara, the pale one, whose
   flowers often fail to set fruit, so growers plant Salamino among the rows to fill the bunches; it smells of
   sour cherry and violets and is nearly a rosé. Turn south into the foothills at Castelvetro and it is
   Grasparossa, dark, tannic, blackberry, the one that behaves like a red wine. Salamino, named for bunches
   shaped like small salami, sits between the two in taste and is planted more than either. *Maestri, Marani,
   Montericco (Reggio and Parma) and the Mantovano across the Po go to the facts box; Latin labrusca, Virgil
   and Pliny go to the box.*

5. **At the table — the dish first, then why** (label: *Gnocco fritto*). A pillow of fried dough torn open,
   steam, a slice of prosciutto pushed into it, a tumbler of cold Sorbara. Then the mechanism: Emilia cooks with
   pork fat, butter, egg pasta and Parmigiano, and the wine answers with three things at once, acidity, cold,
   and carbon dioxide scrubbing the mouth between bites. Widen: tigelle in the hills; zampone and cotechino at
   New Year, boiled for hours over lentils; the bollito misto trolley; Grasparossa for the roast and the aged
   cheese; Sorbara with fish, which no other red manages.

6. **Back to the bottle — the revival, with a person** (label: *Back to the bottle*). Cleto Chiarli, who
   founded his house in 1860, is the oldest name in the revival; around him small producers such as Cantina
   della Volta and Paltrinieri went back to refermenting in the bottle and leaving the wine on its sediment,
   cloudy as the farmhouse version, and a few went the whole way to the Champagne method. Pale Sorbara that
   could pass for something from somewhere grander; Grasparossa with real grip. *Bellei to the facts box (rule:
   three names in prose).*

7. **Ending — image and callback, no advice** (label: *Inside the year*). It will not keep, and it is not
   supposed to. It is made in the autumn to be finished by the next one, cold, in a tumbler, at a table with
   something fried on it, by people who have never once thought of it as a wine that needed to be understood.
   *The label guidance (secco, a named DOC not "dell'Emilia", rifermentato in bottiglia, metodo ancestrale,
   rosato Sorbara is natural) moves whole to the facts box.*

## Boxes (on screen only)

- **Key facts**: the three 1970 DOCs plus Modena, Reggiano, Colli di Parma; secco / semisecco / amabile /
  dolce; frizzante vs spumante; tank vs bottle vs traditional method; 10–12 °C; 10.5–12%; label words;
  Reggio/Parma varieties and Mantovano; labrusca and Pliny; Bellei among the revival names.
- **Tasting table** (Sorbara secco): unchanged.
- **Recap**: three bullets rewritten to match: one grape family, north pale and south dark; the calendar made the
  fizz and the tank made the boom; cold, dry and acidic against pork fat is why it exists.
