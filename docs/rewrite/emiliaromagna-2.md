## Paragraphs

1. **Lead, a person.** Pellegrino Artusi, a silk merchant born at Forlimpopoli in 1820, retired to Florence and
   at seventy-one published a cookbook at his own expense, because no publisher wanted it. It sold in the
   hundreds of thousands and was the first book to treat the cooking of the whole peninsula as one cuisine.
   He was a Romagnolo, and the hills he came from grow the grape the rest of Italy thinks of as Tuscan.
2. **Two regions and a hyphen** (label). Emilia to Bologna: the plain, pork, butter, Lambrusco. Romagna from
   Imola to the sea: hills, oil as well as butter, grilled meat, flatbread, Sangiovese. The name dispute handled
   as doubt: first written down in Tuscany in 1590; one story puts the birthplace on Monte Giove above
   Santarcangelo, monks, sanguis Jovis, and nobody can prove either. Romagna grows more Sangiovese than any
   region but Tuscany, and for most of the last century made it badly, sold in bulk or drunk from the
   demijohn.
3. **The hills behind the road** (label). Drive south off the Via Emilia and the plain lifts into a band of
   hills twenty kilometres deep, Imola to Rimini. Soil as you go: spungone at Bertinoro, clay and limestone at
   Predappio, gypsum and chalk at Brisighella, forest and cool air at Modigliana at 500 metres. Therefore-chain:
   the Adriatic is near, so warmer and more humid than Chianti, so black cherry rather than sour cherry, softer
   tannin. The turn: 1990s growers at Predappio and Modigliana bottling single hillsides; 2011 the 1967 DOC
   rewritten as Romagna DOC with named sub-zones. *Producer names go to the box (Nicolucci, Chiara Condello,
   Villa Papiano, Mutiliana, Fattoria Paradiso); Predappio as Mussolini's birthplace to the box.*
4. **Albana and the whites** (label). Thick tannic skins, golden, apricot and almond, a grip like a red;
   1987 first white DOCG "to some scorn"; the passito; Trebbiano by the carafe; Pagadebit, "pays the debts",
   the grape so reliable it covered a grower's bills.
5. **Artusi's table** (label). Piadina cooked on a hot plate at a kiosk on the coast, folded round prosciutto,
   rocket and squacquerone; cappelletti in capon broth at Christmas; passatelli pressed through a tool into
   broth; castrato at feasts; brodetto argued over port by port. Casa Artusi at Forlimpopoli, where the
   sfogline still teach the rolling pin.
6. **Ending, image.** The book's title, Science in the Kitchen and the Art of Eating Well, and the hills
   behind it; or Artusi's house as a cookery school. Callback to the lead.
