Opening moves and their grammatical shape, so no two neighbours share one (Veneto opened on a palace cellar running low
(situation), a quotation, fishermen drying cod on Røst (place and action) and a table of sugar napkins (object first);
Trentino 3 on a woman in a fresco, so Friuli 3 does not open on the portrait): 1 a named man's upbringing in a Udine inn
(person first, no date in the first sentence); 2 hail destroying a crop (event as subject); 3 unnamed men walking out of
Carnia with chests on their backs (a recurring action); 4 the words for coffee at a Trieste counter (a counter scene, the
listener hears the vocabulary). Neighbours already used: Veneto 2 told the Prosecco village near Trieste and Veneto 3 the
tiramisù claims of Tolmezzo and Pieris; Veneto 4 told Venice's coffee houses (1683, Florian), so Friuli 4's coffee is the
port, the duties, Illy and the counter, not a café history; Emilia-Romagna 3 has the horse-bone needle for Parma, so San
Daniele's is one clause; Liguria 4 and Trentino used the Council of Trent, so San Daniele's 1563 hams stay in the box.

Map facts used without a separate source (general geography): Isola d'Istria across the Gulf of Trieste from Grado;
Fagagna north-west of Udine; Cividale on the Natisone; Tolmezzo in Carnia; Paluzza and Cleulis in the But valley; the
Natisone valleys near the Slovene border above Cividale; Karst/Carso behind Trieste; Miramare up the coast from the city.
General history: Italy declared war on Austria-Hungary on 23 May 1915; Udine was the Venetian capital of Friuli.
General winemaking (the old text's "cold fermentation and steel tanks … clean, dry and intensely varietal"): a white
fermented warm in wood or cement loses scent; steel can be kept cool, closed and clean. General food science: roasted
coffee goes stale in air.

**Checked before drafting** (two research subagents, about fifty-five claims). Errors or overclaims in the old text:
- "the corner of Italy that most Italians have never visited": no source; **cut**.
- Grave spread by "the rivers Tagliamento and Meduna": the Tagliamento, Meduna and Cellina.
- COF "run north from Cormons past Cividale": Cormons is in the Collio; the COF run from Corno di Rosazzo and Manzano
  north past Cividale to Nimis.
- Ponca "holds little water": sandstone drains, marl holds moisture.
- "Roughly 60% of the region's wine is white, the highest share after Alto Adige": 86% in 2022, the highest in Italy.
- Schiopetto "an innkeeper's son from Capriva, went to Germany": grew up at his parents' Osteria ai Pompieri in Udine,
  a lorry driver who reached Alsace, Burgundy and the Rhineland; first Tocai in steel 1965; Capriva leased, bought 1989.
- "Between them they invented the modern Italian white": overstated; Schiopetto credited with the steel whites, Felluga
  with helping move the region from red to white. "sold in bulk … mostly as light red" → mostly red (bulk **cut**).
- "Jermann's Vintage Tunina and Felluga's Terre Alte … made the [Collio Bianco] category famous": neither is Collio
  Bianco (IGT Venezia Giulia; Rosazzo). They made the Friulian blends famous.
- Rocca Bernarda "the estate whose Picolit was the model for the DOCG": not verified; Perusini replanted Picolit there
  in the late nineteenth century.
- Carso "its own tradition of skin-contact whites": not verified as a tradition; "some growers" today.
- Friulano "most planted native grape": about level with Ribolla, far behind Pinot Grigio and Glera.
- "called Tocai Friulano for centuries": arrived nineteenth century, spelled Tocai early twentieth. "when Hungary joined
  the EU it won the argument": the 1993 agreement, eleven years before accession.
- "an old Bordeaux variety": from the Gironde.
- Tajut "at eleven in the morning", "the osterie where the tajut was invented": not verified; before lunch or dinner.
- Picolit "a sweet wine that Habsburg courts paid for in gold": not verified; Asquini's Murano bottles across Europe.
- Schioppettino "saved by one family, the Rapuzzi": kept, with the 1970 refusal and the 1976 Nonino prize.
- Pignolo "needs a decade": not verified; very tannic.
- Merlot "planted since the Habsburgs": about 1880 (Udine was Italian from 1866).
- San Daniele "2.7 million hams": 2.54 million in 2025; "sliced by hand thicker", "sweeter and more delicate than Parma":
  **cut**; "the town's festival every June is the region's biggest party": Aria di Festa ended after 2023.
- Montasio "made since the thirteenth century when the monks of Moggio organised the mountain dairies": consortium
  tradition, no document; first written mention a Udine price list of about 1773.
- Frico "the dish shepherds and woodcutters carried": folklore only; **cut**. "now on every menu in Udine" **cut**.
- Carnia's men "left every spring … came back in autumn with spices and raisins": left in autumn, back in late spring;
  what the museum documents them bringing home is religious art. The spices-in-cjarsons link is told as local lore.
- "toc' in braide, a soft polenta with cheese and butter": Gianni Cosetti's 1980s dish.
- Brovada "turnips buried in grape pomace after the harvest, left to ferment for a month or more": at least 25/30 days,
  red marc, covered with water, salt, red wine or vinegar allowed. Musetto "on Christmas Eve and New Year": New Year's
  Eve and New Year's Day.
- "The digestivo is grappa, which the region distils in quantity": about a tenth of Italy's grappa.
- Trieste "for two centuries the only port of the Habsburg empire": Fiume was made a free port the same day; Trieste
  became Austria's main port after Fiume went to Hungary in 1779.
- "insurance companies, Lloyd and Generali, that still bear the city's name": Generali still in Trieste; Lloyd became
  Lloyd Triestino and Italia Marittima.
- Illy "invented the pressurised coffee machine": the Illetta used compressed air instead of steam; not the espresso
  machine.
- "roasts, trades and drinks more of it per head than any in Italy": not verified; a 2012 survey, 7 kg against 5 kg.
- "Caffè Tommaseo … the oldest, from 1830": 1825 per the comune, 1830 per others; claims to be oldest.
- "ropes and chains fixed along the streets": until about the 1970s, some rings remain.
- "Triestini claim it clears the head": no source; **cut**.
- Aquileia "a hundred thousand people": weak; Ausonius's ninth city instead. "760 m², the largest of its age in the west":
  more than 760 m², "described as the largest early-Christian mosaic in the West" (not repeated in prose).
- Cividale "first capital of the Lombards": seat of the first Lombard duchy.
- Udine "a loggia copied from the Doge's palace": not verified; "Tiepolo's first great frescoes": early, not first.
