## A. Reading 1 — "The Castelli Romani and Frascati"

### W1. "the rim of a volcanic complex that last erupted tens of thousands of years ago"
**CONFIRMED on the date, CORRECTED on the implication, and the recap line is WRONG.**

- The old text's recap says "The Castelli Romani sit on an **extinct** volcano." That is wrong. Every
  institutional source calls it **quiescente** (dormant), not extinct.
- INGV: «L'ultimo centro eruttivo è quello di Albano che ha prodotto depositi di scorie con età di **36.000
  anni**» ("The last eruptive centre is Albano, which produced scoria deposits dated to 36,000 years"). The
  volcano is classified «quiescente» with a "Base" alert level, and the area shows «circolazione idrotermale,
  sismicità e sollevamento del suolo» — hydrothermal circulation, seismicity and ground uplift. Soil emits
  H₂S and CO₂, which INGV names as the main natural hazard of the district.
  https://www.ingv.it/colli-albani
- The academic literature agrees it is quiescent and disputes the last date: "Colli Albani volcano is
  unanimously considered to be quiescent and not extinct… controversy exists about the age of its most recent
  eruption (either 36 ka or <23 ka)"; the last eruption is dated **36 ± 1 ka** from the Albano composite maar,
  and some chronostratigraphy plus lahar deposits pushes activity into the Holocene (<10 ka).
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-65394-2 (Monti Sabatini and Colli Albani: the dormant twin
  volcanoes at the gates of Rome, *Scientific Reports* 2020)
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377027303000271 (Albano maar lake: recent volcanic
  activity and pre-Roman Age catastrophic lahars)
- **The uplift-and-seismicity literature the brief asked for:** an uplift of about **230 cm over 43 years**
  was measured by levelling surveys covering 1950–1993; seismic swarms occur beneath the crater zone and the
  emitted gas has magmatic affinity, which together are read as an active magma chamber. The most likely site
  of a future eruption is the deep crater in the southern part of Lake Albano. [snippet — from the search
  summary of the GPS-monitoring and volcanic-hazard papers; I did not open the PDFs]
  https://www.academia.edu/27952114/Volcanic_unrest_of_the_Colli_Albani_central_Italy_detected_by_GPS_monitoring_test
  https://rischi.protezionecivile.gov.it/it/vulcanico/vulcani-italia/colli-albani/
- The complex began erupting about **600,000 years ago**; three overlapping edifices (Vulcano Laziale, then
  the Tuscolano-Artemisio and Faete stratovolcanoes, then tuff cones and maars). (INGV, as above.)

**For the rewrite:** "a volcano that is sleeping, not dead, and has risen two metres under the vineyards in
living memory" is both true and better than "extinct".

### W2. The craters holding Lake Albano and Lake Nemi; depths and shape
**CORRECTED in one detail: neither lake is a single round crater.**

- **Lake Albano**: Parco dei Castelli Romani: «La sua profondità massima è di circa **170 metri**»; «occupa
  una superficie di circa **600 ettari**»; «si trova a circa **300 metri sul livello del mare**»; «ha una
  forma **ellittica originata dall'unione di due crateri**» — elliptical, formed by the union of two craters.
  It is the deepest volcanic lake in Italy.
  https://www.parcocastelliromani.it/s/content/92003020580/1664528508.885
  (Other sources describe the Albano depression as the product of at least five separate explosions.)
- **Lake Nemi**: Treccani, *Enciclopedia Italiana*: altitude «m. **318**», maximum depth «m. **34**», area
  «kmq. **1,67**», occupying «il fondo di **due antichi crateri laterali**… fusi insieme per il franamento
  delle sponde contigue» — two lateral craters fused by the collapse of the wall between them. Roughly 1.75 km
  long, 1.3 km at its widest, perimeter about 5.5 km.
  https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/lago-di-nemi_(Enciclopedia-Italiana)/
- So "deep, round" is loose for both: Albano is an ellipse from two craters, Nemi a figure-of-eight from two.
  The "startlingly blue" is the writer's, not a source's.

### W3. Soils "tuff and pozzolana, dark, porous, and rich in potassium"
**Tuff and pozzolana CONFIRMED. Potassium CONFIRMED at the rock level, UNVERIFIED at the soil level.
"Mineral edge" is a tasting convention, not a finding.**

- The rocks genuinely are potassium-rich, and unusually so: the Colli Albani belong to the **Roman Magmatic
  Province** and are built of «strongly silica-undersaturated leucite-bearing **ultrapotassic** rocks».
  Pre-caldera leucitites are "characterised by high levels of incompatible trace elements with **peaks at Cs,
  K, Th, U and Pb**".
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00445-009-0278-6 (Isotope geochemistry and petrogenesis of
  leucite-bearing volcanic rocks from Colli Albani, *Bulletin of Volcanology*)
  https://www.alexstrekeisen.it/english/provincie/index.php
- The Jackson/Marra work on Roman concrete independently calls the Pozzolane Rosse ash "scoriaceous, **highly
  potassic**". (See W4.)
- **What I could not find**: any soil study, or any line in the disciplinare, that measures potassium in
  Frascati's *soils* or links it to the wine. The Frascati Superiore rules describe only the landform and the
  drainage: «L'orografia collinare dell'areale di produzione costituita dalle **pendici settentrionali del
  vulcano Laziale**», with naturally well-draining volcanic soils.
  https://www.assovini.it/italia/lazio/item/231-frascati-superiore-docg
  The Frascati DOC text is vaguer still — it speaks only of «la natura del suolo e il clima temperato».
  https://www.agraria.org/vini/frascati-doc.htm
  The official catalogue text (catalogoviti.politicheagricole.it) refused connection twice, and the
  Consorzio's own PDF returned 429; the full art. 9 is the one primary source still unread.
- **Recommendation**: say the rock is potassium-rich (true, sourced, and unusual enough to be interesting) and
  drop the causal chain from potassium to "savoury, mineral edge", which no source supports.

### W4. "The same rock built Rome: the pozzolana that made Roman concrete set under water was quarried here."
**Half CONFIRMED, half WRONG. This is the clearest factual error in the two readings.**

- **True half — the Alban Hills built Rome.** The mortar aggregate of imperial Rome's concrete is the
  **Pozzolane Rosse** ignimbrite, «erupted at **456 ± 3 ka** from the **Alban Hills volcano**», used as
  pozzolanic mortar aggregate in Imperial-age monuments, including mortars from the Forum and Markets of
  Trajan (AD 96–115). Roman builders used this deposit essentially exclusively from about the time Augustus
  became emperor (27 BC). Marie Jackson (with Fabrizio Marra, INGV, and Barry Scheetz): "The mortar was very
  well compacted; the ratio of lime, pozzolanic volcanic ash and water was carefully controlled."
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/gea.20295 (Jackson et al., "Mid-Pleistocene pozzolanic
  volcanic ash in ancient Roman concretes", *Geoarchaeology* 2010 — abstract page returned 403, cited via the
  press write-up and search summaries) [snippet]
  https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna30723672
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1417456111 (Jackson et al., "Mechanical resilience and cementitious
  processes in Imperial Roman architectural mortar", *PNAS* 2014 — page returned 403) [snippet]
- **False half — the underwater concrete did NOT use Alban Hills ash.** The ash that let Roman concrete set
  in seawater is the **pulvis puteolanus** of the Bay of Naples: "The ancient Roman concretes were produced
  through reaction of seawater, lime calcined from limestone, and zeolitized volcanic ash, mainly from the
  **Campi Flegrei** volcano." Marie Jackson: "the Romans created a rock-like concrete that thrives in open
  chemical exchange with seawater"; the strength comes from **Al-tobermorite** and **phillipsite** crystals
  growing over centuries as seawater percolates through.
  https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2017/07/03/ancient-concrete-could-teach-us-to-do-as-romans-did/
  https://pubs.geoscienceworld.org/msa/ammin/article/102/7/1435/353606/ (Jackson et al., "Phillipsite and
  Al-tobermorite mineral cements…", *American Mineralogist* 2017)
- The ancient terminology matches: Vitruvius distinguishes **harenae fossiciae** (excavated sands, the Roman
  land material) from the **pulvis** of Baiae and Vesuvius that «is capable to solidify under water». Pliny,
  Strabo and Seneca all use *puteolanus*, "from Puteoli". Provenance work does find some Colli Albani material
  mixed into two Latium coastal fishponds (Punta della Vipera, Torre Astura), but the systematic marine choice
  was Phlegraean pumice and Neapolitan Yellow Tuff.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X15000139 ("Provenance materials for
  Vitruvius' harenae fossiciae and pulvis puteolanis", abstract page 403) [snippet]
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10011562/
- **Corrected sentence available to the rewrite**: the Alban Hills supplied the red ash that Rome's *standing*
  concrete — Trajan's markets, the great vaults — was made of; the ash that set under seawater came from
  Pozzuoli, 200 km south. The local claim is still strong, just not the marine one.

### W5. Villa Aldobrandini "finished in 1602 for Cardinal Pietro Aldobrandini"
**CONFIRMED on patron and date, INCOMPLETE on the architect and the water theatre.**

- Built for Cardinal **Pietro Aldobrandini** over four years, **1598–1602**, on land belonging to his uncle
  Pope **Clement VIII**, given as compensation for returning Ferrara to the Church.
- Works directed by **Giacomo della Porta**; after his death in 1602 completed by **Carlo Maderno** and
  **Giovanni Fontana**, who made the Teatro delle Acque, the terraces and the hydraulics. The water theatre is
  credited to Maderno with **Orazio Olivieri**; Fontana brought the water from springs on **Monte Algido**.
- Descent: to his niece Olimpia Aldobrandini; 1683 Giovanni Battista Pamphilj; 1760 Borghese; 1837 back to the
  Aldobrandini, who still own it. First performance of Stradella's *La Circe* there in 1668.
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Aldobrandini_(Frascati) (lead only; corroborated by)
  https://baroqueart.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=monument;BAR;it;Mon13;7;it
  https://www.baroque.it/luoghi-del-barocco/la-villa-nel-periodo-barocco/villa-aldobrandini.html
- If the reading says "finished in 1602", note that della Porta died that year and the gardens and water
  theatre came after him — the villa was habitable in 1602, finished later.

### W6. The *fraschetta* and the *frasca* over the door; bringing your own food
**CONFIRMED, including the bring-your-own custom, which has a name.**

- AIFB (Associazione Italiana Food Blogger, a documented-heritage piece): «La parola stessa rimanda alla
  **frasca**, il ramo verde che veniva appeso all'ingresso per segnalare che lì si vendeva **vino nuovo**.»
  The fraschetta began «non una cucina organizzata, ma una cantina aperta, un servizio di vino, un luogo in
  cui la tavola si costruiva attorno a ciò che si portava da casa o si acquistava nei dintorni».
  https://www.aifb.it/patrimonio-unesco/le-fraschette-romane-storie-di-vino-e-convivialita-nei-castelli-romani/
- **The bring-your-own custom has a documented name — use it.** «In questa abitudine rientra anche la
  tradizione dei **fagottari**: persone e famiglie che arrivavano con il fagotto del cibo preparato a casa e
  consumavano il vino acquistato nella fraschetta.» (*fagottari*, "bundle-people", from *fagotto*, a bundle.)
  Same source; the author says she did it herself at her own graduation party.
- A second account has the branch as usually **laurel**: «segnalate da una frasca quasi sempre di **alloro**
  appesa fuori l'ingresso del locale», and notes the fraschette «erano quasi tutte di proprietà dei produttori
  di vino» — owned by the growers themselves, which is the economic point.
  https://nando.roma.it/news/il-vino-e-le-fraschette-dei-castelli-romani/
- **Folk etymology flag**: a widely repeated claim derives the word from the borgo "Frascata" (today Frascati),
  said to be named for the huts of *frasche* built by woodcutters after Tusculum was destroyed in **1191**.
  That is a town-name etymology, told in tourist sources, and I found no scholarly backing. Treat the branch-
  over-the-door as documented custom and the Frascati-from-frasche derivation as tradition.
  https://www.informazioni-turistiche.it/le-fraschette-dei-castelli-romani-cosa-sono-e-perche-sono-cosi-famose/
- **Not found**: any municipal licence or regulation specifically governing fraschette. The AIFB piece has
  none. If the rewrite wants a regulation, the documented one is the wine-measure law (W7), not a tavern law.

### W7. The *fojetta* as "a half-litre jug" — and the rest of the Roman measures
**CONFIRMED, and the full ladder is better material than the single word.**

| Measure | Volume |
| --- | --- |
| **barzilai** (barzillai) | 2 l |
| **tubo** / tubbo | 1 l |
| **fojetta** / foglietta | ½ l |
| **quartino** (= mezza fojetta) | ¼ l |
| **chierichetto** / chirichetto | ⅕ l (200 ml) |
| **sospiro** / sottovoce | 1/10 l |
| **sciacquadenti** | 1/20 l (50 ml) |

https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unità_di_misura_del_vino_romano
https://www.rerumromanarum.com/2019/10/le-unita-di-misura-del-vino-roma.html
https://www.romatoday.it/eventi/cultura/recipienti-vino-litro-mezzo-litro-come-si-chiamano.html

- **The names are the story.** *Chierichetto*, "altar boy", because the 200 ml flask resembled the cruet the
  altar boy pours at Mass. *Barzilai* after **Salvatore Barzilai**, a Republican politician of the late
  nineteenth and early twentieth century, who at election time stood two litres of wine to anyone who voted
  for him — the two-litre measure took his name. *Sospiro*, "a sigh", for a tenth of a litre; *sciacquadenti*,
  "tooth-rinse", for a twentieth.
- **The regulation exists and is datable.** In **1588 Pope Sixtus V** regulated the glass measures to stop the
  fraud known as *sfogliettatura*; the correct level was marked on the glass by a line called the **capello**,
  which is the origin of the Roman phrase «**stai a guardà er capello**» ("you're staring at the hairline",
  i.e. quibbling over a hair's breadth). Under that standard the foglietta was **0.4557 l**, not a round half
  litre. One source adds that **Gregory XIII** introduced the *mezza fojetta* around 1580 to restrain Roman
  drinking. (Wikipedia and rerumromanarum; the underlying citation is the 1932 *Enciclopedia Italiana*.)
  [secondary]
- So "the fojetta (a half-litre jug)" is right in practice and slightly wrong in law: the papal foglietta was
  0.4557 l. That discrepancy — the law says 0.4557, the street says a half — is itself usable.

### W9. "Frascati DOC was created in 1966, one of Italy's earliest denominations"
**CONFIRMED and then some — this is stronger than the old text claims, and worth upgrading.**

- On **3 March 1966** a single presidential decree recognised **four** DOCs: **Vernaccia di San Gimignano,
  Est! Est!! Est!!! di Montefiascone, Ischia and Frascati**. Frascati was in the first four denominations
  Italy ever made — and so, remarkably, was Lazio's other white in this reading.
- The ranking is decided by publication in the *Gazzetta Ufficiale*: Vernaccia **6 May 1966**, Est! Est!! Est!!!
  **7 May**, Ischia **9 May**, Frascati **16 May**. Vernaccia is therefore called the first Italian DOC;
  Frascati's rules took effect **1 November 1966**.
  https://www.valoritalia.it/vernaccia-di-san-gimignano-la-prima-denominazione-riconosciuta-in-italia/
  https://www.wineshop.it/it/blog/i-vini-italiani-doc-e-doc-g-quali-sono-stati-i-primi.html
  https://www.beverfood.com/documenti/consorzio-tutela-denominazione-vini-frascati-storia-territorio-denominazione-vini-wd89475/
  (Consorzio's own words: «la Doc istituita nel 1966, **tra le prime quattro in Italia**»)
- Prior history: the Consorzio itself predates the DOC — «Il Consorzio di controllo fu costituito a Frascati
  il **23 Maggio 1949** su iniziativa di **18 produttori**», originally "Consorzio per la difesa di vini
  pregiati e tipici di Frascati". There is also a **DM 2 maggio 1933** delimiting the zone, referenced inside
  the current disciplinare — so the boundary is older than the DOC by a generation.
  https://www.lucianopignataro.it/a/la-storia-del-frascati-in-un-percorso-storico-millenario-garantito-igp/159169/
  https://www.agraria.org/vini/disciplinarefrascati-superiore.htm

### W10. Frascati Superiore DOCG and Cannellino di Frascati DOCG "promoted in 2011"
**CONFIRMED with exact dates; the old text gives no requirements, and the Cannellino number is worth having.**

- Both recognised by **D.M. 20 settembre 2011**, published **G.U. n. 240 del 14 ottobre 2011**.
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/disciplinari/frascati-superiore-docg/ (D.M. 20.09.2011, updated D.M. 07.03.2014)
  https://www.agraria.org/vini/disciplinarecannellino-di-frascati.htm
  (One source, it.wikipedia, gives 30 November 2011 for Cannellino; the 20 September / 14 October pair is
  given by more and better sources. **Sources disagree** — if a date is printed, use 20 September 2011 and do
  not lean on it hard.)
- **Frascati Superiore**: minimum total alcohol **12.00 % vol**; **Riserva** **13.00 % vol** with «**12 mesi**,
  a partire dal 1° novembre dell'anno di vendemmia, di cui **3 mesi di affinamento in bottiglia**». Maximum
  grape yield **11 t/ha**, maximum grape-to-wine yield **70 %**.
  https://www.agraria.org/vini/disciplinarefrascati-superiore.htm
- **Cannellino di Frascati**: minimum total alcohol **12.50 % vol**; **minimum residual reducing sugars 35 g/l**;
  maximum grape-to-wine yield **65 %**; «Le uve dovranno essere raccolte **tardivamente**» with partial
  *appassimento* permitted «anche in locali idonei» — i.e. the withering need not happen on the vine.
  https://www.agraria.org/vini/disciplinarecannellino-di-frascati.htm
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannellino_di_Frascati
- For scale: base **Frascati DOC** minimum is **11.50 % vol** (spumante 11.00 %).
  https://www.agraria.org/vini/frascati-doc.htm

### W12. Malvasia del Lazio = "Malvasia puntinata", named for speckles on the skin
**CORRECTED — the name is one dot, not speckles, and it is not on the skin generally.**

- «Il termine **Malvasia puntinata** deriva dall'**ombelico dell'acino** molto evidente, in forma di un
  **puntino grigio** facilmente riconoscibile» — from the very visible navel of the berry, a single grey dot
  at the tip where the flower was attached. Not freckling across the skin.
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/malvasia-puntinata/
- **A distinct variety, yes**: Malvasia del Lazio is registered in its own right in the national catalogue of
  vine varieties (given as **since 1970** by one source [secondary]), and «è erroneamente confusa con la
  Malvasia di Candia e la Malvasia del Chianti». Puntinata is a synonym of Malvasia del Lazio, not of Candia.
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/malvasia-del-lazio/
- **Why growers abandoned it, and the sentence to keep**: «La sua notevole **sensibilità alla maggior parte
  delle malattie della vite** ha portato molti produttori a sostituirla con la Malvasia di Candia, **più
  resistente ma di qualità decisamente inferiore**.» That is the real mechanism behind the old text's "more
  productive Malvasia di Candia" — it was disease resistance as much as yield.
- It also «ha una buona capacità di concentrare gli zuccheri», which is why it, not Candia, makes the
  Cannellino.
- **Not found**: any source for the specific aroma list in the old text (white peach, orange blossom, almond).
  That is tasting-note convention. Sourced and safe to keep is only "more aromatic and finer than Candia".
- **DNA gap**: I found no parentage study for Malvasia del Lazio. "Malvasia" is a group name, not a single
  lineage; do not imply the Lazio and Candia Malvasias are close relatives without a source.

### W13. Cannellino: November harvest, noble rot, "the hills' oldest luxury"
**Harvest CONFIRMED. Noble rot PARTLY CONFIRMED. Name CORRECTED. "Oldest luxury" UNVERIFIABLE — drop it.**

- **Late harvest**: confirmed by the rules (W10): late picking, over-ripening on the plant, partial withering
  permitted indoors. Popular sources add «grazie all'azione della **muffa nobile** o della surmaturazione
  sulla pianta» — botrytis *or* over-ripening. So "sometimes helped by noble rot" is defensible, but the
  disciplinare itself does not mention botrytis. Keep the hedge.
  https://www.wineshop.it/it/blog/cannellino-di-frascati-docg-origine-storia-vitigni-zona-di-produzione-vini-abbinamenti-e-curiosita.html
- **The name**: the usual derivation is **not** from anything sweet — «Il nome "Cannellino" deriva dal termine
  "**cannella**", storicamente usato per indicare i piccoli rubinetti delle botti, da cui si spillava il
  vino». The tap on the barrel. [secondary — a wine-shop blog and Wikipedia; no philological source found.
  Flag it as "the usual explanation" rather than as fact.]
- **"The hills' oldest luxury": UNVERIFIABLE, and the sources that claim it are selling something.** The
  strongest claim I found is a marketing line — that 2,000 years ago Cannellino was made from grapes picked at
  the end of October after drying on the vine and was the Romans' most prized sweet wine. No primary source is
  ever given for it; it appears on producer and retailer pages and is reproduced from one another.
  https://www.igrandivini.com/news/cannellino-di-frascati-docg/
  **What IS documented** for sweet wine at Frascati is much later and much thinner: Wikipedia's Cannellino
  entry can only offer general Roman-era viticulture (Cato on cultivation, Varro on the Tusculan *Vinalia*)
  and a medieval papal record of «**vigne sotto Frascati**» under **Pope Sergius I** (687–701) — vineyards,
  not sweet wine. The rewrite should say Cannellino's documented history begins in the twentieth century and
  its 2011 DOCG, and that everything before that is asserted rather than shown.

### W14. Est! Est!! Est!!! di Montefiascone
**The legend has a documented birth date in print — 1680 — which is a far better fact than the legend itself.**

- **The legend as told**: 1111, Henry V travelling to Rome for his imperial coronation from Pope Paschal II;
  in his suite a bishop, **Johannes Defuk**, «un vescovo, Johannes Defuk, **intenditore di vini**», who sends
  his cupbearer **Martino** ahead to chalk *Est* (for *est bonum*, "there is good [wine]") by the door of any
  inn worth stopping at. At Montefiascone Martino writes it three times. Defuk settles there and dies of it,
  traditionally in **1113**.
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est!_Est!!_Est!!!_di_Montefiascone
- **The tomb**: in the church of **San Flaviano**, Montefiascone. The epitaph, as transcribed: «**Est! Est!!
  Est!!! pr[opter] nim[ium] est hic jo[annes] de Fuk do[minus] meus mortuus est**» — "Est! Est!! Est!!!
  Because of too much *Est*, here my lord Johannes Defuk lies dead." Note the grammar of the inscription is
  itself the joke, and that the "my lord" implies the servant Martino set it up, which is how the legend is
  self-sealing.
- **The annual custom is part of the legend, and is described as lapsed**: «ad ogni anniversario della sua
  morte una **botticella di vino** venisse versata sul sepolcro», continuing «per diversi secoli». (Same
  Wikipedia entry.) [secondary]
- **Earliest attestation in print that I could find — and it is a debunking, not a telling**: **Johann Jacob
  Geysius, *Fabulam Montefiasconiam* (Altdorf, 1680)**, on «qvæ vulgò circumferuntur» — "the things commonly
  circulated" — about «Bibulum qvodam Germano», a certain German drinker who died at Montefiascone of too
  much wine. Cited in the bibliography of the Italian Wikipedia entry. So by **1680** the story was already
  old enough to be "commonly circulated" and to be worth a Latin dissertation, and the title word is *fabula*,
  fable. I could not find a telling earlier than that, nor any twelfth-century document naming Defuk.
- **Sources disagree on who Defuk was**: variously a bishop, a baron, a duke; the surname is also given as
  Defuk, Deuc, de Fuk or Fugger. The Fugger identification is a modern guess with no documentary link. Masters
  of Wine describe the tale as apocryphal.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est!_Est!!_Est!!!_di_Montefiascone
- **The DOC**: recognised **3 March 1966** in the same decree as Frascati (W9), published **7 May 1966**.
  Grapes: **Trebbiano toscano** (locally *Procanico*) **50–65 %**, **Trebbiano giallo** (locally **Rossetto**)
  **5–40 %**, **Malvasia bianca lunga and/or Malvasia del Lazio 10–20 %**, other non-aromatic whites up to
  35 %. Types: base, **Classico** (Montefiascone and Bolsena) and **Spumante**. Communes: Montefiascone,
  Bolsena, San Lorenzo Nuovo, Grotte di Castro, Gradoli, Capodimonte, Marta (province of Viterbo).
  https://www.arsial.it/est-est-est-di-montefiascone-doc/
  **Correction to the old text**, which says "a blend of Trebbiano and Malvasia": true but flat. The named
  local varieties — Procanico and **Rossetto** — are the interesting part, and Rossetto is Lazio's own name
  for Trebbiano giallo.
- **Distance**: Rome to Montefiascone is about **81 km** in a straight line [calculated: Rome 41.90 N /
  12.50 E, Montefiascone 42.54 N / 12.03 E] and roughly 100 km by the Via Cassia. "A hundred kilometres
  north" is fine for the road, generous for the crow.

## B. Reading 2 — "Cesanese: Lazio's forgotten red"

### W15. Cesanese: native red; the two types; DNA
**Native status CONFIRMED and unusually well-supported. "Two vines" CORRECTED to "two biotypes". Affile
origin UNVERIFIABLE.**

- **Genetically isolated, which is a strong claim in its favour**: recent genetic studies show Cesanese
  d'Affile and Cesanese comune «non hanno alcuna parentela con nessun altro vitigno italiano» — no
  relationship with any other Italian variety — «ma **tra loro a livello genetico sono molto simili**».
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/cesanese-di-affile/
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/cesanese-comune/
- **Biotypes, not two varieties**: «Cesanese comune e Cesanese d'Affile sono **due biotipi differenti** che
  non si distinguono molto a livello sensoriale o genetico, ma sono distinguibili a livello **ampelografico**».
  The old text's "There are two Cesanese vines" overstates the distinction. They are separately registered in
  the national catalogue (Cesanese d'Affile code 061) but they are not genetically two grapes.
  http://catalogoviti.politicheagricole.it/scheda.php?codice=061
- **The berry difference CONFIRMED**: Affile has «acini medio-piccoli, sferoidali, con **buccia spessa** e
  consistente, di colore blu-nero intenso, **ricca di antociani e tannini**» — smaller berries, thicker skins,
  more colour and tannin. Comune is the vigorous, larger-berried type planted for volume. So the old text's
  substance is right even though its category is wrong.
- **A finding the rewrite could use**: the project «**Alle Radici del Cesanese**» (Comune di Olevano Romano,
  GAL Terre di Pregio, ARSIAL, CREA Viticoltura di Conegliano) DNA-tested vines in historic vineyards and
  found that what is actually growing in the denomination zones traces back to **a single type: Cesanese
  d'Affile**. That is: the "common" Cesanese in the old vineyards largely is not common at all.
  https://www.carlozucchetti.it/cesanese-un-solo-vitigno-affile/
  https://www.triplea.it/it/magazine/appunti-dal-catalogo/vitizionario-il-cesanese-2453.html
- **Odd finding worth a footnote, not a sentence**: a single Cesanese comune accession held at Conegliano —
  *not* from Affile, Olevano or Piglio — shows a DNA relationship to **Coda di Volpe**, a Campanian white.
  One accession is not a parentage.
- **"Thought to have originated at Affile": UNVERIFIABLE.** The variety carries the village name and the
  village is where the finer biotype is associated, but no source I found documents an origin there. The name
  *Cesanese* is itself unexplained: «origini antiche delle quali non si hanno ancora notizie certe». Say the
  name is not understood rather than inventing a derivation.
  https://www.viviilvino.it/vitigni/cesanese/

### W16. Late ripening "well into October", rot, and the off-dry/fizzy past
**CONFIRMED, all three, and the last one has a clean source.**

- Ripening: «matura tra la fine di settembre e i primi di ottobre», harvest «generalmente nella prima metà di
  ottobre», with maturation concluding «nella seconda metà di ottobre» in traditional practice. At Olevano,
  the slow complete ripening «si protrae talvolta fino a **novembre**».
  https://www.viviilvino.it/vitigni/cesanese/ ; https://www.lavinium.it/le-doc-del-lazio-cesanese-di-olevano-romano/
- Rot: «richiede forme di allevamento che garantiscano un buon controllo per limitare il **marciume del
  grappolo compatto**»; the vine is «predisposta alla peronospora e **molto sensibile al marciume**,
  specialmente nelle annate piovose». The compact bunch is the mechanism — worth naming.
- **The sweet/fizzy past, sourced**: «Tradizionalmente questo vitigno, che accumula naturalmente zuccheri,
  veniva utilizzato per produrre vini semplici, **spesso amabili e leggermente frizzanti**, destinati al
  consumo immediato.» That is exactly the old text's claim, confirmed.
- Corroboration in the rules themselves: the Olevano Romano DOC still lists **amabile, dolce and dolce
  frizzante** as legal types alongside secco, Superiore and Riserva. The sweet fizzy past is not folklore; it
  is still in the disciplinare.
  https://www.lavinium.it/le-doc-del-lazio-cesanese-di-olevano-romano/

### W17. Ciociaria named after the *ciocie*
**CONFIRMED, with a sting the old text misses.**

- Treccani: «**ciociaro**… deriva da **ciocia**, perché nel passato era la calzatura tipica di questa gente»;
  the regional name «di uso popolare, deriva da quello delle **ciocie**, caratteristiche calzature portate un
  tempo da contadini e pastori».
  https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/ciociaro/ ; https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ciociaria/
- **The sting**: the name began as a Roman put-down. It comes «dal termine **dispregiativo**, nel dialetto
  romano, applicato ad alcuni poveri pastori a causa delle loro calzature». Rome named the district after the
  shoes of its poorest visitors. The old text's neutral "the country that Romans call Ciociaria" misses that
  this is Rome's word for it, not the district's own.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciociaria
- The shoe itself: a triangle of leather forming the sole, laced up the calf with leather thongs (*strenghe*,
  *curiole*, *corregge*), possibly descended from the Roman *soccus*.

### W18. Cesanese del Piglio DOCG 2008 — "Lazio's first red at that level"
**CORRECTED: it was Lazio's first DOCG of ANY colour, not just its first red. The old text undersells it.**

- **D.M. 1 agosto 2008**, G.U. n. 192 del 18 agosto 2008. Previously DOC by **D.P.R. 29 maggio 1973**.
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/cesanese-del-piglio-o-piglio-docg/
- It was **the first DOCG in Lazio, full stop** — Frascati Superiore and Cannellino only followed in 2011
  (W10), and Lazio had no other DOCG before 2008. Bibenda's headline: "IL CESANESE DEL PIGLIO: **LA PRIMA
  DOCG DEL LAZIO**".
  https://www.bibenda.it/attivita_singola.php?id=708
  http://percorsidivino.blogspot.com/2008/05/arriva-la-prima-docg-lazio-e-il.html
  (Some sources hedge it as "the first Lazio denomination for a red wine to reach that level", which is what
  the old text followed. The unhedged claim is the correct one.)
- **Communes**: «tutto il territorio comunale di **Piglio e Serrone** e parte del territorio di **Acuto,
  Anagni e Paliano**», province of Frosinone. The old text names Piglio, Anagni and Acuto and **omits Serrone
  and Paliano** — and Serrone is one of the two communes wholly inside the zone.
- **Composition**: «**Cesanese di Affile e/o Cesanese comune 90 % minimo**», other red varieties authorised in
  Lazio up to 10 %.
- **Rules**, minimum total alcohol at release: base **12 % vol**, **Superiore 13 % vol**, **Riserva 14 % vol**.
  Yields 11 t/ha base, 9 t/ha Superiore; grape-to-wine 65 %. Base not released before **1 February** after the
  harvest; **Riserva** «almeno **20 mesi** di invecchiamento, dei quali non meno di **6 mesi in bottiglia**».
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/cesanese-del-piglio-o-piglio-docg/
  https://www.agraria.org/vini/disciplinarecesanesedelpiglio.htm
  (**Sources disagree slightly** on whether the 12.5 % figure that circulates is the minimum *natural*
  alcohol of the Superiore grapes versus the 13 % of the finished wine. The finished-wine ladder 12 / 13 / 14
  is the one to print.)
- **The zone is mostly not vineyard**, which is the real story: the delimited territory is «circa **15.317
  ettari**» at 220–980 m on the slopes of the **Monti Ernici**, of which only about **144 hectares** were
  actually under vine for the DOCG (ARSIAL 2020 data).

### W19. Cesanese di Affile DOC and Cesanese di Olevano Romano DOC
**CONFIRMED, both by the same decree, and the Affile zone is genuinely tiny.**

- Both recognised **D.P.R. 29 maggio 1973** — the same day as Cesanese del Piglio's DOC.
- **Cesanese di Affile o Affile DOC**: communes of **Affile, Roiate, Arcinazzo Romano** (province of Rome);
  territory about **1,380 ha** in the valleys of the Monti Affilani; yield limit 65 hl/ha. Soils here are
  **not volcanic**: «terreni residuali, in prevalenza **terre rosse** derivate dall'erosione e
  decarbonatazione di rocce sedimentarie calcaree… di colore rosso scuro per gli ossidi liberi di ferro e
  alluminio». That contrast with the Castelli's volcanic soils is a gift to the rewrite.
  https://www.lavinium.it/le-doc-del-lazio-cesanese-di-affile-o-affile/
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/cesanese-di-affile-o-affile-doc/
  National planted area of the **Cesanese d'Affile variety**: about **372 hectares** (i.e. the grape, across
  Italy — larger than the Affile DOC zone).
- **Cesanese di Olevano Romano o Olevano Romano DOC**: **Olevano Romano** entire plus part of **Genazzano**,
  province of Rome; territory about **3,685 ha**; 211–571 m, west/south-west exposure; rainfall
  1,431–1,606 mm. Composition **min. 85 % Cesanese d'Affile and/or comune**, other reds to 15 %. Minimum
  alcohol **11.50 %** base (12 t/ha), **12.00 %** for Superiore and Riserva (10 t/ha); Riserva «un periodo di
  invecchiamento non inferiore a **24 mesi**», at least 6 in bottle. Types include amabile, dolce and dolce
  frizzante (W16). Documented viticulture back to Roman times, **1364** communal statutes regulating
  vineyards, and a wine festival running since **1929**.
  https://www.lavinium.it/le-doc-del-lazio-cesanese-di-olevano-romano/
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesanese_di_Olevano_Romano_DOC
- **Note the descending hierarchy**: Olevano's *minimum* (11.5 %) is lower than Piglio's (12 %), and Piglio
  is the DOCG. The old text implies Affile is the quality zone; in the rules, Piglio is the strict one.

- **Cesanese del Piglio DOCG**: about **170 hectares** under vine and about **450,000 bottles a year**, of

  https://www.ciociariaturismo.it/cesanese-del-piglio-d-o-c-g/

  https://www.wine-searcher.com/regions-cesanese+del+piglio

### W21. Damiano Ciolli and the other names of the revival
**Ciolli CONFIRMED with dates. Other names CONFIRMED but the 1990s framing is CORRECTED to the 2000s.**

- **Damiano Ciolli**, Olevano Romano, at about 600 m: winery founded and first bottling **2001**, though the
  family had worked the vineyards for at least four generations. His grandfather **Guido** and father
  **Costantino**, like most Olevano growers of their time, «used to make wine to sell **in bulk** in nearby
  Rome» — which is precisely the before-and-after the rewrite needs. [producer, corroborated by importers]
  https://www.damianociolli.it/lazienda/ ; https://www.damianociolli.it/en/damiano-ciolli-cesanese-di-olevano-romano/
- **The vineyard with a date**: *Cirsium* comes from a single hectare of 100 % Cesanese d'Affile **planted in
  1953 by Damiano's grandfather**, originally bush-trained (alberello) and later converted. *Silene*, named
  for the wildflower *Silene vulgaris* growing in the rows, comes from vineyards planted **1981 and 2002**.
  So the grandfather's 1953 vines are the physical link between the bulk era and the revival — one family,
  one plot, two completely different wines.
  https://www.damianociolli.it/en/cirsium-en/ [producer]
- **Antonello Coletti Conti** (Anagni): family producing since 1850 by their own account, but **the first
  vintage bottled under his own name was 2003**, not the 1990s. He is repeatedly named first among those who
  started the turn, followed by **Perinelli** and **Marcella Giuliani**.
  https://www.lavinium.it/portfolio/coletti-conti-azienda-agricola-anton-maria-coletti-conti/ [producer/press]
  https://www.winetaste.it/azienda-coletti-conti-grande-rappresentante-del-cesanese-del-piglio/amp/
- **Marcella Giuliani** (Anagni): the estate «ha preso nuova forma nel **2002**», from family property dating
  to the late 1800s, organic from the start.
  https://www.visitanagni.com/aziende-item.php?id=1334
- **Corte dei Papi** (Anagni): named as one of «le poche eccezioni» that stood out in Cesanese production.
  I found no founding date — do not print one.
- **Casale della Ioria** and **Giovanni Terenzi** (Serrone) also recur in the sources as Piglio names; Terenzi
  is treated as the old-guard grower "alle origini del Cesanese del Piglio". Not verified further.
  https://www.winemag.it/origini-cesanese-del-piglio-giovanni-terenzi/
- **CORRECTION to the old text's timeline**: it says "the turn came in the 1990s and 2000s". Every dated
  first vintage I could find is **2001–2003**. The revival is a 2000s phenomenon, and the DOCG of 2008 came
  only five to seven years after the first serious bottles — which is fast, and is a better fact than a
  vague decade.

### W22. The other reds and a sweet one
**All three CONFIRMED; Atina's story is much better than the old text's one clause.**

- **Nero Buono di Cori**: «vitigno autoctono laziale coltivato **quasi esclusivamente** all'interno del
  territorio del comune di **Cori**», province of Latina; «origini pressoché sconosciute»; vines of moderate
  vigour with «produzione scarsa ma costante»; sensitive to downy mildew, which the airflow of the volcanic
  **Monti Lepini** hills mitigates. **Cori DOC Nero buono**: min. 85 % Nero buono.
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/nero-buono/ ; https://www.quattrocalici.it/tipologie-vino/cori-doc-nero-buono/
- **Atina DOC and Pasquale Visocchi — the old text says "French varieties planted in the nineteenth century"
  and leaves out the man and the dates.** The agronomist **Pasquale Visocchi** began importing French vines
  to the **Valle di Comino** from **1860**: Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah and Gamay,
  plus Pinot bianco, Semillon and Roussanne. The **Fratelli Visocchi** winery started operating about
  **1868**, processing some **4,000 quintals** of grapes and exporting; that production ran for nearly a
  century. Sources call the valley the first area in newly unified Italy where these French varieties were
  grown. The **Atina DOC** was obtained in **1999**, led by **Giovanni Palombo**.
  https://discoverplaces.travel/en/stories/food-wine/atina-doc-wine-and-pasquale-visocchi
  https://www.aislazio.it/atina-doc-quando-la-francia-divenne-valle-di-comino/
  https://www.ciociariaturismo.it/atina-d-o-c/
  (**Sources disagree** on 1860 versus 1868: 1860 is the first importation, 1868 the winery. Both appear.)
- **Aleatico di Gradoli DOC**: recognised **21 June 1972**. Communes **Gradoli, Grotte di Castro, San Lorenzo
  Nuovo** and part of **Latera** (province of Viterbo); **minimum 95 % Aleatico**. Besides the sweet red there
  is a **liquoroso** and a **liquoroso riserva**: the riserva needs «un periodo di invecchiamento di almeno
  **due anni** dalla data di fortificazione, in **botti di rovere** di capacità non superiore a **250 litri**»
  plus bottle ageing, and reaches **17.50 % vol** total, of which at least **15 % naturally developed**.
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/aleatico-di-gradoli-doc/
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/tipologie-vino/aleatico-di-gradoli-doc-liquoroso-riserva/
  The old text's "smells of roses and Muscat" is conventional Aleatico description, not sourced here.

### W25. Belli, 3 October 1831 — the best documented opening I found
Giuseppe Gioachino Belli, sonnet **«Er vino»**, composed at **Terni, 3 October 1831**. Full text (romanesco):

> Er vino è ssempre vino, Lutucarda: / Indove vòi trovà ppiú mmejjo cosa? / Ma gguarda cqui ssi cche ccolore!,
> guarda! / Nun pare un'ambra? senza un fir de posa! / Questo t'aridà fforza, t'ariscarda, / Te fa vviení la
> vojja d'èsse sposa: / E vva', si mmaggni 'na quajja-lommarda, / Un goccetto e arifai bbocc'odorosa. / È
> bbono assciutto, dorce, tonnarello, / Solo e ccor pane in zuppa, e, ssi è ssincero, / Te se confà a lo
> stommico e ar ciarvello. / È bbono bbianco, è bbono rosso e nnero; / **De Ggenzano, d'Orvieti e
> Vviggnanello: / Ma l'este-este è un paradiso vero!**

https://poesieromanesche.altervista.org/index.php/Poetiscrittori/sonetto/233/giuseppe-gioacchino-belli-er-vino

Why it is useful: a Roman, in 1831, running through the wines available to him — **Genzano** (a Castelli town),
Orvieto, Vignanello — and finishing «ma l'**este-este** è un paradiso vero», *but Est! Est! is a real paradise*.
One documented voice, with a date, that ties the Castelli and Montefiascone together and settles the reading's
two halves in fourteen lines. It also records the vocabulary: wine judged *assciutto* (dry), *dorce* (sweet),
*tonnarello*, drunk *cor pane in zuppa*, and above all **sincero** — unadulterated.

### W26. Belli, 10 April 1834 — the fraud, from inside the osteria
Sonnet **«Li vini d'una vorta»** ("the wines of the old days"), **10 April 1834**:

> A ttempi ch'ero regazzotto, allora / Ereno l'anni de ruzzà ccor vino: / Ché sse fasceva er còttimo, ar
> Grottino, / De bbeve, a ssette e a ssei cuadrini l'ora. // E mm'aricorderò ssempr'a **Mmarino**, / Indove
> tutti l'anni annàmio fora / D'**ottobre** a vvilleggià cco la Siggnora, / E cce stàmio inzinent'a
> Ssammartino. // **Llà nnun c'ereno vini misturati / Co cciammelle de sorfo, e cquadrinacci, / E
> mmunizzione, e ttant'artri peccati.** // Bevevio un **quartarolo**, e ddiscevio: essci: / E er vino
> essciva: e vvoi, bbon prò vve facci, / 'Na pissciata, e ssinceri com'e ppessci.

http://www.ggbellimosetti.altervista.org/TESTI/li_vini_d_una_vorta.htm

Why it is useful, and it is the single richest item in this file:
- **It names the adulteration.** Belli's Romans knew their city wine was doctored — with sulphur rings
  (*ciammelle de sorfo*), with *quadrinacci* (bad coin / base metal) and *munizione*. Up at **Marino**, in the
  Castelli, «nun c'ereno vini misturati». The country wine was *sincero*; the city wine was not. That is a
  documented 1834 complaint about wine fraud at exactly the point where the reading needs one.
- **It dates the going-up.** The family went out to Marino «tutti l'anni… **d'ottobre** a vvilleggià» and
  stayed «inzinent'a **Ssammartino**» — until St Martin's, 11 November, which is the traditional day the new
  wine is broached. October to Martinmas, every year, with the household.
- **It has the measure in it**: «bevevio un **quartarolo**» — and the flat-rate drinking deal (*còttimo*)
  at six or seven *quattrini* an hour at an osteria called **er Grottino**. Drink by the hour, at a fixed
  price.

### W27. The papal court at the Castelli: two datable anchors
- **Castel Gandolfo becomes papal.** The town «fu incorporato nei possedimenti della Santa Sede» in **1604**;
  the palace was built from **1626** by **Urban VIII** (Barberini) to **Carlo Maderno**'s design, and
  Urban VIII made the first papal stay there on **10 May 1626**. Alexander VII completed it with a piazza
  façade by Bernini. It stands on the ruins of **Domitian's** Alban villa. After 1870 came «60 anni di
  abbandono»; it returned to use only with the **Lateran Pacts of 1929**.
  https://www.vaticannews.va/it/papa/news/2025-07/castel-gandolfo-estate-papa-leone-xiv-palazzo-apostolico-ville.html
  https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Pontificio
  **So the old text's papal framing is right but a generation out**: the cardinals' villas at Frascati
  (Aldobrandini, 1598–1602) come first; the popes' own house at Castel Gandolfo comes a quarter-century later.
- **Sante Lancerio, the pope's wine steward.** Bottigliere to **Paul III Farnese (1534–1549)**, he wrote a
  letter to **Cardinal Guido Ascanio Sforza** judging some fifty Italian wines — «giustamente considerata la
  prima testimonianza della letteratura enologica italiana», analysing taste, aftertaste, appearance and
  aroma. It was first published by **Prof. Giuseppe Ferraro in 1876** as *I vini d'Italia giudicati da Papa
  Paolo III (Farnese) e dal suo bottigliere Sante Lancerio*. Secondary sources state that Lancerio judged the
  best wines to be those of **Frascati, Marino and Grottaferrata**.
  https://lafillossera.com/2020/04/28/il-sommelier-del-papa-sante-lancerio-bottigliere-alla-corte-di-paolo-iii-farnese/
  https://www.quattrocalici.it/conoscere-il-vino/sante-lancerio/
  https://www.lucianopignataro.it/a/la-storia-del-frascati-in-un-percorso-storico-millenario-garantito-igp/159169/
  **[secondary — IMPORTANT] I could not open Lancerio's own words.** Both pages I fetched quote him on other
  wines but not on Frascati. If the rewrite wants to quote the pope's sommelier on Frascati, the 1876 Ferraro
  edition must be checked first. As it stands, the claim "Paul III's wine steward rated Frascati among the
  best in Italy" is reported by three independent Italian sources but not read at the source.

### W28. The osteria economy of papal Rome, with a number

### W29. The wine carts (*carretti a vino*)
Documented in detail, but **the end date rests on a single source I could not open.**

- The cart: a two-wheeled vehicle with tall wheels and a folding decorated canopy (*furcina*) hung with
  pendants, fringes and bells, drawn by one horse. The barrels, «coperti da una stuoia, avevano una capacità
  di **sessanta litri**, pari a mezza botte, detta "**un carretto**"» — sixty litres, half a barrel, and the
  measure took the cart's own name.
- The journey: down the **Appia** or the **Tuscolana** into Rome at **four to five km/h**, «generalmente di
  notte» with lanterns; on arrival the carter paid a toll at **Porta San Giovanni**, and porters unloaded the
  barrels at the taverns that employed the carters.
- The carter's dress, which is a portrait ready-made: velvet breeches tight at the knee, red stockings, black
  shoes often with gold or silver buckles, a white or coloured scarf at the neck, a velvet jacket over the
  shirt.
  https://www.controluce.it/carretti-a-vino/
- **The end**: one source states that the carts ran until **1917**, when a contract signed at the end of
  **July 1917**, effective that **October**, replaced them with motor transport — though carts survived for
  some decades after in practice. The page (lavocedeicastelli.com, "Carretti a vino: 100 anni fa la loro
  ultima corsa") returned HTTP 500 and I could only see the search summary. **[snippet — single source,
  unopened. Do not print 1917 as fact without re-checking.]**
  https://www.lavocedeicastelli.com/rubriche/cultura-tempo-libero/carretti-a-vino-100-anni-fa-la-loro-ultima-corsa/

### W30. Why Frascati went industrial: phylloxera, then volume
No single fraud or scandal explains it. Two separate mechanisms, forty years apart.

**(a) Phylloxera and the replanting, 1879–1891.** Phylloxera reached Italy in **1879**. In Lazio, and
especially in the Castelli, it spread «in modo **lento e sporadico**» — isolation and destruction of infested
vineyards, soil disinfestation, and the sandy/siliceous soil texture that hindered the insect's migration
between vines. In **1891** the **Regia Cantina Sperimentale** was founded at **Velletri** at the instance of
**Menotti Garibaldi**, Giuseppe's son; there and at the **Consorzio Provinciale per la Viticoltura di
Frascati** a nursery of American mother-vines was set up to produce resistant rootstock and to teach growers
to graft. The same source contains the line the rewrite most wants: Castelli wines went from being among the
most expensive in Italy at the end of the nineteenth century to roughly the opposite today.
**[snippet — the terrelab page redirected to a commercial site and the text survives only in the search
summary; the claim is attributed to a book on viticulture in Rome and the Castelli Romani whose author I
could not confirm. Treat the 1891/Menotti Garibaldi detail as a strong lead to verify, not as verified.]**
https://www.terrelab.it/2017/11/la-viticoltura-a-roma-e-nei-castelli-romani-un-libro-pieno-di-idee-terrelab/

**(b) The volume decades, 1960s–1980s — this is the documented reason.** Verbatim:
- «Negli anni '60 negli anni '70, il Frascati **imperversa in ogni dove**» — Frascati was everywhere; the
  cellars turned to the supermarkets «indotte a competere **sui costi e non sulla qualità**».
- «Negli anni '80 si iniziano a vendere **quantità di Frascati esorbitanti**.»
- The method: «**Malvasia di Candia** e delle grandi quantità di uve coltivate **a tendone**» — the resistant,
  lower-quality Malvasia (W12) on high-yielding overhead pergolas.
- The verdict: «il Frascati in nome del profitto **in vent'anni si brucia secoli di onorata reputazione**» —
  in twenty years, for profit, Frascati burned centuries of honoured reputation. Prices collapsed, vineyards
  disappeared, and «le industrie man mano prendono il posto delle cantine».
- The turn: «**all'inizio del nuovo millennio**… un gruppo di giovani vignaioli sceglie di tornare a credere
  in questo territorio».
  https://www.mangiaebevi.it/il-frascati-alla-riscossa-la-rinascita-del-grande-vino-dei-papi/
- **No fraud scandal found.** I looked for a mass adulteration event and found none for Frascati. The
  adulteration that IS documented is Belli's, in 1834, and it is about Rome's osterie, not the twentieth
  century (W26). Do not manufacture a scandal; the tendone and the supermarket are the honest explanation.

**(c) The co-operative, with dates.** **Cantina Sociale Cooperativa di Marino**, later **Gotto d'Oro**:
founded **10 July 1945** by **41 growers** in a half-wrecked state plant at Ciampino; Marino DOC followed the
Frascati DOC in **1970**; new works at **Frattocchie opened in 1973**. Today about **150 members**, 1,000 ha,
70,000+ quintals of grapes and an average **5,100,000 bottles a year** (other sources say up to ten million),
holding **95 %** of Marino DOC, **20 %** of Castelli Romani DOC and **15 %** of Frascati DOC.
https://www.gottodoro.it/en/history/ [producer]
https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantina_sociale_Gotto_d'oro

### W31. The named grower who made the quality turn at Frascati, with a date
**Castel De Paolis, Grottaferrata, 1985.** Founded by **Giulio Santarelli** — at the time
**Sottosegretario di Stato al Ministero dell'Agricoltura e delle Foreste**, an undersecretary of agriculture
planting a vineyard — together with **Professor Attilio Scienza**, with researchers of the **Istituto Agrario
di San Michele all'Adige**. The 13 hectares came out of a pioneering study run by Scienza between **1985 and
1992** to recover indigenous Lazio varieties «many of which had nearly disappeared after **phylloxera** and
the introduction of international varieties». Vineyards at about **270 m**.
https://vinodabere.it/castel-de-paolis-nei-castelli-romani-un-esempio-di-valorizzazione-territoriale/
https://theitalianwinegirl.com/castel-de-paolis-winery-where-frascati-becomes-aristocratic/
https://www.lucianopignataro.it/a/castel-de-paolis-il-vino-vulcanico-alle-porte-di-roma/274731/

This is the counter-weight to W30(b) and it lands in the right decade: while the tendone was filling
supermarket shelves in the 1980s, one man was running a seven-year varietal recovery programme on the same
hills. Note the same Attilio Scienza turns up in the Marche research on Verdicchio — he is the recurring
figure of Italian ampelographic recovery.

### W32. Cesanese before the twentieth century — documented, and better than the papal name-drop
The old text has nothing before 1990. There is a real paper trail:

- **30 May 1479**: the **Statuti della Terra del Piglio** were issued, regulating every aspect of the
  community; «diversi capitoli degli Statuti riguardavano la **vite e il vino**».
- **Notarial documents in the capitular archive of Anagni** attest the continuity of viticulture after the
  fall of Rome.
- **1838**: the **Libro Maestro** of the **Abbey of Subiaco**, which owned most of the land around Piglio,
  records for the month of October: «vendemmia di Subiaco → **Cesanese**; vendemmia di Piglio → **Cesanese**».
  A named grape, in an estate ledger, with a year.
  https://www.visitanagni.com/enogastronomia-item.php?id=1232
  https://www.assovini.it/italia/lazio/item/1068-strada-del-vino-cesanese
  [secondary — these are quoted by the Strada del Vino and the Anagni tourist board, not read in the archives.
  The 1479 statutes and the 1838 ledger are specific enough to be checkable and are the best pre-modern
  material available.]
- The Piglio DOCG's own art. 9 corroborates the shape of it: the link to the territory runs from Roman times
  through «documented medieval statutes (1479) establishing viticultural regulations», to the 1973 DOC and
  the 2008 DOCG.
  https://www.disciplinare.it/cesanese-del-piglio-piglio-docg.html
- **Anagni, the popes**: four popes born there — **Innocent III, Gregory IX, Alexander IV, Boniface VIII** —
  and the town was a papal residence and the site of the "schiaffo di Anagni", Boniface VIII's arrest by
  Guillaume de Nogaret's party in **1303**.
  https://provincia.fr.it/contenuti/270456/anagni-citta-papi
  **But no source connects any pope to Cesanese by name.** The link is geography, not documented drinking.
  If the rewrite uses the popes, it must be as "the wine of a town that made four popes", not "the popes'
  wine" — that second claim is **UNVERIFIABLE**.

### W33. Goethe at Frascati — do not use it
A "Frascati is a paradise" quotation attributed to Goethe, dated 12 September 1787, circulates on tourism
pages. **I could not verify it and the evidence runs against it.** A dedicated study of wine in the *Italian
Journey* concludes that in Rome Goethe «non parla o quasi» about wine at all; its only substantial wine
anecdotes are from Trento (September 1786) and the road from Palermo to Monreale (18 April 1787), plus Rhine
wine on his birthday in Rome (22 September 1787). Goethe was demonstrably at Frascati in 1787 — the party
spent days there drawing under Reiffenstein's direction — but the wine quotation is **UNVERIFIABLE**.
https://storiedelvino.it/goethe-il-vino/
**Use Belli instead** (W25, W26): a Roman, in Roman, with dates, about the actual wine.
