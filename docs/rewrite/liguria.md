**Checked before drafting** (two research subagents, about sixty claims). Errors found in the old text:
- the 2011 flood did not kill nine: four died in Vernazza and Monterosso, thirteen in the whole storm (with the Val di
  Vara and Aulla); the quiz repeated "nine";
- "the steepest in Italy and among the steepest in the world" has no measured basis (the Calmont and the Douro are
  steeper by the usual accounts);
- terraces "between the eleventh and the nineteenth centuries": the park says from about the year 1000, UNESCO mostly
  the twelfth century; "nineteenth" has no source;
- Bosco does not "grow nowhere else": it is permitted in several eastern Ligurian IGTs and DOCs;
- the Republic of Genoa sending Sciacchetrà to popes: no source; Boccaccio's praise is documented, Petrarch's only in
  secondary sources; the name Sciacchetrà is attested only from the late nineteenth century;
- Byron's swim across the Gulf of Poets is a legend debunked in 1899 and 1922;
- Vermentino "came by sea from Spain" is disputed; the grape registers now give Italy;
- Ormeasco "at 700 metres": up to about 800, mostly 400–500; it has had its own DOC since 2003, so it is no longer a
  label variety of Riviera Ligure di Ponente, which names Moscato instead;
- Liguria "0.2% of Italy's wine": about 0.1% of the wine (0.2% is the vineyard area); "less than a single large estate
  in Puglia" unverifiable;
- Basilico Genovese "picked before the leaves take on the mint flavour that basil from anywhere else has": the DOP basil
  has no minty note and is pulled at 10–12 cm; it may be grown along the whole coast, not only at Pra';
- "three things protected by law … the bread Genoa eats": focaccia genovese has no protected status;
- "Riviera Ligure DOP … the mildest of Italy's great oils": no source;
- Genoa's stockfish "brought home from the north in the sixteenth century" / "from Norway and Iceland": Italian
  stockfish is Norwegian; the first documented commercial cargoes went to Venice in 1596 on Dutch, Danish and German
  ships from Bergen, and the fish reached Genoa in the sixteenth–seventeenth centuries; "more than any Italian city but
  Venice" unverifiable; the quiz repeated "Norway and Iceland";
- cima alla genovese has no "sweet-and-sour touch";
- Genoa's old town is not "the densest medieval old town in Europe" / "one of the largest": it is 113 ha; it is one of
  the most densely inhabited historic centres;
- the port is not simply "the largest in Italy" (Trieste leads on tonnage, Gioia Tauro on containers); it is Italy's
  busiest container gateway;
- the Biosfera is from 2001, not 1992;
- the San Lorenzo shell on display is a replica; the real one was dumped at sea in February 1941;
- the Bank of Saint George was not "one of the first banks in the world": it was the oldest state deposit bank when it
  closed in 1805; "invented much of modern banking" has no source;
- cappon magro as "a joke of a name": the name's origin is uncertain (Toso: *cappone*, the red scorpionfish);
- "cucina del ritorno" is used by one recent press article, not an established term.
