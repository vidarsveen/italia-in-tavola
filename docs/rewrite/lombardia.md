**Checked before drafting** (two research subagents, about sixty-five claims). Errors or overclaims found in the old
text:
- Franciacorta is not "the only place in Italy where an entire appellation is dedicated to" bottle-fermented sparkling
  wine: Trento DOC (1993) was the first such appellation, and Oltrepò Pavese Metodo Classico (DOCG 2007) and Alta Langa
  exist; Franciacorta was the first *bottle-fermented* wine to reach DOCG (1995). "The first Italian sparkling wine
  promoted to DOCG" is wrong as worded (Asti).
- The 1967 DOC also covered still wines; in 1995 only the sparkling wine became DOCG (still wines → Terre di
  Franciacorta, Curtefranca since 2008).
- Soils "over limestone" is not general; "the monks of Cluny were the first to drain and plant the land" overstates
  (prehistoric pips at Provaglio).
- "Rules stricter than most of Champagne's": true for non-vintage lees time (18 v 15 months), false for vintage (30 v 36).
- Release at 25/31/37/67 months is no longer stated in the disciplinare (a consequence of the tirage date).
- Satèn "five atmospheres instead of six": the rule is a maximum of five; six is not in the disciplinare.
- "Satèn … designed to be drunk with food": no source. "Mostly made by estates growing their own fruit": no reliable
  source.
- Valtellina "candidate for UNESCO recognition": not on Italy's current tentative list.
- "100 hours per hectare on the plain, over a thousand here": not found; a producer gives 1,200 against 300–400.
- Valtellina Superiore "12% alcohol minimum": not in the text; natural minimum 11% (11.5% with a sub-zone).
- "went north … to Switzerland and Austria, its main customers for centuries … since the nineteenth century": the trade
  goes back to the 1500s under Grisons rule, to the Grisons, Tyrol and southern Germany.
- "Maroggia the smallest, Valgella the largest" confirmed; "named after landmarks rather than villages" does not fit
  Valgella (dialect for small streams); Inferno's heat etymology is tradition.
- Lombardy's dairies "a third of Italy's milk": nearly half (46–47%).
- The Radetzky letter is a twentieth-century invention; the 1134 *lombolos cum panitio* is known only through Verri
  (1783) and its reading as breadcrumbs is contested.
- Risotto: the 1574 wedding is a legend with no located source; the 1809 recipe included cervellata and did not yet
  bear the name, which first appears in 1829.
- Panettone "documented in Pavia in 1599": the Collegio Borromeo record is for *pani grossi*, not yet named panettone.
- Torrone at the 1441 wedding: legend; first record 1543.
- "Milan invented the modern aperitivo": overclaim (Turin, Carpano 1786); Campari's bar in Milan 1867, the recipe
  settled in Novara 1860.
- Duomo "the only large Gothic cathedral in Italy": wrong; "at the state's expense": doubtful (the diocese says the
  façade drained the Fabbrica); "more statues than any other building in the world": tourist sites only.
- "a uf" from AUF: Treccani calls the etymology unknown; Florence tells the same story.
- Galleria "the model for every covered arcade and shopping mall": overclaim; "17 metres high": the dome is about 47 m
  high, about 37 m across.
- "Milan Fashion Week began in 1958": the Camera Nazionale della Moda was founded in Rome in 1958; the first official
  Milan week was 1975.
- The canals gave Milan "a port bigger than most on the coast": in 1953 the Darsena ranked third in Italy by tonnage
  (thirteenth by goods received).
- "Until the 1930s the city centre was ringed by water": covered 1929–30.

## 1. Franciacorta: Italy's answer to Champagne
- Summary → one sentence: a cloudy white its owner disliked became Italy's first bottle-fermented DOCG, on glacial hills
  where the harvest now starts in summer.
- Lead: Berlucchi's cloudy Pinot del Castello → Ziliani, 24, from Alba → the French method → failures → 1961, 3,000
  bottles, excellent a year later.
- The hills: moraine from the Valcamonica glacier → soils → lake and Alps → the name, 1277, *francae curtes*, Guerrini
  and Cluny → Provaglio, Lamosa and the marshes → prehistoric pips.
- After 1961: Ca' del Bosco, Bellavista → DOC 1967 still and sparkling → consortium 1990 → DOCG 1995 sparkling only →
  Curtefranca → size and market.
- Satèn: crémant banned 1989 → a new name, trademark 1995, coinage disputed → less sugar at tirage → less gas → five
  atmospheres → softer bubbles.
- Warmer than Champagne: ripe fruit → softer acidity → less dosage; lees ageing; disgorgement date.
- Ending: harvest in August, 2025 → Erbamat 2017 against the heat → 30 July 2026, first ever in July, at Gussago (the hero).
- Box: rules with Champagne's lees minimums beside them; label; Lombardy's other wines (Trento as first, Oltrepò Pinot
  Nero and Metodo Classico, Bonarda, Lugana, moved from reading 3).
- Ledger: all old facts kept except as noted. **Changed, sourced**: "the only place in Italy …" → first bottle-fermented
  DOCG, Trento named first appellation; "the first Italian sparkling wine to be promoted to DOCG" → first made by
  fermentation in the bottle; "the DOC came in 1967" → with still wines, sparkling alone at DOCG, Curtefranca; "some
  3,000 hectares and … twenty million bottles, most of them in Italy" → 3,400 ha, nearly nineteen million, nine in ten
  in Italy; "gravel, sand and stones over limestone, poor in nutrients and quick to drain, which forces the vines to work
  and keeps yields low" → moraine soils fairly deep and well drained (limestone, poverty and low yields **cut**, not
  general or not sourced); "the monks of Cluny were the first to drain and plant" → Cluniac priory, draining the marshes,
  vines older; "the young oenologist … persuaded his employer to try" → the fuller sourced story; "Ca' del Bosco, founded
  in 1968 and led by Maurizio Zanella" → house 1964, vines 1968, Maurizio; "rules stricter than most of Champagne's" →
  both minimums in the box; "Release is allowed 25, 31, 37 and 67 months after harvest" → **cut** (no longer in the
  rules); "five atmospheres instead of six" → at most five; "Satèn … bottled at lower pressure … gives its softer,
  creamier mousse" → the sugar-to-pressure mechanism; "the harvest starts in August" → 2025 and 2026; "by law it does not
  need the word spumante" → the word is not allowed; "Riserva … five years on the lees" kept. **New, sourced**: Ziliani's
  age and school, Palazzo Lana, Pinot del Castello, the failed years; Guerrini, 1277, Lamosa; Curtefranca; Crémant 1989;
  Erbamat's reason and Gallo; the July harvest; Champagne's minimums. **Cut**: "Many of the best cuvées are dosaggio zero"
  → "many producers make it"; "Where Champagne is made mostly by big houses … which explains the single-vineyard and
  single-vintage bottlings that have become the region's signature" (no reliable source for either half); "designed to
  be drunk with food rather than as an aperitif" (no source); "the style most people in Brescia order without thinking"
  (quip); "puts it in the company of prestige Champagne" (verdict); "almost everything worth drinking is in the first
  three" (verdict); the price paragraph (buying advice; "years in a cellar rather than weeks in a tank" survives as the
  lees minimums); "Satèn is Franciacorta's own invention" → the naming story.

## 2. Nebbiolo in the Alps: Valtellina

- The trade: Grisons families own vineyards → 1797 auction → peak mid-19th century, names on bottles → Gotthard 1882 →
  750 ha today, under a quarter of Barolo → Grüner Veltliner unrelated.
- The wall: the valley's direction → the south-facing side → walls → heat, drainage, rain shadow → hand work,
  helicopter → hours → heroic viticulture.
- Chiavennasca: name → late ripening → Sondrio. Sub-zones: Sassella, Grumello, Inferno in prose (Maroggia, Valgella and

  Negri and Maule.
- The table: pizzoccheri, sciatt, taragna, bresaola → Bitto (moved from reading 3's cheese paragraph) → the rules → the
  rebels. Ending: Storico Ribelle.
- Box: DOCG rules and Stagafassli; sub-zones with sizes; Sforzato and Rosso; walls and UNESCO corrected; producers.
- Ledger: all old facts kept except as noted. **Changed, sourced**: "one of the few places in the world where a great
