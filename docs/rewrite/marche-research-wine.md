# Marche wine readings: fact check and story leads (research only, no prose)

Checked 2026-09-15. Scope: `content/marche.js` lessons 1 ("Verdicchio: a white that ages like a red") and
2 ("Montepulciano on the Conero").

Verdicts: **confirmed / corrected / overstated / legend / not verifiable**.
- [producer] marks a producer's own account of its own history.
- [snippet] marks a fact I saw only in a search-engine summary, because the page itself refused the fetch (403) or was not opened. Treat [snippet] items as leads, not as verified.

Primary texts used:
- The disciplinari (production rules) as published by Regione Marche (static.regione.marche.it, DM_2011_24457 set) and IMT (imtdoc.it).
- Art. 9 of each disciplinare ("Legame con l'ambiente") carries the consortium's own history of the zone. It is official, but it is not always accurate: see W8 and W15c.

## A. Reading 1, "Verdicchio: a white that ages like a red"

### W1. "grows almost nowhere except the Marche"; relation to Soave / Lugana; name; earliest mention
- **"almost nowhere except the Marche": corrected.**
  - DNA work shows Verdicchio and **Trebbiano di Soave are the same variety**, so the grape is also planted in the Veneto under that name.
  - Wikipedia also lists Verdicchio plantings in Umbria and Lazio, which was not checked further.
- **Identity with Trebbiano di Soave: confirmed.**
  - Calò A., Costacurta A., Cancellier S., Forti R. (1991), "Verdicchio bianco, Trebbiano di Soave: un unico vitigno", was the first to show identity.
  - Labra M. et al. (2001, *Vitis* 40): «The only samples that were proven to be genetically identical were Trebbiano di Soave and Marche's Verdicchio», and both were «very close matches with Trebbiano di Lugana».
  - https://italianwinecentral.com/trebbiano_family/ ; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebbiano_di_Soave
- **Identity with Turbiana (Lugana): disputed, so the reading should not call them one grape.**
  - Villa, Failla, Labra, Scienza (2001), "Il Trebbiano di Lugana: aspetti storici e genetici", call Turbiana and Verdicchio close relatives but **genetically distinct**. Attilio Scienza puts the band overlap at 97% with both Trebbiano di Soave and Verdicchio.
  - The Lugana disciplinare adopted the name "Turbiana" in 2011, and three Turbiana clones were registered in 2021.
  - https://cadeifrati.it/il-professor-attilio-scienza-in-ca-dei-frati/ ; https://www.consorziolugana.it/news/registrati-tre-nuovi-cloni-di-turbiana
  - Wine Grapes (Robinson/Harding/Vouillamoz 2012) reportedly treats all three as one variety. I saw that only in a search summary, not the book itself.
- **"the clones are near-identical, which is why Soave and Verdicchio can taste like cousins": overstated.**
  - "Clones" is the wrong word: the finding is about the variety, not about clones.
  - Scienza suggests the long separation of the populations explains the marked aromatic differences between Soave and Verdicchio despite genetic identity. https://www.bibenda.it/news_bibenda_singola.php?id=5577 [snippet]
- **Name from the green tinge: confirmed** as the conventional etymology (Latin *viridis*, Italian *verde*). No documentary source for the naming exists.
- **Earliest mentions: corrected / dated.** Three candidates, in order:
  1. **1569**, in the botanist **Costanzo Felici** of Piobbico (1525–1585), in his long letter on salads to Ulisse Aldrovandi (*Del'insalata*, written 1565–72). He lists verdicchio among **table** grapes and distinguishes it from trebbiano and malvasia.
     - Source: Password Magazine, drawing on Francesco Annibali, *Il Verdicchio fra Jesi e Matelica* (Aliberti). https://www.leggopassword.it/curiosando-tra-storie-e-leggende-legate-al-verdicchio/ ; on Felici: https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/costanzo-felici_(Dizionario-Biografico)/
  2. **Mid-16th century**, **Mambrino Roseo** of Fabriano (c.1500–1580) translated Gabriel Alonso de Herrera's Spanish *Agricultura general* and rendered the Spanish "torrontés" as "verdicchio" (same Password source).
     - Both Jesi disciplinari (DOC and Riserva DOCG, art. 9) say "già ai primi del 1500 lo spagnolo Herrera … descrive … il Verdicchio" and quote the Italian text. That quote is Roseo's translation, not Herrera's original. Correct the claim if it is used.
  3. **12 January 1579**, a notarial contract drawn up at Matelica (notary Niccolò Attucci, Archivio di Stato di Macerata). The tenant undertakes to plant «Verdicchio et Brungentile».
     - Stated in the Verdicchio di Matelica DOC disciplinare, art. 9: «Proprio in questo periodo (12 gennaio 1579) un contratto notarile, in quel di Matelica, cita la parola "Verdicchio"».
     - Notary's name and the quote come from https://decanto.wine/unicita-verdicchio-di-matelica/ and a search summary of the Gambero Rosso Matelica guide.
  - The claim that the grape has been in the Marche "since the 14th century" (en.wikipedia) is **unsourced**.
- **Origin hypothesis (not established):** brought by Lombard settlers invited into the Jesi countryside around 1470 («poco oltre la metà del Quattrocento»). https://www.assoenologi.it/enologonlinerubriche/verdicchio/ and Password Magazine.
- **Alaric legend: legend (actually a forgery).**
  - The story has forty pack animals loaded with barrels of Verdicchio for the Visigoths' march on Rome in 409–410.
  - It is attributed to Vincenzo Maria Cimarelli, *Istorie dello Stato d'Urbino* (1642), Book III ch. 2.
  - Checked against the original, the Verdicchio passage turned out to be an invented insertion. It was nonetheless repeated by Luigi Veronelli and others.
  - A similar tale about Hannibal treating his horses with Verdicchio rests on *vina praetutiana* (wines of the Teramo district), not Verdicchio. (Password Magazine)
  - decanto.wine repeats Alaric as "potentially romanticised", with the date 408.

### W2. Castelli di Jesi zone
- **"named after the castle-villages that crown them": confirmed.**
  - DOC disciplinare art. 9: the zone lies in the Esino basin, «nei territori di 22 Comuni della Provincia di Ancona e 2 di Macerata storicamente Castelli perché gravitanti nella politica e nell'economia di Jesi che nel 1194 ha dato i natali a Federico II di Svevia».
- **Cupramontana, Montecarotto, Staffolo, Monte Roberto: confirmed** inside the zone. Staffolo and Monteroberto are named in the art. 3 boundary. Cupramontana and Montecarotto are the seats of Colonnara and Moncaro.
- **"It runs down almost to the sea": overstated.**
  - Art. 9: «L'area dista circa Km 20 dal mare e si sviluppa nelle colline poste attorno alla valle Esina che ha in Jesi una quota di mt 96 s.l.m. fino ai 630 mt di Cingoli».
  - The northern boundary does reach into Ostra and the Senigallia–Morro d'Alba municipal border, but the rules themselves describe the zone as about 20 km inland.
  - Quattrocalici gives vineyard altitudes of about 80–500 m.
- **Classico: confirmed, but it is most of the zone, not a small core.**
  - Art. 3: «"Classico" è riservata al vino ottenuto dalle uve raccolte nella zona originaria più antica. Tale zona è costituita da quella delimitata … con l'esclusione dei territori posti alla sinistra del Fiume Misa e dei territori appartenenti ai comuni di Ostra e di Senigallia».
- **"two separate zones about forty kilometres apart": overstated.** These are my own great-circle calculations from town coordinates:
  - Jesi–Matelica 35 km, Cupramontana–Matelica 23 km.
  - The Jesi zone's western edge follows the Fabriano / Serra San Quirico border, and the Matelica zone includes part of Fabriano and Cerreto d'Esi. So the two boundaries lie roughly 10–15 km apart (Serra San Quirico–Cerreto d'Esi 14 km).
  - Safer wording: "two zones, the smaller one some 25–35 km further inland".
- **Size:** Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC produced 119,000 hl in 2023 on about 1,500–1,600 ha (ISTAT/AGEA/ISMEA via https://www.inumeridelvino.it/2026/01/marche-produzione-di-vino-2024-dettaglio-per-denominazione-doc-2023-fonte-istat-agea-ismea.html).
  - Other figures in circulation: 2,500 ha with "over 1,000 companies" and 2,762 ha (AIS Lombardia [snippet]).
  - Use "about 2,000 ha" or cite the year.

### W3. Matelica
- **Valley runs north to south: confirmed.**
  - Matelica DOC disciplinare art. 9: «La zona è attraversata dal fiume Esino nella fase iniziale del suo percorso che scorre parallelo verso nord alla zona montuosa appenninica ed alla costa adriatica».
  - Geologically the valley is the «Sinclinale Camerte» (Camerino syncline), which begins at Fabriano.
- **River Esino: confirmed** (its upper course; the Jesi zone is on its middle course).
- **"much higher": confirmed.**
  - Art. 9: «altimetricamente compresa tra 250 mt. s.l.m. fino a 700 mt. s.l.m. con una percentuale di presenza dell'80% tra mt 280 e mt 480», average about 350 m, one vineyard found at 720 m.
  - Compare Jesi at 96 m and the Castelli zone at roughly 80–500 m.
  - Vendemmie gives 350–600 m.
- **"no sea breeze at all": confirmed as the rules' own claim.**
  - Art. 9: «decisamente interna e lontana dall'ambiente e dall'influenza marina … L'assenza sul clima di qualsiasi influenza marina … fa registrare periodi invernali con freddi intensi e mesi estivi con temperature elevate».
  - decanto.wine attributes the shelter to Monte San Vicino on the east side.
- **"small": confirmed.** No reliable hectare figure found.
  - 2024 production was about 20,000 hl (inumeridelvino, same page as W2).
  - "Ten times smaller than Jesi" (≈270–300 ha) is a derived figure [snippet].
- **Communes: confirmed** (disciplinare art. 3): parts of Matelica, Esanatoglia, Gagliole, Castelraimondo, Camerino, Pioraco (MC) and Cerreto d'Esi, Fabriano (AN). Eight communes in all.
- **NEW, affects the text: the appellation has been renamed "Matelica" DOC.**
  - "Verdicchio" becomes optional and may appear only last on the label, and bottling must now take place in the zone.
  - Dates conflict:
    - Vendemmie: process began 2018, published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale 16 May 2025.
    - MASAF lists the Matelica disciplinare in GURI n. 264 of 13.11.2025, sent to the European Commission on 27.01.2026.
  - Check the status before print. https://vendemmie.com/news/da-verdicchio-di-matelica-a-matelica-doc-il-bianco-marchigiano-sceglie-il-territorio/ ; https://www.masaf.gov.it/flex/cm/pages/ServeBLOB.php/L/IT/IDPagina/22762

### W4. Rules and dates
- **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC: confirmed 1968** (DPR 11.08.1968, G.U. 245 of 26.09.1968).
- **Verdicchio di Matelica DOC: confirmed 1967** (DPR 21.07.1967, G.U. 211 of 23.08.1967).
- **Both Riserva DOCGs: confirmed 2010.**
  - Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva: DM 18.02.2010, G.U. 50 of 02.03.2010.
  - Verdicchio di Matelica Riserva: DM 18.02.2010, G.U. 49 of 01.03.2010.
  - Both had existed as Riserva types of the DOCs since DM 12/13.09.1995.
  - Assoenologi's "2009" is wrong.
- **Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva DOCG: confirmed.**
  - Verdicchio at least 85%, other white grapes up to 15%.
  - Minimum 12.5% total alcohol (12% natural).
  - At least 18 months' ageing, of which at least 6 in bottle, counted from 1 December of the vintage (Quattrocalici summary of the disciplinare).
  - A Classico Riserva also exists.
- **Matelica Riserva DOCG:** 85% Verdicchio and 12.5% alcohol confirmed (art. 2, art. 6). Ageing was not extracted in this pass.
- **DOC minimum 11.5%: confirmed.** Art. 6 of the Jesi DOC gives:
  - base and Classico 11.5%
  - **Classico Superiore 12%**
  - **Passito** 15% total (12% actual), not released before 1 December of the year after harvest
  - **Spumante** 11.5%, extra brut to dry
- Official taste description in art. 6: «retrogusto gradevolmente amarognolo» (see W6).

### W5. The Fazi Battaglia amphora
- **"In the middle of the last century": confirmed, now dated.**
  - Fazi Battaglia was formed **1949** «dall'unione delle famiglie Fazi e Battaglia», beginning at Cupramontana.
  - **Francesco Angelini** is described as co-founder. The Jesi disciplinare calls the later owner «un altro industriale farmaceutico».
  - In **1953** the firm set up a **design competition**, won by the **architect Antonio Maiocchi**.
  - **Patent registered 1953.**
  - Sources: https://winenews.it/it/titulus-un-simbolo-del-mondo-vino-in-mostra-la-bottiglia_303091/ ; https://www.beverfood.com/bottiglia-anfora-verdicchio-fazi-battaglia-entra-museo-ara-pacis-roma-wd2984/ (press release 23.11.2009) ; https://fazibattaglia.com/en/company [producer]
- **"a little scroll of paper round the neck": confirmed.** «il prezioso cartiglio attaccato al collo» (WineNews, Beverfood).
- **Inspiration: two versions, and they conflict.**
  - The firm says «ispirata agli antichi contenitori etruschi».
  - The Jesi disciplinare art. 9 says «l'anfora greca in riferimento alla civiltà dorica che fondò la città di Ancona», adding that Verdicchio gained commercial fame «all'inizio degli anni '50» when two producers built a cellar in one of the castelli.
- **Nickname:** "the Sofia Loren" in America [producer].
- **Exhibited** in "Disegno e Design – Brevetti e creatività italiani", Museo dell'Ara Pacis, Rome, 4 Nov 2009 – 31 Jan 2010, as one of the Italian patents that founded a lasting design line.
- **"the amphora survives mainly as nostalgia": corrected.**
  - The amphora (now "Titulus") is still sold worldwide: «oltre 3 m.ni di bottiglie prodotte», turnover about €13 m, figures for 2008 (Beverfood 2009).
  - The Angelini family has returned to the company [producer].
  - No figure for peak historical sales was found.
- **"By the 1980s the amphora was shorthand for trattoria carafe wine": not verifiable as worded.**
  - What is sourced is a quality crisis in the **1970s**: «Sono in molti, lungo gli anni Settanta, a recitare il requiem per il Verdicchio».
  - The 1980s are described as the recovery: «Gli anni Ottanta premiano l'impegno …» (Assoenologi, above).
  - The disciplinare (art. 9) blames the separate sharecropper vinifications for poor quality, not the bottle.
- **"spent thirty years in a novelty bottle" (summary): overstated**, since the bottle never stopped.
- **"Most serious producers now use an ordinary Burgundy bottle": not verifiable.** One source says quality producers moved to "standard **Bordeaux** bottles" in the 1980s (TerroirSense [snippet]).

### W6. Taste, ageing and producers
- **Bitter almond finish: confirmed in substance.** Both disciplinari describe the finish as bitterish («retrogusto gradevolmente amarognolo», Jesi; «leggermente amarognolo», Matelica Riserva). "Almond" is a taster's word.
- **Ageing: confirmed in general.**
  - Ian D'Agata (TerroirSense, 15 May 2024) calls twenty-year-old Verdicchio «amongst Italy's oldest lived white wines». https://terroirsense.com/en/p/10540.html
  - No single documented very old bottle tasted at great age was found. Garofoli sells library vintages and runs verticals (Civiltà del bere, Roger Sesto, 17 Nov 2010: Podium created 1991, best year 1997). https://www.civiltadelbere.com/garofoli-vendesi-vecchie-annate/
- **Bucci: "release wines at four or five years": overstated.**
  - Villa Bucci Riserva spends 12–18 months in 50 and 75 hl Slavonian oak plus at least a year in bottle, so it is released at about three years (Empson, importer [snippet]).
  - Ampelio Bucci:
    - born **1936 in Montecarotto**, studied economics at Bocconi, worked in fashion and design, vice-president of Domus Academy
    - **died August 2025, aged 89** (ANSA, 21 Aug 2025)
    - estate **sold July 2024** to the Veronesi family (Oniverse, ex-Calzedonia)
    - https://www.ansa.it/canale_terraegusto/notizie/vino/2025/08/21/vino-muore-ampelio-bucci-pioniere-verdicchio-castelli-jesi_f01ea5ed-c49d-4f2c-8d6c-e6ee39acba84.html
  - First Verdicchio 1982, Villa Bucci founded 1983 at Ostra Vetere, style built with the enologist **Giorgio Grai** (Sky TG24 / Kerin O'Keefe [snippet]).
  - Barrels described as very old and "exhausted"; Bucci on vines: «occorrono almeno sette anni prima che una vigna inizi a "saper fare", e quasi quindici prima che "faccia bene"». https://www.identitagolose.it/sito/it/136/32922/in-cantina/ampelio-bucci-il-suo-pensiero-sulle-botti-eterne-del-verdicchio-villa-bucci.html
  - The 2019 Villa Bucci ranked no. 2 in Wine Enthusiast's Top 100 (same source).
  - "the most famous example" is opinion. "expect them to be drunk at fifteen" is not verifiable.
- **Garofoli [producer]:**
  - 1871, Antonio Garofoli sold wine from a tavern on the pilgrim road to Loreto.
  - 1901, his son Gioacchino registered the firm in Loreto.
  - Claims «the first» metodo classico in the Marche «in the 1970s».
  - https://garofolivini.it/en/hystory/
  - Search summaries give 1974 (with Montepulciano and Pinot Nero in the blend) and Serra Fiorese (released 1984) as the first Verdicchio aged two years in wood and bottle [snippet].
  - Caution: Assoenologi says **Ubaldo Rosi** was already making sparkling Verdicchio in the 1800s, so do not write "first sparkling Verdicchio".
- **Others:**
  - Umani Ronchi: founded 1957 in Cupramontana by Gino Umani Ronchi; Roberto Bianchi and Massimo Bernetti joined 1959 [producer/snippet].
  - Sartarelli: 1972, Poggio San Marcello [snippet].
  - La Monacesca: 1966, Casimiro Cifola; first bottling under the name 1973 [producer]. https://www.monacesca.it/azienda/
  - Bisci: 1972, Giuseppe Bisci [producer].
  - Moroder (lesson 2): not checked.
- **"Almost none of them is expensive": not verifiable.**

### W7. Pecorino, Passerina, Offida, Falerio
- **"rescued from near-extinction in the 1980s": confirmed.** Two accounts disagree on details.
  - **Consorzio Vini Piceni**:
    - The sommelier **Teodoro Bugari** told **Guido Cocci Grifoni** of an old vineyard.
    - «one morning in September … I and others went to Arquata del Tronto, in the hamlet of Pescara», where a Mr **Cafini** grew Pecorino.
    - Grafted **February 1983**, first wine «just two years later».
    - https://www.vinopecorino.com/en/the-history-of-pecorino-grapewine/
  - **Tenuta Cocci Grifoni [producer]**:
    - learned of it in 1982
    - trials on soils 1984–90
    - first mother vineyard 1987
    - market debut **1990 with 1,800 bottles**
    - https://www.tenutacoccigrifoni.it/en/a-family-story/
  - **it.wikipedia**: vines survived phylloxera in old vineyards at Pescara del Tronto, reintroduced from 1984, first pure Pecorino 1990. https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_(vitigno)
  - Resolve the dates with the Tenuta if the story is used.
- **"planted well beyond the region": confirmed.**
  - Grown in the Marche, Abruzzo, and to a lesser extent Lazio and Umbria. 1,114 ha by 2010 (it.wikipedia, citing the Registro Nazionale).
- **Passerina "made in every style from still to sparkling to passito": not verified in this pass.**
- **Offida DOCG: confirmed.**
  - DOC DM 23.05.2001; DOCG DM 15.06.2011 (G.U. 149 of 29.06.2011), from the 2011 vintage.
  - Types: Offida Pecorino and Offida Passerina (each at least 85%), and Offida Rosso (Montepulciano at least 85%).
  - https://www.disciplinare.it/offida-docg.html
- **Falerio DOC: confirmed, but it is a blend.**
  - DPR 28.04.1975 (G.U. 226 of 26.08.1975).
  - Trebbiano Toscano 20–50%, Passerina 10–30%, Pecorino 10–30%; Passerina and Pecorino were written into the rules in 1997.
  - https://www.agraria.org/vini/falerio-doc.htm
- **"the argument between Jesi and Matelica … for a century": not verifiable.**

## B. Reading 2, "Montepulciano on the Conero"

### W8. Monte Conero
- **572 m: confirmed.** Conero DOCG disciplinare art. 9: «dallo stesso Conero, mt 572 s.l.m.».
- **"For fifteen kilometres south of Ancona": corrected to about 20 km.**
  - The Riviera del Conero runs from the port of Ancona to Numana with «uno sviluppo costiero di circa venti chilometri … di costa alta, frastagliata e rocciosa». https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riviera_del_Conero
- **White limestone cliffs: confirmed.** Rules: «basse pendici bianche (calcaree)»; Wikipedia: «falesie calcaree bianche».
  - Treccani notes the promontory makes the "elbow" of the Italian Adriatic coast: «estrema propaggine orientale del fascio di pieghe appenniniche centrali».
- **"unusually clear water": not verifiable as a general claim.** The mussel beds lie in class A (cleanest) shellfish waters (W15h).
- **Nature reserve: confirmed, with its proper name.**
  - **Parco regionale del Conero**, founded by L.R. Marche **23 April 1987, n. 21**, nearly 6,000 ha across Ancona, Camerano, Sirolo and Numana. https://edizionieuropee.it/law/html/128/ma5_04_009.html
  - A bill to make it a national park is before the Chamber of Deputies (A.C. 1798 and 2246, 19th legislature). https://documenti.camera.it/leg19/dossier/pdf/Am0082.pdf
- **"the only high ground on this stretch of coast": confirmed as commonly stated.**
  - Usual wording: «l'unico promontorio roccioso della costa adriatica da Trieste al Gargano».
  - Only tourist and Wikipedia sources say this. I did not check it against other cliffs, such as San Bartolo near Pesaro.
- **Name from Greek *kómaros*, strawberry tree: confirmed as the conventional etymology** (park and tourism sources; no philological source consulted).
- **Ancona's Greek foundation: confirmed with a caveat.**
  - Treccani: «fondata da Dionisio di Siracusa circa il 390», or «da esiliati siracusani per sottrarsi alla tirannia di Dionisio, secondo Strabone (V, 4, 2)». Juvenal's «dorica Ancon».
  - The usual date is 387 BC (it.wikipedia). https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ancona_(Enciclopedia-Italiana)/
- **Greeks bringing vines: not verifiable.**
  - The Conero disciplinare art. 9 claims «i coloni greci 10 secoli a.C. hanno lasciato tracce sicure di viticoltura». That is impossible for a 4th-century-BC colony; do not use it.
  - The same article's Pliny quote is wrong (see W15f).
  - What is sound: **about 200 *Vitis vinifera* pips in a 7th-century-BC Picene princely tomb at Matelica** (Matelica and Conero disciplinari, art. 9).

### W9. Rosso Conero and Conero DOCG
- **Rosso Conero DOC 1967: confirmed** (DPR 21.07.1967, G.U. 210 of 22.08.1967).
- **Conero DOCG 2004: confirmed** (DM 01.09.2004, G.U. 212 of 09.09.2004).
- **Rules: confirmed.**
  - Montepulciano at least 85%, Sangiovese at most 15%.
  - Minimum 12.5% total alcohol (12% natural).
  - **At least 2 years' ageing from 1 November of the vintage.** The reading gives no ageing figure; worth adding.
  - The word «Riserva» is mandatory on the label.
  - Bordeaux-type dark glass bottles only.
  - Yield 9 t/ha.
- **Communes: confirmed exactly.** All of Ancona, Offagna, Camerano, Sirolo, Numana, and part of Castelfidardo and Osimo. Wine may also be made at Barcaglione and Guastuglia (Falconara Marittima).
- **"on the mountain itself": overstated.**
  - Art. 9: «L'altimetria dell'area investita a vigneto dà una percentuale del 70% tra mt 20 e mt 140 s.l.m. con una presenza dell'1% a mt 550».
  - Dominant exposure is south-west (80%); average rainfall 775 mm.
  - The vineyards are mostly on the low hills behind the mountain.
  - Soils are mixed: limestone near the coast, «affioramenti pelitico-arenacei e marnosi di età mio-pliocenica» on the hills. "On limestone" is a simplification.
- Source: IMT PDF of the disciplinare, https://imtdoc.it/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/disciplinare-conero-riserva.pdf

### W10. Rosso Piceno
- **DOC 1968: confirmed** (DPR 11.08.1968, G.U. 245 of 26.09.1968).
- **Blend: confirmed and quantified.**
  - Montepulciano 35–85%, Sangiovese 15–50%, other non-aromatic reds up to 15%.
  - There is also a Sangiovese type (at least 85%).
- **"a much larger zone running down the southern half of the region": corrected.**
  - Art. 3 runs along the coast «dal confine provinciale Pesaro – Ancona fino a Porto d'Ascoli» and inland across the provinces of Ancona (minus the Rosso Conero zone), Macerata, Fermo and Ascoli Piceno.
  - That is most of the region south of Pesaro-Urbino, not only its southern half.
- **Superiore "around the villages behind San Benedetto del Tronto": confirmed.**
  - Art. 3 delimits the coast from Grottammare to Porto d'Ascoli and inland to include Appignano, Offida, Cossignano and Ripatransone.
  - A summary lists 13 communes in Ascoli Piceno province:
    - wholly: Acquaviva Picena, Appignano del Tronto, Castorano, Offida
    - partly: Ascoli Piceno, Castel di Lama, Colli del Tronto, Grottammare, Monsampolo del Tronto, Monteprandone, Ripatransone, San Benedetto del Tronto, Spinetoli
  - Superiore rules: minimum 12%, one year of ageing [snippet for the list and the Superiore rules].
- **Scale:** about 38,000 hl on about 800 ha in 2023 (inumeridelvino).
- Source: Regione Marche PDF "DOC Rosso Piceno o Piceno"; https://www.disciplinare.it/rosso-piceno-o-piceno-doc.html

### W11. Lacrima di Morro d'Alba
- **DOC 1985: confirmed** (DPR 09.01.1985, G.U. 171 of 22.07.1985; last changed DM 6.8.2021, G.U. 24.8.2021).
- **Lacrima at least 85%: confirmed.** Types: base, **Superiore**, **Passito**.
- **Six communes: confirmed.**
  - Morro d'Alba, Monte San Vito, San Marcello, Belvedere Ostrense, Ostra, Senigallia, all in Ancona province.
  - Excluded: valley floors, and the Senigallia hill slopes facing the sea (art. 3).
- **No relation to Lacryma Christi: confirmed.**
  - Art. 9: the 1879 *Ampelografia italiana* description matches today's Marche Lacrima «ma non concorda con quella che era stata precedentemente inserita dal Gallesio nella Pomona italiana, né corrisponde ad altri vitigni che portano lo stesso nome e che sono diffusi nelle aree meridionali».
- **No relation to Alba in Piedmont: confirmed.** The commune has been called «Morro d'Alba» since 1862, after its ancient Roman name (art. 9).
- **Name from weeping berries: probable, not documented.**
  - Ripe berries split and «lacrimare, perdendo succo» (Stefano Mancinelli in winemag.it). Alternatives: the elongated berry shape (it.wikipedia).
- **"After the Second World War it was down to fewer than ten hectares": corrected (misdated).**
  - The disciplinare says only that the grape shrank «durante la ricostituzione viticola del secondo dopoguerra».
  - The "fewer than 10 ha" figure belongs to **the time of the 1985 DOC** («meno di 10 ettari di vigneti residui»; 7 ha in 1985, citing Gambero Rosso). https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrima_(vitigno)
- **Today:** 207 ha and 1.7 million bottles in 2020 (winemag.it, 2021); **256 ha in 2025** (it.wikipedia citing Gambero Rosso); 11,000 hl in 2024 (inumeridelvino).
  - The consortium is pursuing a **Riserva** type. https://www.gamberorosso.it/notizie/settimanale/notizie-trebicchieri/ldoc-lacrima-morro-alba-versione-riserva/
- **Who kept it going:**
  - **Stefano Mancinelli**, back from agricultural studies in Florence in 1985, persuaded his father to bottle. The family grew from 7 ha to 25 ha [producer interview]. https://www.winemag.it/stefano-mancinelli-lacrima-di-morro-d-alba/
  - For about a decade after the DOC, local growers still ranked the grape below Verdicchio, Montepulciano and Sangiovese (same source).
  - "First three historic producers Mancinelli, Lucchetti, Vicari" [snippet].
- **Barbarossa at Morro d'Alba during the 1167 siege of Ancona: legend.** Only «secondo la tradizione» (it.wikipedia); no documentary source found.

### W12. Vernaccia di Serrapetrona
- **DOCG 2004: confirmed** (DOC DPR 22.07.1971; DOCG DM 18.08.2004, G.U. 205 of 01.09.2004). Spumante only, «secco» and «dolce».
- **"a red sparkling wine made partly from dried grapes": confirmed, and quantified.**
  - Vernaccia nera at least 85%.
  - «non più del 60% delle uve … deve essere vinificato all'atto della vendemmia; il rimanente, non meno del 40% delle uve, costituito per la totalità da quelle provenienti dal vitigno Vernaccia nera … deve essere sottoposto ad appassimento» to reach at least 13% natural alcohol.
  - The dried-grape must is added to the fresh wine or fermented separately and then blended. The blend is made sparkling «mediante fermentazione naturale».
  - Not released before 30 June of the year after harvest.
  - Zone: all of Serrapetrona, part of Belforte del Chienti and San Severino Marche; vineyards up to 700 m. (IMT PDF of the disciplinare)
- **Three fermentations: confirmed** as the standard description (harvest, dried grapes in about January, then autoclave). https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/vernaccia-di-serrapetrona-docg/ ; https://www.assoenologi.it/enologonlinerubriche/la-vernaccia-di-serrapetrona-docg/
- **"unlike anything else in Italy": overstated.**
  - Local sources call it «l'unico vino rosso spumante DOCG» or even the only red sparkling wine in Italy. The second is plainly false (Lambrusco), and the first should be checked against Brachetto d'Acqui DOCG.
  - What holds up: a red sparkling wine made with dried grapes and a second fermentation is distinctive.
- **Hectares: not verifiable.** About 45 ha [snippet, unattributed]; Quacquarini, the largest producer, 35 ha [snippet].

## C. W15. Sourced stories (WANTED)

Ranked by strength of sourcing.

**a. The notary at Matelica, 12 January 1579 (Verdicchio reading).**
- A contract drawn up by the Matelica notary Niccolò Attucci obliges a tenant to plant «tutti buoni vitiami, et per di più Verdicchio et Brungentile».
- Earliest known document with the name tied to a place. Kept at the Archivio di Stato di Macerata. Cited in the official Matelica disciplinare (art. 9).
- It also shows mezzadria-style obligations on the tenant, which links to W13.
- Pair with Costanzo Felici's 1569 list, where verdicchio is a **table** grape.
- Sources: disciplinare (W3); https://decanto.wine/unicita-verdicchio-di-matelica/ ; Password Magazine (W1).

**b. The amphora competition, 1953 (Verdicchio reading).**
- Fazi Battaglia (1949, Cupramontana) set up a competition for a bottle that would make an unknown grape recognisable; architect Antonio Maiocchi won.
- Details: patented 1953, the paper cartouche at the neck, "the Sofia Loren" in the US, shown at the Ara Pacis in 2009–10, still over 3 million bottles a year in 2008.
- Two founding myths for the shape: Etruscan (the firm) or Doric-Greek after Ancona (the disciplinare).
- Sources: W5.

**c. The forged Alaric and the smuggled translation (Verdicchio reading, as a correction).**
- A 1642 local history was later found to contain an invented passage sending forty mules of Verdicchio with the Visigoths, and Veronelli repeated it.
- The genuine early trace is a Fabriano translator, Mambrino Roseo, putting "verdicchio" in place of the Spanish "torrontés" in his Italian Herrera.
- Both Jesi disciplinari still quote that translation as Herrera's own words.
- Source quality is medium (a magazine summarising Annibali's book). Check the book before using: Francesco Annibali, *Il Verdicchio fra Jesi e Matelica* (Aliberti).

**d. The Pecorino vineyard at Pescara del Tronto (either reading, or the Offida paragraph).**
- A September morning before harvest; the sommelier Teodoro Bugari; Mr Cafini's old vineyard in the hamlet of Pescara, Arquata del Tronto.
- Guido Cocci Grifoni grafts the cuttings in February 1983. First commercial bottles 1990, 1,800 of them.
- Dates conflict between the consortium and the estate (W7).
- Lead, not verified here: Pescara del Tronto was destroyed in the earthquake of 24 August 2016. Check before use.

**e. Lacrima at the Ancona ampelographic exhibition, September 1872 (Conero reading).**
- The 1873 volume *La esposizione ampelografica marchigiana-abruzzese tenuta in Ancona il settembre 1872 e studi sulla vite e sul vino della provincia anconitana* describes Lacrima from leaves and bunches collected across the Marche.
- The Regio Istituto Tecnico di Ancona analysed its wine, and the book judges it «Ottimo vino aromatico, atteso il grato profumo».
- In 1879 Lacrima was one of only three Marche varieties in the first volume of the *Ampelografia italiana* (Turin, Comitato Ampelografico Centrale).
- It shrank in the post-war replanting and was down to under 10 ha when Stefano Mancinelli began bottling in 1985. 256 ha by 2025.
- Sources: Lacrima disciplinare art. 9 (Regione Marche PDF); W11.

**f. Pliny on the wines "born at Ancona" (Conero reading).**
- *Naturalis Historia* XIV.67: «Ex reliquis autem a supero mari Praetutia atque Ancone nascentia, et quae a palma una forte enata palmensia appellavere».
- On the Adriatic side Pliny names the Praetutian wines (the Teramo district), those "born at Ancona", and the "Palmensia", named after a single palm that happened to grow there.
- The Conero disciplinare misquotes this as a «vino "Pretoriano" prodotto nella zona di Ancona»; the passage names two wines, not one.
- Latin text: https://www.latin.it/autore/plinio_il_vecchio/naturalis_historia/!14!liber_xiv
- Where the ager Palmensis lay was not verified here.

**g. Andrea Bacci, *De naturali vinorum historia* (Rome 1596) (either reading).**
- Bacci was born 1524 at Sant'Elpidio a Mare, practised medicine at Serra San Quirico in the Jesi hills, then became physician to Pope Sixtus V. WineNews calls his book the first Italian wine guide.
- Dedicated to Cardinal Ascanio Colonna.
- It has a chapter **"In Picenis"** that surveys wines from Ascoli and the Tronto valley north to Pesaro; it reportedly mentions a "Pretuziana" grape at Ancona making a very clear wine [snippet].
- Primary text is on the Internet Archive (https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_RPdmcHgp1QEC). Read the Picenum chapter before quoting.
- Source: https://winenews.it/it/500-anni-dalla-nascita-di-andrea-bacci-autore-della-prima-guida-del-vino-italiano-della-storia_529464/ (24 June 2024).

**h. The wild moscioli of Portonovo (food link for the Conero reading).**
- Wild mussels grow on submerged rocks, above all the «Trave» reef off Mezzavalle, on the stretch from Pietralacroce to the Sassi Neri at Sirolo, at 5–10 m.
- Harvested by divers who tear them off the rock, traditionally from April or May to October; the season opened 15 May until 2025.
- **Slow Food Presidium since 2004**, set up by the Slow Food Conero branch with Slow Food Italia.
- On **26 June 2025** Slow Food suspended the Presidium and asked for a full fishing stop for 2025, because of warmer water, mucilage and over-harvesting, including illegal and private collection.
  - Raffaella Ponzio: «L'obiettivo è ovviamente riaprire il Presidio in futuro».
  - Edoardo Baleani: «l'unica soluzione a breve termine … è la chiusura completa della pesca per la stagione 2025».
- Portonovo lies under the cliffs of the park. The natural pairing is a Verdicchio, not the Conero red.
- Sources: https://www.slowfood.it/comunicati-stampa/serve-stop-pesca-mosciolo-selvatico-portonovo/ ; https://letsmarche.it/-/mosciolo-selvatico-di-portonovo-presidio-slow-food ; https://www.cibotoday.it/storie/territorio/moscioli-selvatici-conero-marche.html

**i. Ampelio Bucci (Verdicchio reading, as a person rather than an opener).**
- Born Montecarotto 1936. A Bocconi economist who taught design and fashion strategy.
- Made his first Verdicchio in 1982 and built the Villa Bucci style with Giorgio Grai in old Slavonian casks.
- The 2019 vintage was no. 2 in Wine Enthusiast's Top 100. He sold the estate in July 2024 and died in August 2025 aged 89.
- Sources: W6.
