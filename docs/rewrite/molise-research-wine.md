# Molise — research pass for the two wine readings (1: the region and its DOCs; 2: Tintilia)

Research done 2026-09-16 for the rewrite of `content/molise.js` readings 1 and 2 (`window.READINGS['IT-67']`).
Claims extracted from the existing text; verdicts CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / UNSUPPORTED / CANNOT VERIFY.
Labels: **[producer]** the estate's own account, **[promotional]** consortium/marketing, **[press]** trade or
local press, **[calculated]** arithmetic of mine.

**Note on primary sources.** The Ministry's catalogue, `catalogoviti.politicheagricole.it`, refused every
connection this session (ECONNREFUSED) — the same failure the Abruzzo pass hit. Every *disciplinare* quoted
below therefore comes from mirrors (disciplinare.it, quattrocalici, assovini, agraria.org, italianwinecentral),
which agree with each other on every number I checked twice. Anyone revisiting this should retry the ministry
host for literal article text. `tandfonline.com`, `academia.edu` and `medium.com` all returned 403 to the
fetcher, so two items below are cited from search-result metadata rather than from an opened page; they are
marked as such.

**Headline: the reading contains one hard date error (the Molise DOC is 1998, not 1988), one rule error
(the Riserva yield), one geography error (the latitude of Naples), one producer duplicated as two estates,
and it misses the region's fourth DOC entirely.** The Tintilia origin story it tells is also the wrong way
round: the single oldest document points to *France*, not Spain.

## Part A — verification

### Reading 1

### W1. "Il Molise non esiste"
**Claim:** a running joke on the Italian internet, with maps that leave a hole between Abruzzo and Puglia.
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**, and it can be dated.
The phrase appears on Italian forums in the early 2000s and goes viral around 2013–2014 on Facebook, Reddit
and Twitter, with the hashtag `#ilmolisenonesiste`; the region's own tourist marketing later borrowed it.
The stated cause is the region's invisibility: youngest, smallest-but-one, under 300,000 people.
URL: https://www.primonumero.it/2025/04/il-molise-non-esiste-storia-di-un-meme-culturale-italiano/1530857713/
Corroborated: https://www.geopop.it/perche-si-dice-che-il-molise-non-esiste-storia-e-origine-del-meme-culturale-sulla-regione-italiana/ **[press]**
**Useful:** it is a *documented* meme with a decade attached, not a vague "people joke that". The rewrite can
date it instead of gesturing at it.

### W2. "It became a region in its own right only in 1963"
**Verdict: CORRECTED — the date is defensible but the sentence as written is wrong in two ways.**
- The instrument is **legge costituzionale 27 dicembre 1963, n. 3**, which rewrote Article 131 of the
  Constitution to list twenty regions for the first time and split Abruzzi e Molise in two. So 1963 is the
  *law*.
- The region came into being administratively on **19 gennaio 1964**.
  URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise
- **The bigger omission:** from 1963 the region consisted of the province of Campobasso *alone*. The province
  of **Isernia was created only in 1970**. So the reading's "Molise has only two provinces" is true today but
  the region spent its first seven years as a single province.
  URL: https://www.molisecitta.it/la-nascita-della-regione-molise-un-viaggio-attraverso-la-storia/ **[press]**
- It is the **only case in Italian history of a region formed by detaching from another**. That is a stronger
  and better-sourced line than "the newest region".
**Safe wording:** "split from Abruzzo by a constitutional law of December 1963, in being from January 1964, and
a one-province region until Isernia was created in 1970."

### W3. Size: "second smallest after Valle d'Aosta, about 4,460 square kilometres"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** «4 460,65 km²». URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise
(One press source rounds it to 4,459.80 km²; the difference is noise.)

### W4. Population: "fewer than 290,000 people… under one per cent of the country"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** 284,970 inhabitants at 30 June 2026. URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise
**[calculated]** against ~59 million, that is about 0.48% — "under one per cent" understates how small it is.
The rewrite could say "under half a per cent."

### W5. "Roughly 55% of the region is mountain and almost all the rest is hill"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and it is exact.** «55,3%» mountain, «44,7%» hill, **no plain at all** (0%).
URLs: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise and https://www.assovini.it/italia/molise/item/88-i-vini-della-regione-molise
The assovini wine sheet repeats the same split for the *vineyard*: «Montagna: 55% | Collina: 45% | Pianura: 0%».
**Useful:** the vineyard has the same shape as the region. That is a better sentence than "very little flat
land" because it is a measured coincidence.

### W6. "a short Adriatic coastline of about thirty-five kilometres"
**Verdict: CORRECTED, trivially — the figure usually given is 36 km.** URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise
Not worth a correction on its own; write "about thirty-five" or "thirty-six", not "exactly".

### W7. The Biferno: "rises at Bojano under the Matese and runs eighty-five kilometres to the coast between
Termoli and Campomarino… dammed into the long reservoir of Guardialfiera"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, every element, and there is better detail available.**
- «nasce nel comune di Bojano, in località **Pietrecadute**, a **500 m s.l.m.**», formed «dall'unione di
  svariati corsi d'acqua provenienti dal massiccio calcareo del Matese, il principale dei quali è il fiume
  Calderari».
- Length 84–85 km.
- «sfocia nel Lago di Guardialfiera, ampio bacino artificiale costruito tra gli anni sessanta e settanta»,
  then «giungendo così dopo circa 30 km con una foce a estuario nel Mar Adriatico, tra Termoli e Campomarino».
URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biferno
**Note:** the reservoir is 1960s–70s, i.e. it post-dates the region itself. The reading calls the valley below
the dam "the warmest, broadest and most planted land in Molise" — that judgement is **not sourced**; what is
sourced is that the Biferno DOC zone is the province of Campobasso and caps vineyards at 500 m for reds and
600 m for whites (W10), which does place it below the Tintilia country.

### W8. "Campobasso, is a town of around 45,000 people"
**Verdict: CORRECTED — 47,519 residents at 31 December 2024.** URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise
Reading 4 of the same file says "about 45,000" and calls it Italy's smallest regional capital; the population
figure should be fixed in both, or written as "not quite fifty thousand".

### W9. Emigration: "between 1951 and 1971 the region emptied… falling by about seven per cent in the decade
to 2021"
**Verdict: partly CONFIRMED, partly UNSUPPORTED.**
- Documented: a peak of about **331,000 inhabitants in 1951**, and «In circa 30 anni, la popolazione è scesa
  da 331 000 a 291 000 abitanti». URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise
- **[calculated]** 331,000 (1951) → 284,970 (2026) is a fall of about 14% across the whole period.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** the specific "about seven per cent in the decade to 2021". I found no source for that
  decadal figure this session. Either drop it or replace it with the sourced 1951 peak and the current number,
  which make the same point and are checkable.

### W10. Biferno DOC — date, grapes, Riserva
**Claim:** "Biferno DOC (1983), named after the river: Rosso and Rosato are 70–80% Montepulciano with 10–20%
Aglianico; Bianco is mostly Trebbiano Toscano. Riserva needs three years."
**Verdict: CONFIRMED on every element, with one internal disagreement about the white.**
- Decree: «Approvata con **D.P.R. 26.04.1983, G.U. 269 del 30.09.1983**»; «Ultime modifiche introdotte dal
  D.M. 07.03.2014». URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/biferno-doc/
- Rosso and Rosato: «Montepulciano: minimo 70% – massimo 80%; Aglianico: minimo 10% - massimo 20%», balance up
  to 20% other permitted non-aromatic black grapes.
  URL: https://www.agraria.org/vini/biferno-doc.htm
- Minimum total alcohol: Rosso and Rosato **11.50%**, Bianco **10.50%**, Rosso Superiore **12.50%**, Rosso
  Riserva **13.00%**. URL: https://www.agraria.org/vini/biferno-doc.htm
- **Riserva: minimum 3 years**, "including 6 months in bottle" per one source.
  URLs: https://italianwinecentral.com/denomination/biferno-doc/ and
  https://drinkitalian.com/italian-wines-guide/biferno-doc/ **[press]**
  (The disciplinare mirrors I could open did not print the ageing article; two secondary sources agree on
  three years, so the reading's "three years" stands, but the "six months in bottle" detail rests on one
  source — attribute it or leave it out.)
- Zone: **province of Campobasso only**, about forty-odd comuni including Termoli, Larino and Campobasso.
  Altitude ceiling: «max 500 m.l.m.» for black grapes, «max 600 m.l.m.» for white.
  URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/biferno-doc.html
  **This is worth using:** Biferno is capped *above* at 500 m and Tintilia is floored *below* at 200 m. The two
  appellations are stacked on the same hillsides, one for the lower ground and one for the higher. The reading
  gestures at this ("Tintilia belongs to the hills above") but does not know it is written into both rulebooks.
- **Flagged disagreement on the Bianco.** Three mirrors give three compositions:
  «Trebbiano Toscano 70% - 80%» + 20–30% others (agraria.org, disciplinare.it);
  «Trebbiano Toscano (65-70%), Bombino bianco (25-30%), Malvasia (5-10%)» (quattrocalici, drinkitalian).
  The reading's "mostly Trebbiano Toscano" is safe either way. Do not print a precise percentage for the white.
- Scale: **48 hectares and about 2,950 hl / 32,800 cases on a five-year average, 2021 data.**
  URL: https://italianwinecentral.com/denomination/biferno-doc/
  That is a startling number — the region's "best known" appellation is forty-eight hectares — and it supports
  the reading's whole argument far better than any adjective.

### W11. "Molise DOC (1988)"
**Verdict: CORRECTED — it is 1998, not 1988. This is the clearest factual error in the reading.**
- «riconosciuta con Decreto Ministeriale del **18 maggio 1998**», published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale of
  2 June 1998. URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise_Montepulciano
- "Established as a DOC in **1998**." URL: https://italianwinecentral.com/denomination/molise-doc/
- «Molise o del Molise DOC, istituita nel **1998**». URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/regione/molise/
Three independent sources, one date. The recap bullet ("the catch-all Molise DOC (1988)") must change too.
- Content: it is indeed a catch-all. Red/rosato varietals at «Montepulciano (min. 85%), Aglianico, Tintilia,
  Cabernet Sauvignon, Sangiovese»; whites at «Moscato Bianco, Chardonnay, Falanghina, Greco Bianco, Pinot
  Bianco, Sauvignon, Trebbiano, (min. 85%)», each 85% varietal plus 15% other permitted grapes (50% for
  sparkling). Zone: over 80 comuni in Campobasso and over 40 in Isernia — effectively both provinces entire.
  URLs: https://www.disciplinare.it/molise-o-del-molise-doc.html and https://www.agraria.org/vini/molise-doc.htm
- Scale: **177 hectares, 4,740 hl / 52,700 cases on a five-year average, 2021 data.**
  URL: https://italianwinecentral.com/denomination/molise-doc/

### W12. Tintilia del Molise DOC (2011) and the 200-metre rule
**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — see W22–W24 for the full text. Decree **1 giugno 2011, G.U. 139 del 17.06.2011**,
with a corrective decree of 20 September 2011 (G.U. 231 of 4 October 2011) and an ordinary modification
D.M. 22.09.2022. URLs: https://www.disciplinare.it/tintilia-del-molise-doc.html and
https://www.assovini.it/italia/molise/item/317-tintilia-del-molise-doc

### W13. "Montepulciano was permitted under the appellation until 2000, when it was removed after a legal
challenge from Tuscan producers over the use of the name Montepulciano"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and it is a far better story than the reading's one-sentence "curiosity" suggests.**
- The type was called **Molise Montepulciano**, permitted **from 1998 to 2000** in both provinces.
- «La denominazione fu annullata a seguito di un ricorso sull'uso del nome "Montepulciano" depositato dalla
  **Regione Toscana**, dalla **Provincia di Siena** e dal **Consorzio del Vino Nobile di Montepulciano** presso
  il **TAR del Lazio** nel **2000**.»
- The instrument: a **D.M. of 10 October 2000** (Ministero delle Politiche Agricole e Forestali), published in
  the Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 240 of 13 October 2000, annulling the relevant provisions of the decree of
  18 May 1998.
- **What survived:** the Molise DOC itself. Wines of at least 85% Montepulciano are sold today as **Molise
  Rosso DOC** — the grape is still there, the *word* is not allowed on the label as the type name.
URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise_Montepulciano
**The point the rewrite should make:** Molise had been growing a grape called Montepulciano for generations,
and Tuscany — whose Vino Nobile di Montepulciano is made from Sangiovese and named after a *town* — took the
name off Molise's labels through an administrative court. Abruzzo, which had held Montepulciano d'Abruzzo
since 1968, kept its name; Molise, which asked in 1998, did not. That asymmetry is documented and is worth
staging honestly. (The Abruzzo research file, W1, establishes that the Tuscan wine has no connection with the
grape at all.)
**Caution:** do not write that Tuscany "sued Molise's growers". The parties were a region, a province and a
consortium; the respondent was the ministry; the remedy was the annulment of a decree.

### W14. The fourth DOC — **Pentro di Isernia, which the reading does not mention at all**
**Verdict: a real omission, and it undercuts reading 2's central claim.**
- «Denominazione di Origine Controllata … riconosciuta con **Decreto del Presidente della Repubblica il
  18 maggio 1983**, pubblicato sulla G.U. del 26/01/1984 n. 26». Zone: seventeen comuni in the province of
  Isernia. URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/pentro-di-isernia-o-pentro-doc.html
- Composition, Rosso and Rosato: **«Montepulciano: dal 75% al 80%; Tintilia: dal 20% al 25%»**.
  Bianco: «Trebbiano toscano dal 60% al 70%; Bombino bianco dal 30% al 40%» (agraria.org gives 60–70/30–40;
  disciplinare.it gives «Falanghina 80%; Trebbiano toscano dal 15% al 20%» — **flagged disagreement**, do not
  print the white).
  URLs: https://www.agraria.org/vini/pentro-doc.htm and https://www.disciplinare.it/pentro-di-isernia-o-pentro-doc.html
- Minimum alcohol: Bianco 10.50%, Rosso and Rosato 11.00%, Rosso Riserva 12.50%. Yields 9 t/ha white,
  11 t/ha red.
**Why this matters:** **Tintilia was written into an Italian appellation in 1983 — twenty-eight years before
its own DOC.** Reading 2 says the grape "was close to gone" until "about thirty years ago" and that growers
"pushed Tintilia into bottles" leading to 2011. That is broadly true of *varietal* Tintilia, but the flat
narrative of rescue-from-nothing has to survive the fact that the state named the grape, at a mandatory
20–25% of a red blend, in a 1983 decree. Say both. The reading should add Pentro as the region's fourth DOC
regardless — a reading that lists "three names on the label" is simply missing one.
(The Molise sheet in `italia-course.html` lists four DOCs' worth of wines but the reading says three; assovini
confirms «Vini DOCG: 0 | Vini DOC: 4 | Vini IGT: 2».)

### W15. "Vinmonopolet lists a single wine from Molise… a Biferno Rosso Riserva"
**Verdict: CANNOT VERIFY this session.** `vinmonopolet.no/search?q=molise` returns only the page shell to the
fetcher (it is a client-rendered app), so no product list could be read. The claim is inherently perishable
anyway. **Recommendation:** either check it live immediately before publishing and date it in the text ("in
September 2026 Vinmonopolet listed…"), or rewrite it as the structural point, which is fully sourced: the
whole Tintilia denomination is around a hundred hectares (W25) and Biferno is forty-eight (W10), so there is
almost nothing to export.

### W16. "most of the crop still goes to cooperatives or is sold as bulk wine to blenders elsewhere"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance, by a good proxy and by named co-operatives — but no direct bulk figure
exists in the sources I reached.**
- The proxy, and it is strong: of the region's total wine production, **DOP wines are 13% and IGP 17.6%**
  (2013 data, «Fonte: UIV - ISTAT ed elaborazioni»). That leaves roughly **70% outside any denomination**.
  URL: https://www.assovini.it/italia/molise/item/88-i-vini-della-regione-molise
  Quattrocalici gives a harsher split on a different dataset: «vini DOP 6% vini IGP 12%», i.e. over 80%
  undenominated. URL: https://www.quattrocalici.it/regione/molise/
  **Flagged disagreement: 13%/17.6% vs 6%/12%.** Either way the conclusion holds.
- The co-operatives are real and old, and several are far larger than the estates:
  - **Cantina Sociale San Zenone**, Montenero di Bisaccia, founded in the mid-1970s by about thirty growers,
    now about 100 members with hundreds of hectares; it sells «in bottiglia, in bag-in-box da 5 litri o
    sfuso» — **bottles, bag-in-box and bulk, in its own description**.
    URL: https://cittadelvino.com/aziende/societa-cooperativa-cantina-sociale-san-zenone-s-r-l/
  - **Cantina Valtappino**, founded 1969 «grazie all'iniziativa di 274 viticoltori».
  - **Cantina Cliternia**, founded 1972, about 150 members.
    URL: https://cantinamolisana.it/alla-scoperta-elle-cantne-vini-molise/ **[press]**
- **Scale comparison [calculated]:** the region makes 319,000 hl; Biferno DOC and Molise DOC together account
  for about 7,690 hl (W10, W11). That is **under 3% of the region's wine** carrying its two main appellations.
  This single calculation is the most eloquent fact in the whole file and I recommend it be the spine of the
  "why you have never drunk it" section, in place of the Vinmonopolet anecdote.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** "sold as bulk wine to blenders elsewhere". That the wine leaves as bulk is supported; that
  its destination is blenders in other regions is not documented in anything I read for Molise. (It *is*
  documented for Abruzzo — see the Abruzzo file W18 — so do not import that finding across the border.)

### W17. "The region has perhaps a few dozen bottling estates"

### Reading 2

### W18. Tintilia was nearly lost and came back
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and the chronology in the reading is vague where the sources are precise.**
The documented sequence:
- **Phylloxera and post-war abandonment.** One source gives the collapse in hectares: the region fell from
  **20,000 hectares of vineyard to 8,900 by the 1950s**, with post-war land reform turning the interior over
  to cereals. URL: http://www.scuoladelgusto.net/tintilia-dalle-origini-ad-oggi-2/ **[single source — attribute
  it or leave the figures out]**
- «Nel secondo dopoguerra» Molise saw «progressivo abbandono della viticoltura, soprattutto nelle zone di
  antica coltivazione della Tintilia», with more productive Montepulciano and Trebbiano put in its place —
  which is exactly what the reading says. (This passage is reported to sit in the **DOC's own historical
  section**, which would make it the best possible source; I could not open the ministry text to confirm that
  attribution, so cite it as the mirror.) URL: https://www.disciplinare.it/tintilia-del-molise-doc.html
- **Early 1990s: Michele Tanno.** «Il recupero varietale, attraverso uno studio approfondito di materiale
  genetico di viti di Tintilia sparse su tutto il territorio regionale, nei primi anni '90, è da attribuire, in
  modo particolare, allo studioso dott. Michele Tanno, che per primo ha creduto nel recupero del vitigno,
  favorendone così la reintroduzione progressiva in varie realtà produttive.» An agronomist «esperto di frutti,
  grani antichi e di uve rare». URL: https://cucineditalia.com/tintilia-molise-vitigno-che-ha-sfidato-il-tempo/
- **1990s: Claudio Cipressi**, working with Tanno on microvinifications and pushing the genetic studies;
  he recovered old clones and replanted at **450 m at San Felice del Molise**.
  URLs: https://cucineditalia.com/tintilia-molise-vitigno-che-ha-sfidato-il-tempo/ and
  https://www.ilgolosario.it/it/cipressi-tintilia-molise **[press]**
- **1998: the first commercial bottle**, reported as coming from Valtappino di Campobasso.
  URL: https://www.hellotaste.it/vino/storia-del-vino/la-tintilia-del-molise **[single source — attribute it]**
- **2002/2003: entry in the national register.** «Dal 2002 la tintilia è iscritta al Registro Nazionale varietà
  di vite da vino come varietà autorizzata per il solo Molise» (vitae.aisitalia.it); another source dates the
  inscription to January 2003 (cucineditalia). **Flagged disagreement: 2002 vs January 2003.**
  URL: https://vitae.aisitalia.it/la-tintilia-espressione-di-un-territorio-che-esiste/
- **2011: the DOC.**
- **2017: the Consorzio di Tutela** (W26).
**So "a small group of growers and the regional agricultural service went looking for what was left" is right
in outline but anonymous.** Every step above has a name and a year. Use them.

### W19. "Where the name comes from, probably" — the Spanish story
**Claim:** the obvious explanation is *tingere*; "the explanation most sources prefer is the Spanish *tinto*…
which would date the name to the long Spanish rule over the Kingdom of Naples. Neither is proven."
**Verdict: the reading's caution is right, but it has the evidence backwards, and the honest version is a
better story.**
- The *tinto* story is indeed what most sources say, and they say it without evidence: «presumibilmente
  dall'etimo Tinto che in lingua iberica significa "rosso"» (vinoway); «potrebbe derivare da "tinto"»
  (quattrocalici); «Introdotto in Molise dalla dominazione spagnola dei Borboni nella seconda metà del
  Settecento» (vitae.aisitalia). Note the hedges — *presumibilmente*, *potrebbe*. It is a plausible etymology
  repeated as a history.
  URLs: https://www.vinoway.com/enografia/vitigni/tintilia/ , https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/tintilia/ ,
  https://vitae.aisitalia.it/la-tintilia-espressione-di-un-territorio-che-esiste/
- **But the single oldest document points to France, not Spain.** In the 1810 Pepe manuscript the vine is
  called **«Le Tenturier d'Espagne = Tintiglia»** — and *Tenturier d'Espagne* is a **French** varietal name
  ("the dyer of Spain"), not a Spanish one. Two independent sources report this:
  - «Le Tenturier d'Espagne = Tintiglia», with Pepe requesting **«marze forastiere»** — *foreign cuttings* —
    for the interior of Molise, to strengthen light wines short of colour and structure.
    URL: http://www.scuoladelgusto.net/tintilia-dalle-origini-ad-oggi-2/
  - The 1810 memoir names it «Tintilia» and «Tintiglia», also as «Tenturier d'Espagne», and «secondo
    l'agronomo molisano **proveniva dalla Francia**» — according to the Molise agronomist it came **from
    France**. URL: https://www.hellotaste.it/vino/storia-del-vino/la-tintilia-del-molise
  The same source notes **Di Rovasenda (1877)** citing the Spanish ampelographer **D. Roxas Clemente**, and
  distinguishing it from the Alicante varieties.
- **And the DNA says neither.** Tintilia from Molise is genetically distinct from Tintilia elsewhere in Italy
  **and from Spain** (W21).
**How to stage it honestly — this is the paragraph the rewrite should build:** three answers are on offer and
none of them is proven. The folk etymology says Spanish *tinto*. The oldest surviving document, from 1810,
gives it a French name and says it came from France — and asks for cuttings from outside, which is a request
to *import* a vine, not a description of a native one. The genetics, two centuries later, match nothing in
Spain. **Do not write "the explanation most sources prefer is the Spanish tinto" without saying that the one
primary document contradicts it.** That is exactly the kind of legend the brief asks to be staged rather than
repeated.
**What is NOT documented:** that Bourbon soldiers brought it; that it arrived "in the second half of the
1700s"; any archival record of introduction. Those are all assertion. Also note the puzzle nobody resolves: a
*teinturier* is properly a red-fleshed dyer grape, and no source I read states that Tintilia is one. Flag the
name as unexplained rather than explaining it.

### W20. "an agronomist's manuscript of 1810 describes it in Molise"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and the agronomist has a name and a village.**
**Raffaele Pepe, of Civitacampomarano** (province of Campobasso), in a memoir/manuscript of **1810**. His
concern was practical: he «invitava i produttori a migliorare la vinificazione» **«senza ricorrere a
sofisticate pratiche enologiche»** — urging growers to improve their winemaking without resorting to
sophisticated oenological practices.
URLs: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tintilia_del_Molise ,
https://www.disciplinare.it/tintilia-del-molise-doc-modifica-ordinaria-al-disciplinare-di-produzione-2022.html ,
https://www.lavinium.it/le-doc-del-molise-tintilia-del-molise/
**This reference sits in the appellation's own historical section**, which is the best place a fact of this

**Documented:** the name, the village, the year, the two quoted phrases, the fact that the reference sits in

**Not documented:** the manuscript's full title or where it is held; whether Pepe was describing a vine already
in Molise or proposing to bring one in (the sources differ in emphasis and the phrase «marze forastiere» cuts
against the native reading); whether *Tenturier* implies a red-fleshed dyer grape, which Tintilia is not
described as being.

### B3. **2000: the year Molise lost a word**
Molise got a DOC in 1998 that included a type called **Molise Montepulciano**. Within two years the **Regione
Toscana**, the **Provincia di Siena** and the **Consorzio del Vino Nobile di Montepulciano** went to the **TAR
del Lazio** over the use of the name, and a ministerial decree of **10 October 2000** struck it out. Molise's
growers kept the grape and lost the label; the wine is sold today as **Molise Rosso**, at least 85%
Montepulciano, with the grape's name off the front.
**Documented:** the 1998 decree and its date, the three challengers, the court, the year, the annulling decree
and its Gazzetta citation, and the surviving Molise Rosso rule.
**Not documented:** the legal reasoning; whether any Molise producer was heard; what the change cost anyone.
URL: https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise_Montepulciano
*Why it works:* it is the smallest region in the country being told by one of the largest what it may call its
own grape — and the Tuscan wine at issue is not even made from that grape (Abruzzo file W1). It also explains
the Molise DOC's odd catch-all shape, which the reading currently calls "confusing for drinkers" without
knowing why it is like that.

### B5. **2006: the catalogue was wrong**
**Reale, Pilla and Angiolillo** of the Università del Molise ran **thirteen SSR loci** across **37 accessions**
of Tintilia and its supposed relatives from southern Italy and Spain. The Molise samples were all identical to
each other, and different from every 'Tintilia' elsewhere in Italy and from Spain; and **Tintilia and Bovale
grande turned out to be different varieties "even though they have been reported in the *Vitis International
Variety Catalogue* as being identical."** Pilla's own conclusion stayed careful: an Iberian origin remains
plausible, «la cultivar originale potrebbe essersi persa o la Tintilia aver subito qualche mutazione».

### B7. **Paris, 1900: the gold medal**
The **Casa Vinicola Janigro** of **Montagano** «nella seconda metà dell'800, produceva e imbottigliava

### B8. **Giuseppe Mogavero (1937–2015)**
Born in **Chieti, 30 August 1937**, agronomy degree at Naples in 1960, a bank executive at the Banco di Napoli,
then elected to the Molise regional council in its **first legislature (1970)** and re-elected through four
more **until 1995**, serving as regional assessor for industry, commerce and craft. In **1975** he founded
**Colle Sereno – Tenute Mogavero** at **Petrella Tifernina** and put Tintilia back in the ground there. He died
in Campobasso on **14 January 2015**, aged 77. His obituary calls him «considerato lo scopritore» of the
variety, and another source calls him its modern "father".
**Documented:** all the dates, the offices, the estate and its founding year, the death.
**Not documented — and this matters:** the "discoverer" claim is an obituarist's phrase and it **conflicts with
the Tanno account (B4)**, which credits the early-1990s genetic recovery to someone else. Both are in
circulation. If the rewrite uses Mogavero, it should say that the region has more than one candidate for who
saved the grape, which is itself true and characteristic.

*Why it works:* a regional politician who spent twenty-five years in the council and also planted the grape he
