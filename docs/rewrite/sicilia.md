### 1a. The template risk

Nineteen regions in, the two shapes most likely to repeat are **"poor isolated place, therefore authentic food"**
(used up by Basilicata, Molise, Calabria, Abruzzo, and refused for Sardegna) and **"conquered repeatedly,
therefore layered cuisine"**. The second is the one Sicilia will reach for, because it is true. But
**Sardegna's ledger §1d already assigned the conquest-list opening to Sicilia** on the grounds that for Sicily
the conquests genuinely are the point. So it may be used — *once*, in one reading, with each conqueror
attached to something specific — and never as a mood.

## 2. Map and scene facts, stated up front

Sicilia's trap is not direction, as Calabria's was, nor inland-versus-coast, as Sardegna's. It is **period**.
The island ran Greek, Roman, Byzantine, **Arab (827–1091)**, Norman, Swabian, Angevin, Aragonese, Spanish and
Bourbon, and the food claims attach to specific periods that are routinely collapsed into "the Arabs brought
everything". Every claim of Arab introduction needs its own date, and several are wrong: the **sweet orange
arrives centuries after the Arab period**, brought by the Portuguese, while the **bitter orange is Arab**.
Second trap: **Etna is three mountains for a grower** — the north-east slope, the east, and the south-east —
and they are not interchangeable.

## 3b. Propagation watch list — written BEFORE the verdicts

As in Sardegna, this is recorded ahead of the research so the check is honest rather than retrofitted.

**Quiz (12 questions, both languages). Five at risk, and the first is the familiar trap:**

- **Q1.1** scores *"The soil is so sandy that it [phylloxera] could not survive"* on Etna. Campania's research
  did establish sand as the mechanism — but §1 of this ledger has just ruled that **Etna's argument must be
  altitude and ash**, because the sand case is spent three ways over. If the research says altitude, **the
  scored answer is itself the error.**
- **Q1.3** scores *"Every eruption lays down another layer of soil"* under the framing *"the one region where
  the terroir is still being made"*. That is the kind of claim research deflates; watch for how fast lava
  actually weathers into soil.
- **Q2.1** scores *"Temperature control in the cellar"* as what fixed Sicilian whites. Plausible, unsourced.
- **Q2.3** scores the **cooking-Marsala** story as what ruined the name. The brief asks specifically about the
  **1984 reform** that banned flavoured Marsala from the DOC — if the ruin is the 1984 regulation rather than
  the tax dodge, the explanation is wrong even if the answer stands.
- **Q3.3** scores granita as *"a smooth, slowly churned semi-frozen water ice"*. The region's own recipe
  headnote says the smoothness comes from **a fork dragged through it every half hour**, which is not
  churning. **The quiz and the recipe already contradict each other**, independently of any research.

**Recipes (5). Three assert Arab introduction flatly**, and §2 of this ledger warns that these claims are
routinely collapsed:

- `arancini`: *"Nothing in an arancino is native to Sicily. Rice, saffron and the habit of deep-frying all
  arrived with the Emirate of Sicily between 827 and 1091."*
- `caponata`: *"part of what two hundred years of Arab rule left behind, along with the aubergine itself."*
- `granita`: *"descends from Arab sharbat and from a trade that sounds impossible: snow cut on Etna…"*
- `pasta-alla-norma` attributes the name to a local writer's compliment about Bellini's opera — the brief asks
  whether that is documented.
- `pasta-con-le-sarde` calls the pine nuts, raisins and saffron *"the Arab pantry"*.

**Sheet, both languages.** The intro reads *"has been Greek, Arab, Norman and Spanish, and the kitchen shows
it"* — which is the conquest-list framing §1a assigns to this region, so it stands. The landmark pairs Etna
with the Valley of the Temples and calls Etna *"Europe's most active volcano (3,357 m)"*: **the height changes
with eruptions and needs checking against a current figure.**

## 5. Standing rules carried from Sardegna

1. **Check the four leads against each other**, not only against other regions. Sardegna's readings 1 and 2
   both opened "There is a…" and only a reviewer caught it.
2. **Grep the English for pseudo-clefts before drafting Norwegian.** `What … is`, `, is ` — seven Norwegian
   clefts in Sardegna all came from English sentences built that way, and the sweep must match **both**
   `Det som …, er` and `Hva …, er`.
3. **A prose linter cannot see spelling.** Six typos reached review in Sardegna, three of them Danish forms.
4. **Every fix needs its own re-lint.** Three fixes in Sardegna each created the next defect.
5. **Grep every ledger promise against the draft before the reviews**, and remember the audit and the grep are
   not substitutes: the grep searches the whole file, the audit only spoken prose.
6. **A compressed ledger generates false UNSUPPORTED findings.** Tell fact reviewers to write "not in the
   ledger" instead, as the Sardegna re-run brief did.
