## 1. Sangiovese: one grape, many faces
- Summary → one sentence: Chianti and Brunello taste like different countries and are the same grape an hour
  apart; Sangiovese shows where it grew and how it was treated.
- Lead: the two glasses side by side (plan §4c illustration, approved). Then: the grape is difficult (early
  bud, late ripening, thin skins, generous yields → 1970s thin sour wine) and therefore transparent: cool clay
  at 500 m vs warm limestone south of Montalcino. The family (Grosso = Brunello / Prugnolo Gentile; Piccolo in
  old Chianti). In the glass: colour, smell, mouth, as sentences with verbs. The Tuscan map: four places with
  verbs. At the table: acidity against fat, protein softens tannin, never an aperitif, shaped for the table
  the last chapter describes. Ending: the two glasses again, no longer a puzzle.
- Box: 10% of Italy's vineyard, two-thirds in Tuscany; sanguis Jovis; soils (galestro, alberese; volcanic and
  clay at Montalcino; sandy clay at Montepulciano); Romagna, Montefalco, Marche. Tasting table unchanged.
- Ledger: all old facts kept or to box. Cut: "Understanding why is the first lesson of Tuscany" (meta).
  "the fourth reading is about that table" → "the last chapter of this region". New: none.

## 3. Brunello, Nobile and the Super Tuscans
- Summary → one man's grape in the 1870s, its gentler cousin, and a marchese's private Cabernet that outsold
  Barolo.
- Lead: Mario Incisa della Rocchetta, 1944, Cabernet at Tenuta San Guido because the gravel looked like the
  Médoc; family wine for 25 years; nephew Piero Antinori; 1968 vintage sold 1971 as vino da tavola; more than
  Barolo. Then Montalcino: Ferruccio Biondi-Santi, 1870s, Brunello "the little brown one", 1888 alive a century
  later; first DOCG 1980; a hundred then two hundred producers; 2008 scandal, rule reaffirmed; Rosso di
  Montalcino. Vino Nobile: papal court, Prugnolo Gentile, ≥70%, two/three years, between the two in weight,
  not Montepulciano d'Abruzzo. Back to the coast: Tignanello 1971, Solaia, Ornellaia, Masseto; Bolgheri DOC
  1994, Sassicaia 2013, IGT Toscana; the rebels forced Chianti to reform. Vernaccia 1966, Dante; Vin Santo.
  Ending: callback to the marchese's table wine.
- Box: Brunello rules (100%, 5 years, 6 Riserva, 2 in wood; best need 10–15, live 30); Nobile rules;
  Bolgheri DOC 1994; Sassicaia DOC 2013; IGT Toscana; Vernaccia first DOC 1966. Tasting table unchanged.
- Ledger: all kept or to box. "poured at the end of every Tuscan meal" softened to "a Tuscan meal". New: none.

### 1. Sangiovese
Reader:
1. "Sangiovese does that… shows you… forgives neither": three personifications (rule 15). **Accepted**: the grape is
   named in the sentence before, and the wine "tastes of the hill it grew on and of how carefully it was farmed".
   "comes alive" became "tastes quite different", and "the grape shows both" in the ending was cut.
2. "Four names carry the grape's reputation": an inventory paragraph (rule 6). **Accepted**: rebuilt as a walk from
   the cool hills to the warm south to the coast. The Bolgheri Super Tuscans clause was dropped here because they are
   the subject of reading 3.
3. "Those same faults are what make it interesting" and "Same grape, different face." **Accepted**: both cut, and
   one sentence now carries the idea: "But a grape that reacts to everything also reacts to place."
4. "a wine of acidity and tannin, not of colour and weight" is a not-X-but-Y construction. **Accepted**: now "high in
   acidity and tannin and only moderate in colour and body" (the tasting table says medium ruby and medium body).
5. "have never drunk it as an aperitif" is an overclaim. **Accepted**: now "do not drink it as an aperitif", and the
   quiz question was reworded to match in both languages.
Facts:
- "fifteen years" had no source. Now "ten years or more", from reading 3's box: best Brunello needs 10–15 years.
- "pale, sharp" Chianti against "dark, dense" Brunello. Now "lighter and sharper" and "darker and denser", which the
  two tasting tables support (medium ruby and medium body; medium to deep garnet and full body).
- "an hour's drive apart": **kept**. Castellina in Chianti to Montalcino is about 60–70 km by road, roughly an hour.
- The ending pinned clay to Chianti, which contradicted the box (galestro and alberese). It now says "the higher,
  cooler hills between Florence and Siena" (from reading 2: Radda and Gaiole are high and cool) against "south of
  Montalcino, warmer and drier, with the sea air of the Maremma" (the montalcino caption, and reading 3's old text).
- "a spring frost can take the crop" and "more fruit than they could ripen": **kept**. Each is the general
  viticultural consequence of a fault the old text names (early budding, generous yields), and neither adds a fact.

### 2. Chianti Classico
Reader:
1. Gran Selezione was named in the prose before anything explained it. **Accepted**: "the top tier, Gran Selezione",
   and the sentence was split.
2. "The hills… separating themselves… they did it" is personification. **Accepted**: the growers are now the
   subject.
3. "made law, with far too much white grape in it… nearly killed the wine's name": ambiguous, and figurative.
   **Accepted, with a sourced fact**: the 1967 law allowed 10–30% white grapes, most of them Trebbiano, where
   Ricasoli's recipe used about 10% Malvasia. Sources: Jancis Robinson, "Chianti Classico – thoroughly red now"
   (jancisrobinson.com); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_wine.
4. "Chianti Classico is learning to talk about places" is personification. **Accepted**: now "As in the Langhe, the
   talk here is now of villages."
5. Six village names in one paragraph is a list. **Accepted in part**: Castellina ("between them" in style) moved to
   the chianti figure caption; Greve and Castelnuovo Berardenga stay because they finish the cool-to-ripe contrast.
Facts:
- Lost: "became one of Italy's finest wines". **Restored** in the lead.
- "on the neck of every bottle": **kept, now sourced and dated**. Since 2005 the Gallo Nero has been the symbol of
  the whole appellation, printed on the government neck band of every Chianti Classico bottle (chianticlassico.com,
  "The Consortiums are reunited and the Black Rooster becomes the symbol of all Chianti Classico wines"). The ending
  "a black rooster on its neck" rests on the same source.
- "on every trattoria table abroad" in the summary is now "on trattoria tables abroad".
- "they did it by changing the rules" was an implied cause. It is now two facts side by side: "They changed the rules,
  and Chianti Classico became one of Italy's finest wines."
- Cut: "how to choose a bottle today" (the old summary's description of scope, not a fact).

### 3. Brunello, Nobile and the Super Tuscans
Reader:
1. The Montalcino paragraph read as a timeline. **Accepted in part**: the handful of estates now grows into two
   hundred producers, and the scandal follows from that. The suggested "among the first DOCG" was **rejected**:
   "the first" is the old text, the fortezza caption and the recap, and it is how the award is usually described.
2. "…best understood beside it" announces, and without the heading there was no spoken bridge to Montalcino.
   **Accepted**: the lead ends on "It was called Sassicaia", and the next paragraph opens "Inland, two hill towns
   south of Siena make Sangiovese under rules stricter than Chianti's", which also restores a lost fact.
3. IGT Toscana was explained three times. **Accepted**: the dates are box-only now, and the closer names the category
   once.
4. "The rebels became the establishment" is a stock phrase that repeats the heading. **Accepted**: cut.
5. The Nobile sentence ran to 32 words and repeated the box's ageing rules. **Accepted**: the ageing is in the box
   only. "Often the best value of the three" is **kept**: it is the old text and the recap, and it is not the ending.
Facts:
- Lost: Tenuta San Guido, "rules stricter than Chianti's", "priced like the finest Bordeaux", "they changed Italian
  wine". **All restored**: the lead, the Montalcino bridge, and the rebels paragraph.
- "outsold Barolo" (the summary and the outline) was a change of meaning, since the old text is about price. Now
  "cost more than Barolo". The summary was also given a main verb (rule 12).
- The 2008 scandal had been made to reaffirm the five-year release too. Now two sentences: the rule reaffirmed was
  "Sangiovese and nothing else", and the wine "is still released five years after the harvest".
- "where a Tuscan grower goes today when the rules get in the way": an unsupported claim, **cut**. The closer now
  restates the box: IGT Toscana is the category for wines that follow no traditional recipe.
