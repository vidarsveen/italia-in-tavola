## Paragraph by paragraph

Target 900–1,000 words. Headings are labels for the eye only.

1. **Lead — a scene at a time of day, then the contradiction.** Eleven in the morning by the market in
   Florence. The trippaio lifts a piece of lampredotto, the cow's fourth stomach, out of a pot where it has
   simmered for hours with tomato and celery, chops it, dips the bun in the broth, adds green sauce and
   chilli oil, and hands it over. A builder and a lawyer eat it standing up. Then the pivot: the most
   expensive thing on a Florentine menu, the steak, and the cheapest, this, are made by the same rule.
   Nothing is wasted, and every ingredient has to be good. Tuscans call it cucina povera and say it with
   pride. *(Same logic as Rome's fifth quarter, as now, in one sentence. Trippa alla fiorentina as the
   sit-down version, one sentence.)*

2. **Bread without salt** (label). Bite into Tuscan bread and something is missing. Why it has no salt is
   not settled, and the doubt is handled openly: a medieval salt tax, a Pisan blockade of Florence's salt,
   or simply that everything eaten with it is salty already, prosciutto, pecorino, finocchiona, anchovies;
   Florentines are content to blame Pisa. Consequence chain: no salt, so the bread goes stale in a day, so
   half the kitchen exists to use it. The dishes arrive as a year, each with a verb: ribollita in winter
   (cooked, left, boiled again until the spoon stands), pappa al pomodoro when the tomatoes come, panzanella
   soaked and squeezed for summer, and in November the first slice of grilled bread rubbed with garlic and
   drowned in oil so new and green it makes you cough, which Tuscans take as a sign of quality.

3. **Bean-eaters** (label). The nickname mangiafagioli, and why: beans were the protein, cannellini and the
   small yellow zolfini, cooked soft and dressed with the same oil. Short paragraph. It ends by putting a
   dish of beans beside the steak, which is where the next paragraph starts.

4. **The steak** (label). Chianina first: the huge white cattle of the Val di Chiana, one of the oldest
   breeds anywhere. Then the rules as acts, the butcher's and the griller's: a T-bone at least four fingers
   thick and over a kilo, dry-aged; oak or olive embers; a few minutes a side, then standing on the bone;
   charred outside, red inside; salt and pepper after, a thread of oil, never lemon; for two or more, sliced
   from the bone at the table, beans beside it, Chianti Classico in the glass because its acidity was made
   for this fat. The waiter's sad look at "well done" stays. **Then the history that reaches the plate,
   new and sourced**: Italy's first case of mad cow disease was found in January 2001; from 1 April 2001 the
   EU banned the sale of beef on the bone from cattle over twelve months old, which is exactly what a
   bistecca is; Florence protested to Brussels; the ban held until the end of 2005 and the steak came back
   in full in 2008. For nearly five years the city's most famous dish was illegal in the city.

5. **Autumn and the coast** (label). Three dishes with verbs, not a list: pici rolled between the palms in
   the hills around Siena and dressed with garlic and tomato or with breadcrumbs; pappardelle cut wide for
   the boar that comes out of the Chianti woods in autumn, with porcini from the Apennines; and at Livorno
   cacciucco, at least five kinds of fish stewed with garlic, chilli and tomato and poured over garlic
   bread. Kept short.

6. **Under the dome** (label) — the landmark, and the ending. Brunelleschi raised the dome between 1420 and
   1436 without scaffolding from the ground, a double shell of four million bricks laid in a herringbone
   pattern, still the largest masonry dome ever built. An hour west, Pisa's tower has leaned since the
   builders reached the third storey in 1178 and found the ground giving way; it was stabilised between 1990
   and 2001 and tilts a safe four degrees. **Ending**: the two buildings as the region's two poles, genius
   on a grand scale and a beautiful mistake everyone has learned to love, which is also ribollita and the
   bistecca. This is the current ending and it is an image and a callback, so it stays, reworded plainly.
   *(Alternative if the owner finds the parallel too neat: return to the cart at eleven, the broth pot
   empty by one.)*
