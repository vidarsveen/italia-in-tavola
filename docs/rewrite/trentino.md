**Checked before drafting** (two research subagents, about seventy claims). Errors or overclaims in the old text:
- "Trentino … Italian in feeling since the Renaissance … Alto Adige … Austrian until 1919": both provinces were
  Austrian Tyrol until 1918 (Trentino annexed to Tyrol 1803, merged 1816); both went to Italy by Saint-Germain, 1919.
- "together less than a tenth of Puglia": about a sixth (15,700 ha against about 90,000).
- "98% DOC, the highest share of any Italian region": 96–98% by source; "highest" not verified.
- "sold by the tanker to Austria and Germany": mainly Switzerland, by rail tank wagon from Bolzano, then road tanker.
- "within twenty years … Italy's most reliable white wines": opinion, no source.
- Ferrari "the biggest and best-known", "the wine … of every bar in Trento": no source. "It is the wine of Italian
  state occasions": a Quirinale custom since Pertini (producer and press), not a title.
- "Elisabetta Foradori rescued Teroldego from a century of overcropping": overstated; she began a massal selection in
  1985 against commercial clones.
- Gewürztraminer "takes its name from Tramin … where the Traminer family of grapes was recorded in the Middle Ages":
  the medieval Traminer of Tramin was a wine name, probably White Lagrein (consortium); the grape came from the north in
  1848.
- Gewürztraminer "dry": the consortium calls it the only "sweet" wine of Alto Adige, usually 5–10 g/l.
- Valle Isarco grapes "that nowhere else in Italy bothers with": Trentino grows three quarters of Italy's Müller-Thurgau.
- Val Venosta "Riesling at 1,000 metres": typical top sites 700–750 m.
- Muri-Gries "has made it for a century and a half": at Gries since 1845, Lagrein first only from 1985.
- Speck "smoked over beech wood": the rule is non-resinous wood; "at least twenty-two weeks": 20–32 weeks by weight;
  "rested for three weeks": not in the specification.
- "people who spent six months of the year in snow": no source.
- Kaiserschmarrn "with cranberry jam": lingonberry (Preiselbeeren), with apple purée; Vienna uses plums.
- Strudel "comes from Vienna": the oldest recipe (Vienna 1696) is turnip and milk-cream; the pastry came via Hungary.
- "Every one of them … better here": verdict.
- Carne salada is from the Alto Garda (Tenno, Arco, Riva), not general Trentino; IGP 2025.
- Graukäse "rindless": has a thin rind; the PDO Tiroler Graukäse covers only Austrian Tyrol.
- "a tenth of Europe's apples": 8–10%. "Melinda DOP": the DOP is Mela Val di Non; Melinda is the brand.
- Dolomites "coral reefs": platforms built mostly by microbes and algae, with sponges and some corals; 240–220 Ma, not 250.
- The moon-princess legend explains the pale colour; the pink glow is Laurin's rose garden.
- "via ferrata … a darker origin" (from the war): the first routes predate it (1899, 1903).
- "Men died of cold and avalanche more than of bullets": one popular historian's estimate for the whole White War.
- Ötzi's axe "rewrote the history of metalworking in Europe": overclaim (the 2017 study traced the copper to Tuscany);
  "the most visited museum in the region": not found. "Christmas market … the oldest in Italy": first held 1991.
- Buonconsiglio "the finest cycle of medieval frescoes in the Alps": verdict.
