Research only, no course prose. Checked 2026-09-15 against the current `content/umbria.js`.
Verdicts: confirmed / corrected / overstated / legend / not verifiable.
FF = Fonti Francescane number. AC = Assisi Compilation (Compilatio Assisiensis, in older Italian editions

## A. Reading 3, "Norcia: truffles, salumi and the norcino"

### 1. norcineria / norcino
**Confirmed, with a nuance.** Treccani: *norcino* is "abitante o nativo di Norcia" and also the man who
castrates pigs, butchers them and works and sells the meat. *Norcineria* is "nome dato un tempo a Roma" to the
shop where pork was butchered and sold, "rimasto più a lungo (e ancora vivo)" as a shop sign. So the word is
Roman and central Italian in origin, not used "in half the cities of Italy" with equal force. Safe wording:
"In Rome and much of central Italy the pork butcher's shop is still a *norcineria*."
- https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/norcineria/
- https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/norcino/

### 2. Norcia 604 m; Benedict and Scholastica c. 480
**Confirmed.** 604 m a.s.l. Tradition (from Gregory the Great's *Dialogues*, book II) makes Benedict and his
twin Scholastica natives of Norcia, born around 480. The exact day and the twin detail are tradition, not
documented. Say "c. 480".
- https://www.comune.norcia.pg.it/c054035/zf/index.php/servizi-aggiuntivi/index/index/idtesto/234
- https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/benedetto-da-norcia-santo_(Enciclopedia-dei-ragazzi)/
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scolastica_da_Norcia

### 3. Norcini travelling for the winter slaughter
**Confirmed.** Norcini went seasonally to Rome, Tuscany and the rest of central Italy. The slaughter season ran
"tra il giorno di sant'Andrea (30 novembre) e quello di sant'Antonio Abate (17 gennaio)". Documents for Rome:
- February 1615: people from Norcia founded a confraternity of Saints Benedict and Scholastica. Paul V
  approved it on 9 November 1615, and Gregory XV made it an archconfraternity on 4 February 1623. It still has
  its church, Santi Benedetto e Scolastica all'Argentina (the "chiesa dei Norcini").
- 1677: "l'Università dei Pizzicaroli Norcini e Casciani" (the guild of norcini and cascia pork dealers) and
  "i Medici Empirici Norcini" (the empirical surgeons from Norcia) both joined that confraternity.
  **Butchers and surgeons in the same brotherhood** is a documented food link (see Wanted stories).
- "since when": no source found fixing a start date for the migration. The guilds date from the 17th
  century. Do not write "for centuries" as if it were documented earlier.
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norcino
- https://www.nursini.org/quisumus.html
- https://it.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesa_dei_Santi_Benedetto_e_Scolastica_all'Argentina

### 4. Surgery: the Scuola chirurgica preciana
**Overstated in its reasoning.** The sources do not say "because they owned the sharp knives". The documented
link runs through **castration**: people from Preci, already skilled at castrating livestock, carried the
technique over into surgery on people. They were called "norcini" because Preci then belonged to Norcia.
- Setting: Val Castoriana, near the Benedictine abbey of Sant'Eutizio. The Rule's "infirmorum cura ante omnia"
  is cited as the monastic root.
- The four classic operations were lithotomy (bladder stones), cataract, hernia and castration. The school was
  active from the late Middle Ages and at its peak in the 16th century. Families still practised in the 18th.
- The Scacchi family: **Durante Scacchi** (b. 1540 in Preci) wrote ***Subsidium medicinae*, 1596**. The
  place of printing is usually given as Urbino, but I did not confirm that.
- **Elizabeth I: legend, or at best unproven.** The Italian tradition says Durante's brother, Cesare (or
  Francesco) Scacchi, was called to England c. 1588 to couch the Queen's cataract. A 2021 paper in the
  *American Journal of Ophthalmology* found "no English sources that allege Queen Elizabeth had cataracts, or
  substantiate the story". It suggests Scacchi may have come to treat Francis Walsingham, who had urinary
  trouble. The story comes from the Preci museum's archives. If used at all, say "the town tells that…".
- A "sultan" (Orazio Cattani serving "Sultan Mehmed" to 1620): it.wikipedia only, uncited. **Not verifiable.**
- **Castrati: sourced only weakly.** it.wikipedia says norcini were "molto richiesti per la castrazione dei
  bambini" for the *voci bianche*, and local writers repeat it. There is no archival citation. Better left out,
  or given as "said to".
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_chirurgica_preciana
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8446104/ (First Cataract Surgeons in the British Isles)
- https://www.accademiachirurgica.com/subsidium-medicinae/

### 5. Prosciutto di Norcia IGP
**Confirmed. Details:**
- Registered 12 June 1997 (Reg. CE 1065/97). The specification was amended again in 2026.
- Ageing: "non può essere inferiore a dodici mesi" from salting to sale.
- Weight: at least 8.5 kg. Pear shape, foot removed.
- Pigs: **"Non vi è limitazione geografica all'origine dei suini"**, so they need not come from Umbria.
  Heavy pigs (Large White Italiana, Landrace Italiana, Duroc crosses), slaughtered at 215–450 days, carcass
  110.1–180 kg.
- Salting in two stages with sea salt. Pepper 0.5–2% of the salt used. Fresh garlic, granules or powder
  allowed up to 2% of the salt.
- Zone: parts of Norcia, Preci, Cascia, Monteleone di Spoleto and Poggiodomo **above 500 m**.
- Taste in the spec: "sapore sapido, ma non salato", aroma "caratteristico, leggermente speziato". The
  comparison with Po valley hams is not in the spec, so it is an editorial judgement. "Slightly spiced from
  pepper" is supported.
- https://www.qualigeo.eu/prodotto-qualigeo/prosciutto-di-norcia-igp/
- https://www.disciplinare.it/prosciutto-di-norcia-igp-modifica-disciplinare-di-produzione-2026.html
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosciutto_di_Norcia

### 6. Other products; wild boar
**Products confirmed.** Norcia's norcinerie list salame di Norcia, capocollo (called lonza locally),
lombetto/lonzino, salsiccia, **mazzafegato** (sweet or salted liver sausage), barbozzo, coppa di testa (boiled
head meat with orange peel), sanguinaccio, ventresca, "coglioni di mulo", truffle salami, and wild boar
sausage and loin.
**"Unusually for Italy, wild boar" is overstated.** ISPRA estimated about 1.5 million wild boar in Italy in
2021, present across the peninsula. Wild boar salumi are a Norcia line but are not unusual.
- https://www.bottegadinorcia.it/tag-prodotto/salumi/
- https://www.deliciousitaly.com/umbria-food-wine/norcia-salumi
- https://www.isprambiente.gov.it/files2023/area-stampa/comunicati-stampa/comunicatocinghiali-1.pdf

### 7. Tuber melanosporum in Umbria
- **Season: corrected.** The old text says November to March. Umbria's law says **1 December to 15 March**
  ("b) dal 1° dicembre al 15 marzo: per il Tuber melanosporum Vitt, detto volgarmente tartufo nero pregiato").
  Source: LR 9 April 2015 n. 12, the Testo unico agricoltura, which absorbed LR 6/1994. The Giunta may shift
  dates for weather.
- Host trees: downy oak (roverella), holm oak, Turkey oak, hop hornbeam (carpino nero), hazel. Soils are
  calcareous, well drained, pH 7.5–8.5. It is hunted with trained dogs. **Confirmed.**
- Umbria's share: a Coldiretti-type figure says about 30% of Italy's black truffle, with Umbria and Abruzzo
  together about 53%, and 45% of truffle *exports*. These are secondary figures. **Not firmly verifiable**;
  avoid a number.
- "Takes heat": trade sources agree its aroma "non tende ad alterarsi con il calore", suited to warm sauces but
  not long cooking or frying. The contrast with white truffle, which is only shaved raw, is standard.
  **Confirmed as trade consensus.**
- https://leggi.alumbria.it/mostra_atto.php?id=81446 (LR 12/2015)
- https://www.prodottidinorcia.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/CALENDARIO-RACCOLTA-TARTUFO-da-comunita-montana-valnerina.pdf
- https://www.piantatartufigenadiqualita.it/specie-simbionti/

### 8. Scorzone and white truffle
**Confirmed.** Tuber aestivum (scorzone, summer truffle): last Sunday of May to 31 August. Tuber magnatum
(white): last Sunday of September to 31 December (same law). Fairs:
- **Città di Castello**: Mostra nazionale del tartufo bianco, first held **November 1980**, explicitly modelled
  on Norcia's black-truffle fair. Now the "Salone Nazionale Tartufo Bianco Pregiato" (45th edition 2025).
- **Gubbio**: Mostra Mercato Nazionale del Tartufo Bianco, 44th edition 2025 (first c. 1981–82), late
  October to early November.
- https://www.corrieredellumbria.it/news/attualita/8205/citta-di-castello-ecco-la-storia-di-come-40-anni-fa-e-nata-la-mostra-del-tartufo-bianco.html
- https://tartufodigubbio.it/la-mostra/

### 9. Norcia's truffle fair
**Corrected in detail.** It is called **Nero Norcia – Mostra mercato nazionale del tartufo nero pregiato**. It
now runs over the last weekend of February and the first of March (2026: 20–22 Feb and 27 Feb–1 Mar), so it is
not simply "February". The first truffle show in Norcia was held **in 1951**, before Christmas, in the Circolo
Cittadino (Palazzo Santoni), organised by Antonio Bianconi, Gaetano Allegrini and Giuseppe Millefiorini. **Its
stated aim was to cut out the middlemen** so truffle hunters got a fair price. Edition numbering is not
continuous: 2026 was the "62nd".
- https://www.e-borghi.com/eventi/norcia/nero-norcia-2026/
- https://www.ternitoday.it/eventi/via-edizione-2026-nero-norcia-tartufo-dettagli-programma.html
- https://www.valnerinaonline.it/neronorcia/

### 10. Cooking with black truffle; "alla norcina"
- Truffle warmed in oil with garlic (and often anchovy) for spaghetti or strangozzi **al tartufo** / *alla
  spoletina*: common in recipe literature. The anchovy is a variant, not universal. **Confirmed as common.**
- **"Alla norcina" means sausage, not truffle alone.** Standard recipe: crumbled Norcia sausage, onion, cream,
  pecorino, and truffle when affordable. At home often just sausage and cream. Keep "al tartufo" and "alla
  norcina" distinct.
- Strangozzi alla spoletina: tomato, garlic, oil, chilli.
- https://www.agrodolce.it/ricette/strangozzi-alla-norcina
- https://www.prodottidinorcia.it/pasta-strangozzi-alla-norcina-alla-spoletina-al-tartufo/

### 11. Castelluccio and the lentils
- Village: **1,452 m** (highest settlement in the Apennines). Pian Grande floor: **about 1,270 m**. The old
  "plain around 1,400 m" is **corrected** for the plain itself. The IGP specification, however, describes the
  cultivated upland (about 20 km², Norcia and Castelsantangelo sul Nera) as "un'altitudine media di 1400 m",
  since fields also lie on Pian Piccolo, Pian Perduto and the slopes. Safe wording: "the plain lies at about
  1,270 m, the village above it at 1,450 m".
- IGP: registered 12 June 1997, Reg. CE 1065/97; specification approved by DM 23 December 1998. Sowing mid-March
  to mid-May, yield at most 800 kg/ha, local ecotypes, 1,000 seeds ≈ 23 g, harvest by August.
- **No soaking**: stated by the consortium and producers ("non necessita di ammollo"), not a rule in the spec.
- **No chemicals**: not required by the specification. Sources say chemical weedkillers are not used in
  practice and altitude makes pesticides largely unnecessary. Word it as practice, not law.
- **Fioritura: mechanism confirmed.** The flowers are mostly arable weeds (*flora messicola*) growing among the
  lentils, "campi coltivati altamente infestati di malerbe che sono in concorrenza con la lenticchia". They
  survive because no herbicides are used. They bloom in sequence: wild mustard/charlock (yellow) first, then
  poppies (red), chamomile and ox-eye daisy (white), and cornflower (blue) last, early July. Period: **late May
  to early/mid July, peak late June to the first half of July.**
- https://www.qualigeo.eu/prodotto-qualigeo/lenticchia-di-castelluccio-di-norcia-igp/
- https://www.parcodelnera.it/saporivalnerina/disciplinare-produzione-della-indicazione-geografica-protetta-lenticchia-castelluccio-norcia-igp/index.html
- https://www.olioofficina.it/magazine/esperienze/gea-terra/un-utile-chiarimento-intorno-alla-fiorita-di-castelluccio.htm
- https://castelluccio.org/en/pian-grande/

### 12. Lentils with cotechino on New Year's Eve
**Overstated.** It is a pan-Italian custom (lentils for coins), not an Umbrian or Castelluccio rule, and
cotechino is a Po valley sausage. The ancient Roman "scarsella of lentils" origin is popular lore, **not
verifiable**. Write "the lentils Italians eat with cotechino at New Year".
- https://www.gamberorosso.it/notizie/rubriche/storie/perche-mangiano-cotechino-lenticchie-capodanno/

### 13. The 2016 earthquakes and Norcia's food trade
- Shocks: **24 August 2016** (M6.0, Amatrice) and **30 October 2016, M6.5**, epicentre near Norcia, the
  strongest of the sequence. On 30 October the roof, half the bell tower and most of the Portico delle Misure
  fell. **The nave became a heap of rubble and only the façade stood.** Confirmed.
- Rebuilding began 16 December 2021. **The basilica was returned to worship on 30–31 October 2025**, nine years
  to the day. Confirmed.
- **Deltaplano**, Castelluccio: inaugurated **29 October 2018**, 1,500 m² just below the village. It holds the
  ten businesses that were in the village before the quake: **eight restaurants**, a bar and a hang-gliding
  association. It was controversial as a possible "ecomostro" in the national park.
- Butchers after the quake: 48 military containers were delivered first. Norcinerie whose shops were unusable
  sold at fairs elsewhere and by mail. National press ran lists of producers so buyers could order directly
  "per fare vera solidarietà". Individual shop stories exist (e.g. Norcineria Laudani's own account).
- https://www.vaticannews.va/it/chiesa/news/2025-10/chiesa-religione-europa-italia-benedettini.html
- https://uss-sisma2016.cultura.gov.it/interventi-sul-territorio/umbria/basilica-san-benedetto-norcia/
- https://tg24.sky.it/perugia/2018/10/29/inaugurato-il-deltaplano-a-castelluccio
- https://www.scattidigusto.it/terremoto-lista-produttori-prosciutto-prodotti-norcia
- https://norcinerialaudani.com/oltre-la-scossa-come-abbiamo-salvato-la-nostra-norcineria/

### 14. Strangozzi and its names
**Partly corrected.** It is flour and water, no egg: confirmed. Names by place, from Umbrian food writers:
- strangozzi/stringozzi: Spoleto, Foligno and the Valnerina
- ciriole: Terni
- umbricelli/umbrichelli: Perugia, and also used around Orvieto; the name is from *lombrichi*, earthworms
- manfricoli: Orvieto
- anguillette: Lake Trasimeno
- picchiarelli: San Gemini

The old text's "strangozzi or strozzapreti in the south, umbricelli around Perugia and the lake" is roughly
right. Strozzapreti is a generic central-Italian name, so better to give Spoleto/strangozzi, Terni/ciriole,
Perugia/umbricelli. **Etymology: legend.** Shoelaces (*stringhe*) used by anticlericals to strangle priests in
the Papal State is folk tale. The shoelace resemblance is the plain explanation.
- https://www.ilgiornaledelcibo.it/pasta-tipica-umbra/
- https://www.aboutumbriamagazine.it/en/2017/03/03/umbrian-strangozzi-and-strozzapreti/

### 15. Porchetta
- **"An Umbrian invention as much as a Roman one": not verifiable.** Norcia, Costano and Ariccia all claim it.
  Say "Umbria and Lazio both claim it".
- **The fennel as the tell: confirmed.** In Umbria, the Marche and upper Lazio porchetta is flavoured with
  *finocchio selvatico*. **Porchetta di Ariccia IGP** (registered 2011) must use black pepper, **rosemary and
  garlic**, 150–250 g per 100 kg, each at least 20% of the mix. The Umbrian word for the dripping collected
  under the roasting pig is *intocco*.
- **Costano** (Bastia Umbra): Sagra della porchetta, first held in the **1960s** (founded by football-club
  officials Olimpio Lunghi and Francesco Del Bianco), last ten days of August, 50th edition 2025. The claim of
  "500 years of porchettai" there is local lore.
- **Regina in porchetta**: Trasimeno carp roasted with lard, garlic, salt, pepper and wild fennel, the pork
  treatment applied to a fish. Confirmed.
- https://www.qualigeo.eu/prodotto-qualigeo/porchetta-di-ariccia-igp/
- https://www.denticoporchetta.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Disciplinare_di_Produzione_Porchetta_di_Ariccia.pdf
- https://blog.cosedelposto.com/2020/10/02/porchetta-umbra-storia-e-tradizioni/
- https://www.comune.bastia.pg.it/vivere_il_comune/territorio/territorio_11.html

### 16. Lake Trasimeno food
**Confirmed.** Lake fish: carp, tench, eel, perch (persico reale), pike, *latterino*.
- **Tegamaccio**: lake-fish stew (tench, pike, eel, plus tomato, oil, chilli), named after the earthenware pan.
- **Carp was introduced in 1710**: 26 fish moved from Lake Bracciano to raise the lake's yield. The Region now
  lists it as a "pesce storico".
- **Fagiolina del Trasimeno**: Slow Food Presidium. A tiny multicoloured cowpea-type bean, grown for centuries,
  nearly lost after the 1950s; all work is by hand.
- https://www.umbriatourism.it/it/w/carpa-del-trasimeno
- https://www.mifacciodicultura.it/2025/08/03/le-carpe-del-trasimeno-pesca-storia-valorizzazione/
- https://www.fondazioneslowfood.com/it/presidi-slow-food/fagiolina-del-lago-trasimeno/

### 17. Unsalted bread and the Salt War
- The war was real. Late 1539 Paul III raised the salt price by three quattrini a pound during a famine; the
  increase was announced in Perugia on 20 February 1540. Perugia rebelled, led by Ridolfo Baglioni and Ascanio
  della Corgna. Papal troops under Pier Luigi Farnese, the pope's son, arrived in April. **Perugia capitulated on
  3 June 1540** and lost its autonomy.
- **Rocca Paolina**: built 1540–43 by Antonio da Sangallo the Younger over the razed Baglioni houses and the
  Colle Landone quarter, as a symbol of papal rule. Confirmed.
- **Unsalted bread as protest: legend.** it.wikipedia: "Una ricerca recente però sembra smentire questa
  leggenda". Other sources say it "non trova fonti storiche". Unsalted bread was already eaten in Florence
  before 1540, and the unsalted-bread belt runs from inner Tuscany through Umbria to the Marche. Dante,
  exiled from Florence, is told "come sa di sale / lo pane altrui" (*Paradiso* XVII, 58–59, c. 1316),
  usually read as the salted bread outside Tuscany. The Pisa–Florence salt blockade story is also legend.
  Alternative explanations: salt was dear and under state monopoly everywhere; the bread goes with salty salumi
  and pecorino. Write it as "Perugians like to say…".
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerra_del_sale_(1540)
- https://www.academia.edu/1965967/Il_pane_sciapo_e_la_Guerra_del_sale_di_Perugia (403 to fetch; title only)
- https://www.cookist.it/perche-il-pane-a-firenze-si-mangia-senza-sale/

### 18. Torta al testo
**Confirmed.** A flat bread cooked on the *testo*, from Latin *testum*. Originally a refractory-stone disc, now
usually cast iron about 3 cm thick. Names: **torta al testo** south of Perugia, **crescia** in Gubbio,
**ciaccia** in Città di Castello and the Alto Tevere (often with egg), "pizza sotto il fuoco" around Terni.
Claims of an ancient Umbrian origin are lore.
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torta_al_testo
- https://www.umbriatourism.it/en/w/vr-torta-al-testo-en

### 19. Perugina, Bacio, Eurochocolate
- **Founded 30 November 1907** as "Società Perugina per la fabbricazione dei confetti", capital 100,000 lire,
  by **Francesco Buitoni, Annibale Spagnoli, Leone Ascoli and Francesco Andreani**. Luisa Spagnoli, Annibale's
  wife, ran production. The move to Fontivegge came in 1915, and to **San Sisto in the 1960s**. **Nestlé
  acquired it in 1988.** Confirmed.
- **Bacio, 1922**: Luisa Spagnoli used hazelnut fragments left over from other production, with gianduja and a
  whole hazelnut, under dark chocolate. **First name "Cazzotto"** (fist), for the knuckle shape. Renamed
  "Bacio" by **Giovanni Buitoni**, Francesco's son, the young managing director and by tradition her lover.
- **Love notes: corrected.** The old caption says "wrapped since 1922 with a love note". The *cartigli* came
  **in the 1930s**, introduced by art director **Federico Seneca**, who also designed the silver wrapper and
  the Hayez-inspired blue box. Their origin in the lovers' notes is "according to legend".
- "Confectionery since the nineteenth century": **not verifiable** for Perugia as a trade. Drop it or say
  since 1907.
- **Eurochocolate: first edition 23 October 1994**, four days, about 50,000 visitors. The idea came from
  architect Eugenio Guarducci after Munich's Oktoberfest. Still in October. Confirmed.
- **Luisa Spagnoli's second business: 1928**, "Angora Spagnoli" at Santa Lucia, Perugia. She bred angora
  rabbits whose fur was combed, not sheared, and made shawls and knitwear. It became the Luisa Spagnoli fashion
  house.
- https://www.perugina.com/it/mondo-perugina/storia
- https://www.baciperugina.com/it/mondo/storia-baci-perugina
- https://www.magnanirocca.it/design-del-bacio-perugina/
- https://www.eurochocolate.com/ecimages/lugano2009/pdf/it_eurochocolate.pdf
- https://www.luisaspagnoli.com/it/us/history-page.html

## B. Reading 4, "Assisi and the hill towns"

### 20. Francis and the basilica
- **Renunciation before Bishop Guido II**: 1206 in most accounts, 1207 in some. Say "1206" or "in 1206 or
  1207".
- **Death**: evening of **3 October 1226**, at the Porziuncola. **Canonised 16 July 1228** in Assisi by
  Gregory IX. **Foundation stone 17 July 1228**, the very next day, laid by Gregory IX, with **Brother Elias**
  directing the building. The lower church was far enough along for the body to be moved in on 25 May 1230.
  **Both churches consecrated by Innocent IV in 1253** (25 May). UNESCO 2000.
- **Corrected in the old lead.** "Made a saint two years later, and the year after that the church began
  building" is wrong. Building began the day after the canonisation, in the same year. The body of the old text
  itself says 1228.
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Francesco_(Assisi)
- https://www.vaticannews.va/it/chiesa/news/2022-07/canonizzazione-san-francesco-16-luglio-1228.html
- https://www.foliamagazine.it/17-luglio-1228-iniziano-i-lavori-della-basilica-di-san-francesco/

### 21. Painters
**Confirmed.** Lower Church: Cimabue, Simone Martini, Pietro Lorenzetti and the Giotto workshop. Upper Church:
28 scenes of the life of Francis; Giotto's authorship has been disputed for a century. **"French Gothic" is
overstated.** The usual description is one of the first Gothic churches in Italy, an Italian Gothic with
French influence, over a Romanesque lower church. Write "a tall early Gothic hall".
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Francesco_(Assisi)

### 22. The 1997 earthquake
- **26 September 1997.** A first shock came at 02:33. The second, at **11:42**, brought down the vault while
  inspectors were inside.
- **Dead: corrected, "two surveyors" is wrong.** The four were **Bruno Brunacci and Claudio Bugiantella,
  technicians of the Soprintendenza**, and the friars **Angelo Api and Zdzisław Borowiec**. Chief restorer
  Sergio Fusetti survived.
- Lost: Cimabue's **Saint Matthew** from the Vault of the Evangelists; the **Vault of the Doctors of the
  Church** with **Saint Jerome** (attributed by some to young Giotto); eight saints on the counter-façade arch;
  the 19th-century starry vault.
- **Fragments: corrected.** "Tens of thousands" understates it: **over 300,000** fragments were recovered.
  The St Jerome vault alone was rebuilt from about 80,000.
- **Reopened 28 November 1999.** The recomposed vaults were unveiled later: 2002 (Jerome) and 2006 (Matthew).
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restauro_della_basilica_superiore_di_Assisi
- https://www.vaticannews.va/it/mondo/news/2022-09/assisi-umbria-marche-terremoto-1997-anniversario-basilica-morti.html

### 23. Food and Francis (primary sources)

**(a) Christmas: the walls should eat meat. Confirmed, 2 Cel 199 (FF 787), chapter CLI of the *Memoriale*.**
Christmas fell on a Friday and Brother Morico asked whether meat was allowed. Translation (FA:ED, vol. 2):
"You sin, brother, calling the day on which the child is born to us a day of fast. It is my wish that even the
walls should eat meat on such a day; and if they cannot, they should be smeared with meat on the outside."
Italian: «Voglio che in un giorno come questo anche i muri mangino carne».
2 Cel 200 continues. Francis wanted the rich to feed the poor, and oxen and asses to get more grain and hay
than usual. He said: "If I could speak to the emperor, I would ask that a general law be made that all who can
should scatter corn and grain along the roads so that the birds might have an abundance of food on that day of
such great solemnity, especially our sisters the larks."
The same wish appears in fuller form in **AC 14 (Leggenda perugina 110, FF 1669)** and **SP 114**. The
podestà and lords of every town and castle should each Christmas "incitare la gente che getti frumento e altre
granaglie sulle strade… E che a Natale si dia da mangiare in abbondanza ai fratelli buoi e asinelli. E ancora,
in quella festività, i poveri vengano ben provvisti di cibo dai benestanti".

- https://www.gliscritti.it/antologia/entry/1047

**(b) Jacopa de' Settesoli and the mostaccioli. Confirmed, three sources.**
- **AC 8 = Leggenda perugina 101 (FF 1657).** Dying at the Porziuncola, Francis asks the friars to write to
  "donna Jacopa dei Settesogli" for ash-grey cloth for a habit, "e insieme, invii un po' di quel dolce che era
  solita prepararmi quando soggiornavo a Roma". The text adds: **"Si tratta del dolce che i romani chiamano
  mostacciolo, ed è fatto con mandorle, zucchero o miele e altri ingredienti."** The Latin term is
  *mortariolum*. Before the letter could be sent, Jacopa knocked at the door, having come from Rome in haste.
  **"Donna Jacopa preparò poi il dolce che piaceva a Francesco. Ma egli lo assaggiò appena"**, because he was
  too weak. She also brought wax for candles, and the cloth became his burial habit.
- **SP 112 (FF 1812)**, "Del cibo e del panno che, presso a morire, egli desiderava": the same story. "I
  romani chiamano quel dolce: mostaccioli, ed è fatto di mandorle, zucchero e altri ingredienti… ma egli ne
  mangiò ben poco." He died the same week.
- **3 Cel 37–39** (*Treatise on the Miracles*) tells the visit more fully. There Francis calls her "frate
  Jacopa", and Elias invites her to embrace the body.
- Note: these are 1240s–1310s compilations from the companions' memories, not eyewitness diaries. Fine to say
  "his companions recorded".
- **Mostaccioli today**: *mostaccioli di San Francesco* recipes (almonds, honey, must, spices) are widely
  circulated and are made around 4 October. L'Osservatore Romano (2021) describes biscuits "come quelli che
  frate Jacopa preparò" handed out at Pope Francis's Assisi visit. I found **no reliable source naming a
  specific Assisi bakery or convent** selling them as a standing local product, so **not verifiable**. Avoid
  "still sold in Assisi".
- https://assisiofm.it/i-mostaccioli-nella-tradizione-medica-francescana/
- https://www.ofsliguria.it/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Specchio-di-perfezione.pdf
- https://www.osservatoreromano.va/it/news/2021-11/quo-258/quei-biscotti-cosi-buoni-come-quelli-che-frate-jacopa-preparo-a.html

**(c) Rule of 1223 (Regula bullata), ch. 3. Confirmed.** "Et secundum sanctum Evangelium de omnibus cibis, qui
apponuntur eis, liceat manducare" (Luke 10:8): they may eat whatever is set before them. Fasting: from All
Saints to Christmas, and on Fridays. The "blessed Lent" from Epiphany for forty days is voluntary: "coloro che
volontariamente la digiunano siano benedetti dal Signore, e coloro che non vogliono non vi siano obbligati".
Approved by Honorius III, *Solet annuere*, 29 November 1223.

**(d) Other food episodes tied to Umbria.**
- **SP 111 (FF 1811), the fish at the bishop's palace in Assisi.** Gravely ill, Francis refused food but said he
  might eat "quel pesce che si chiama squalo" (Latin *squalus*). A man then arrived with three large ones well
  cooked, and **"pasticci di gamberi, che il Santo mangiava volentieri"** (crayfish pies), sent by Brother
  Gerard, minister at Rieti, though it was winter and none could be had in Assisi. **Caution**: medieval Latin
  *squalus* here is almost certainly a freshwater fish (probably the chub, *Squalius cephalus*, "cavedano"),
  not a shark. I did not confirm the identification, so say "a river fish".
  Source: the Specchio di perfezione PDF above.
- **Wolf of Gubbio: legend** (*Fioretti* ch. 21, vernacular, late 14th c., from the Latin *Actus*, 1327–37).
  The pact is about food. «Io ti prometto ch'io ti farò dare le spese continuamente, mentre tu viverai, dagli
  uomini di questa terra, sicché tu non patirai più fame». The wolf lived two years in Gubbio, "cortesemente
  nutricato" by the townspeople.
  https://www.la-notizia.net/2022/09/09/il-lupo-di-gubbio-secondo-il-capitolo-xxi-dei-fioretti-di-san-francesco/
- The *Canticle*'s "frate focu" is praise of fire, not food. No food link found.

### 24. Orvieto cathedral
- **Miracle of Bolsena: 1263** (some sources give 1264). The corporal was brought to Urban IV at Orvieto.
- **Foundation stone 1290**, Nicholas IV. The day is traditionally given as **13 November 1290**; the Opera del
  Duomo page gives only the year.
- **Maitani: corrected from 1309 to 1310.** Documented in **1310** as "universalis capud magister", with
  Orvieto citizenship that year. He was active in the early 1300s; 1308–09 dates occur in secondary sources.
- **Signorelli: 1499–1504**, San Brizio (Cappella Nova). Confirmed.
- **Michelangelo: corrected.** Vasari, *Vita di Luca Signorelli*: "l'opere di Luca furono da Michelagnolo sempre
  sommamente lodate", and "in alcune cose del suo divino **Giudizio**, che fece nella cappella, furono da lui
  gentilmente tolte in parte dall'invenzioni di Luca". The influence Vasari names is on Michelangelo's **Last
  Judgement (1536–41)**, **not the Sistine ceiling (1508–12)**. That Michelangelo visited Orvieto is inference.
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duomo_di_Orvieto
- https://www.duomodiorvieto.it/opera-del-duomo/la-storia
- https://www.duomodiorvieto.it/duomo-di-orvieto/la-cappella-di-san-brizio

### 25. Orvieto underground, pigeons, the well
- **Over 1,200 cavities** (grottoes, tunnels, wells, cisterns) cut into the tufa over nearly three millennia,
  from the Etruscans on. Rediscovered by local speleologists in the late 1970s.
- **Colombaie: confirmed.** Caves with rows of square niches were used to breed pigeons intensively for meat,
  especially under siege, and for *colombina* (pigeon dung) to fertilise orchards and vineyards. Orvieto
  Underground's tour includes one. It also shows a **medieval olive press** in a cave near the Duomo, with
  millstones, press, hearth and cisterns. Food link.
- **Dish**: *piccione alla leccarda* (spit-roasted pigeon basted from the dripping pan, the *leccarda*, with
  wine, sage, olives and giblets). *Palombo*/*piccione alla ghiotta* is the same family of dish, "ghiotta"
  being the dripping pan. It is linked to Orvieto's banquets in the papal period by local sources.
- **Pozzo di San Patrizio: corrected dates.** Commissioned by **Clement VII in 1527**, after his flight from the
  Sack of Rome. Architect **Antonio da Sangallo the Younger**; **finished 1537** under Paul III. **54 m deep,
  13 m across, 248 steps, 72 windows.** Two independent helical ramps let mules carrying water go down and up
  without meeting. The old "sunk in the 1520s" becomes "begun in 1527, finished in 1537". "Two staircases" is
  better as "two spiral ramps". Inscription: "quod natura munimento inviderat industria adiecit".
- https://www.orvietounderground.it/index.php/it/orvieto-underground/la-scoperta
- https://vivereebenessere.altervista.org/orvieto-sotterranea/
- https://orvieto.italiani.it/scopricitta/piccione-alla-leccarda/
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozzo_di_San_Patrizio

### 26. Gubbio
- **Iguvine Tables: confirmed.** Seven bronze tablets. Traditionally found in **1444** near Scheggia (en.wiki);
  it.wiki calls the findspot uncertain. Bought by the comune by notarial deed on **25 August 1456**. Tablets
  I–IV and part of V are in the Umbrian alphabet (3rd–2nd c. BC), VI–VII in Latin letters (1st c. BC). The
  language is Umbrian throughout. They are the ritual texts of the priestly college of the **Atiedian Brothers**.
- **Content (food): confirmed.** Sacrifices of **oxen** (three to Jupiter Grabovius), **sows, piglets, sheep,
  ewe-lambs, calves, heifers, boars**. Cake offerings **ficla** and **strucla/struhçla** (sweet ritual cakes) and
  **mefa spefa** (a sacrificial cake). **Wine** libations. The brotherhood's shares of the sacrificial meat were
  **"divided upon the breadcrusts"** at a ritual meal (Tablet II, en.wiki summary). If quoting a tablet number,
  check the translation (Poultney 1959 or Ancillotti–Cerri).
- **Corsa dei Ceri: 15 May**, the eve of Sant'Ubaldo's death day (16 May 1160). Three ceri crowned with
  **Sant'Ubaldo, San Giorgio, Sant'Antonio Abate**. Heights with statue about 4.9–5.0 m; weights **263, 271,
  287 kg**. They are run about 4.3 km up Monte Ingino to the basilica of Sant'Ubaldo. **Food of the day**: the
  *ceraioli* eat **baccalà alla ceraiola** and **coratella d'agnello** under the arcades. The civic *tavola
  bona* lunch is held in the Palazzo dei Consoli.
- **Palazzo dei Consoli: 1332–1349**, Angelo da Orvieto with Matteo Gattapone, over 60 m high. Confirmed.
- White truffle fair: see item 8.
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavole_eugubine
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iguvine_Tablets
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festa_dei_Ceri
- https://www.palazzodeiconsoli.it/en/storia/

### 27. Spoleto
- **Ponte delle Torri: about 80 m high, 230 m long, nine arches** (Goethe counted ten). In its present form it
  is **late 14th century**, after 1363 under Cardinal Albornoz, attributed to Gattapone, possibly on Roman or
  earlier foundations. It carried an aqueduct and a footway. Confirmed.
- **Goethe, 27 October 1786** (*Italian Journey*): "I climbed Spoleto and was on the aqueduct, which is also a
  bridge from one mountain to another… the water still flows in every corner of Spoleto". Also: "three works
  by the ancients… a second nature serving civic ends". Note that he took it for Roman.
- **Turner**: *Spoleto*, c. 1840–45, Tate Britain. Confirmed.
- **Food links**: **Spoleto DOC** (disciplinare published 30 November 2011) for **Trebbiano Spoletino**, at
  least 85%, grown around Spoleto, Montefalco, Trevi, Foligno, Castel Ritaldi and Campello. Also *strangozzi
  alla spoletina* (tomato, garlic, chilli), and the black truffle of the Norcia–Spoleto area.

- https://www.consorziomontefalco.it/spoleto-doc/

### 28. Trasimeno, 217 BC
**Battle confirmed.** Hannibal ambushed consul Gaius Flaminius in morning mist on the north shore near Tuoro,
June 217 BC (Ovid's 21 June). About 15,000 Romans were killed. Archaeologists identified cremation pits
(*ustrina*) in the Sanguineto valley.
**The names Sanguineto and Ossaia are folk etymology ("tradizione popolare").** Write "local tradition connects
the names with blood and bones". No food link needed.
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battaglia_del_lago_Trasimeno

### 29. Cascata delle Marmore
- **271 BC**: consul Manius Curius Dentatus cut the **Cavo Curiano** to drain the Velino marshes of Rieti over
  the Marmore cliff into the Nera. Later cuts: Cavo Reatino (1422), Cavo Paolino (1545/47), Cava Clementina
  (1598). Andrea Vici reshaped the falls in 1787 for Pius VI, giving the present form.
- **165 m in three drops, the highest about 83–85 m.** Confirmed.
- **"One of the tallest waterfalls in Europe" is overstated.** Natural European falls are far taller. The
  defensible claim is **the highest man-made waterfall in Europe**, and some say in the world.
- Since **1929** the water feeds the Galleto hydroelectric plant. The falls run at full flow only at scheduled
  release times published by the park. Confirmed.
- https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascata_delle_Marmore
- https://cascatadellemarmore.info/orari-e-aperture/

## Wanted stories found

Each one is sourced and each one arrives at a dish, an ingredient, a wine or a way of eating.

1. **Francis wanted the walls to eat meat at Christmas** (2 Cel 199). Christmas fell on a Friday; Brother Morico
   asked about abstaining, and Francis answered that the walls should eat meat or be smeared with it. In the
   companions' version (AC 14 / SP 114) he would have the emperor order grain thrown on the roads for the
   larks, extra fodder for the oxen and asses, and the poor fed by the rich. It closes on a feast, not a fast.
2. **Jacopa's mostaccioli** (AC 8 / SP 112 / 3 Cel 37–39). The dying Francis dictates a letter to Rome asking
   for grey cloth and "that sweet the Romans call mostacciolo, made of almonds and sugar or honey". She knocks
   at the door before the letter is sent, having set out on her own. She makes the sweet, and he can manage
   only a taste. Almonds, honey and must: a biscuit still baked for 4 October.
3. **A sick man's appetite at the bishop's palace** (SP 111). Francis will not eat, but might manage a certain
   river fish. A basket arrives from the minister at Rieti with the fish and crayfish pies "which the saint ate
   gladly", in winter. Check the fish's identity before naming it.
4. **The Rule's table manners** (Regula bullata 3, 1223): eat whatever is put in front of you. The Lent of
   Epiphany is voluntary. A friar's diet is decided by the host.
5. **Butchers and surgeons in one brotherhood** (Rome, 1677). The guild of *pizzicaroli norcini e casciani* and
   the *medici empirici norcini* joined the same confraternity of Saints Benedict and Scholastica, which people
   from Norcia had founded in 1615. The skill that joined them was castrating pigs (Preci school). It works as
   an opener for the norcino and his salumi.
6. **The norcino's calendar**: the slaughter season ran from St Andrew (30 November) to St Anthony Abbot (17
   January), the patron of pigs, when norcini left the mountains for Rome and Tuscany.
7. **A truffle fair to beat the middlemen** (Norcia, 1951). The first black-truffle show was set up so hunters
   could sell straight to buyers at a fair price. Città di Castello copied it for white truffle in 1980.
8. **The lentil fields flower because they are weedy.** The famous colours of the Pian Grande are arable weeds
   (charlock, poppy, cornflower) that survive in lentil fields where no herbicide is used. They bloom in turn,
   cornflower last in July. The view is a by-product of how the lentil is grown.
9. **After 2016: eight restaurants in a shed.** Castelluccio's pre-quake businesses reopened in October 2018 in
   the "Deltaplano", eight of the ten being restaurants. Norcia's norcinerie sold by post and at fairs while
   their shops stood condemned, and buyers ordered direct in solidarity.
10. **The Iguvine Tables as a menu of the gods.** Oxen, sows and lambs sacrificed; cakes called *ficla* and
    *strucla*; wine poured; the brothers' meat shares laid on bread. Pork and bread in Umbria's oldest text.
11. **Pigeons in the rock** (Orvieto). Families bred pigeons in niche-lined caves under their houses for meat in
    a siege and dung for the vines. It arrives at *piccione alla leccarda*, and the cave olive press shows oil.
12. **The Ceri meal** (Gubbio, 15 May): *baccalà alla ceraiola* and lamb's *coratella* eaten by the runners
    under the arcades, and the *tavola bona* in the Palazzo dei Consoli.
13. **Carp from Bracciano** (1710). Twenty-six carp brought to Trasimeno became the lake's "regina", and it is
    cooked *in porchetta* with wild fennel, the pork recipe given to a fish.
14. **Fennel against rosemary.** Umbrian porchetta uses wild fennel; the Ariccia IGP specification requires
    rosemary, garlic and pepper. That is a checkable way to tell the two apart.
15. **The Cazzotto that became a Kiss** (Perugia, 1922). Luisa Spagnoli rescued broken hazelnuts from the
    production line and made a knobbly chocolate called "the punch". Giovanni Buitoni renamed it; Federico
    Seneca added the love notes in the 1930s. Six years later she was combing angora rabbits.
16. **Salt, bread and a fortress** (Perugia, 1540). Use only as the story Perugians tell: the salt tax, the lost
    war, the Rocca Paolina over the Baglioni houses, and bread without salt ever since. State plainly that
    historians find no source for the bread, and that Florence ate unsalted bread first (Dante's "pane altrui").
17. **The wolf of Gubbio was fed** (*Fioretti* 21, legend). Francis's peace terms were a food pact: the town
    would feed the wolf for life so hunger would not drive it to kill. Usable only if labelled as legend.

## Errors found in the old text (summary)
- Black truffle season "November to March": Umbrian law says 1 December–15 March.
- Piano Grande "around 1,400 m": the plain is ~1,270 m, the village 1,452 m (the IGP gives 1,400 m as the
  cultivated upland's average).
- "Unusually for Italy, wild boar": Italy has ~1.5 million boar.
- Surgery "since they owned the sharp knives": the documented link is castration technique (Preci school).
- Lentils "the compulsory New Year's Eve dish with cotechino": a pan-Italian custom; cotechino is northern.
- "An Umbrian invention as much as a Roman one" (porchetta): contested, not verifiable.
- Unsalted bread: the Salt War origin is legend; unsalted bread predates 1540 in Tuscany.
- Bacio "wrapped since 1922 with a love note": the notes came in the 1930s (Seneca). The name was changed from
  "Cazzotto" by Giovanni Buitoni.
- "Confectionery since the nineteenth century": unsupported.
- Assisi lead "made a saint two years later, and the year after that the church began": the foundation stone
  was laid the day after canonisation, 17 July 1228.
- 1997: "two surveyors" were two Soprintendenza technicians; "tens of thousands of fragments" was over 300,000.
- "French Gothic": early Italian Gothic with French influence.
- Orvieto façade "Maitani from 1309": documented 1310.
- "Michelangelo studied it before the Sistine ceiling": Vasari ties Signorelli to Michelangelo's Last
  Judgement, not the ceiling.
- Pozzo di San Patrizio "sunk in the 1520s": 1527–1537. It has two helical ramps, not staircases.
- Sanguineto and Ossaia "blood and bones": folk etymology.
- Marmore "one of the tallest waterfalls in Europe": the tallest man-made one.
- Norcia truffle fair "February": late February to early March (Nero Norcia).
