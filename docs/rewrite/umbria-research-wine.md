# Umbria wine readings: fact check (research only, no prose)

Checked 2026-09-15. Verdicts: **confirmed / corrected / overstated / legend / not verifiable**.
[producer] marks a producer's own account of its own history.

## A. Reading 1, "The landlocked vineyard"

### 1. Only peninsular region with no coast, and no foreign border
**Confirmed.** Umbria is the only Italian region with neither a coastline nor an international border. Its neighbours are Toscana, Lazio and Marche.
- it.wikipedia Umbria: «l'unica regione italiana a non avere contemporaneamente né sbocchi sul mare né confini internazionali» — https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbria
- https://www.focusjunior.it/scuola/geografia/geografia-ditalia-lumbria/

### 2. "Wettest, greenest, least built-up part of central Italy"; «cuore verde d'Italia»
- **Wettest: not verifiable, and probably wrong.** Annual rainfall is 700–1,100 mm over 80–100 rainy days, which is ordinary for central Italy. No source makes Umbria the wettest. (it.wikipedia Umbria)
- **Greenest: overstated.** Forest index 45% (INFC 2015; another reading gives 49%). That makes Umbria fourth in Italy, behind Liguria (72%), Trentino-Alto Adige (54.7%) and **Toscana (52%)**. Toscana is greener. https://www.ilmessaggero.it/umbria/umbria_ancora_cuore_verde_record_boschi_meta_territorio_coperto_boschi-9590778.html
- **Least built-up: partly supported.** ISPRA: 5.3% of land consumed (44,508 ha), against 7.17% nationally in 2024. That is below the national average, but I found no central-Italy ranking. https://www.umbriajournal.com/ambiente/consumo-di-suolo-in-umbria-dati-stabili-sotto-la-media-nazionale-578169/ ; https://www.snpambiente.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Sintesi-rapporto-consumo-di-suolo-2024-ISPRA-SNPA.pdf
- **Nickname: corrected origin (it is a tourism slogan).**
  - It was coined in the **1970s** by the adman **Serena Ciai** for the regional tourism assessor **Alberto Provantini**, as «L'Italia ha un cuore verde, l'Umbria». Ciai says she was paid «quattro lire».
  - In 2022 the Region adopted «Umbria Cuore Verde d'Italia» as its umbrella brand.
  - **Carducci is a false attribution.** His ode *Alle fonti del Clitunno* says «Salve, Umbria verde», not "heart of Italy".
  - Sources: https://passaggimagazine.it/ecco-come-nacque-il-cuore-verde-il-racconto-di-chi-lo-invento-per-poche-lire/ ; https://www.regione.umbria.it/turismo/marchio-collettivo-umbria-cuore-verde-d-italia ; https://blog.emmavillas.com/umbria-cuore-verde-italia/

### 3. Hills "between 200 and 500 m"; Sibillini on the eastern edge
**Confirmed as an approximation.**
- The Montefalco Sagrantino zone runs from 220 to 472 m (quattrocalici, from the disciplinare).
- The Orvieto disciplinare requires vineyards at 100–500 m.
- Fronzi (1915) described Montefalco's hills as «da 400 a 500 metri».
- The Sibillini form the Umbria–Marche border. Monte Vettore (2,476 m) is the highest point.
- Sources: https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/montefalco-sagrantino-docg/ ; Orvieto DOC disciplinare art. 4 (Regione Umbria PDF below); https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbria

### 4. Soils
**Overstated (simplified).**
- **Montefalco.** The disciplinare (art. 9) lists a mosaic: marls, lacustrine clays and sands (Plio-Pleistocene), alluvium, sandstones, calcarenites. "Clay and limestone" is a fair shorthand, but not the whole picture.
- **Torgiano.** Deep clay-loam, moderately calcareous soils on Villafranchian sands and clays. https://consorziotutelavinitorgiano.it/territorio/
- **Orvieto.** The disciplinare (art. 9) names *both* marine and sublittoral clays (Pliocene–Pleistocene) *and* volcanic formations (lavas, tuffs). The town's rock is tuff and pozzolana, but many vineyards sit on clay; Antinori calls Castello della Sala's soil «clay-based, rich in marine fossils». Better wording: "volcanic tuff and marine clays".
- **Trasimeno.** Mixed. The disciplinare has lacustrine sediments, with clay on the higher ground and sand lower down, and deltaic sands in the west. "Sandier lake soils" is **confirmed** for the lower slopes.
- Sources: Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG disciplinare (link at item 28); https://www.disciplinare.it/colli-del-trasimeno-o-trasimeno-doc.html

### 5. Trasimeno fourth-largest lake, shallow
**Confirmed.** Fourth-largest lake in Italy and the largest in peninsular Italy.
- Area 125–128 km², surface at 258 m.
- **Maximum depth 6.3 m**, mean about 4.3 m.
- Treccani: «profondità massima di appena 6,30 m» — https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/trasimeno/

### 6. "About 12,000 hectares" of vineyard
**Confirmed (round figure).**
- Regione Umbria: «circa 12.700 ettari», 1.8% of the national vineyard.
- Quattrocalici gives 12,100 ha.
- Average holding is «poco superiore all'ettaro».
- Sources: https://www.regione.umbria.it/agricoltura/vitivinicoltura ; https://www.quattrocalici.it/regione/umbria/

### 7. Two DOCGs; Torgiano rules and dates
**Confirmed.**
- **Torgiano DOC 1968.** The first DOC in Umbria (Rosso and Bianco).
- **Torgiano Rosso Riserva DOCG:** DPR 20 Oct 1990 (G.U. 11 Mar 1991), retroactive to the 1983 vintage.
- **Riserva rules:**
  - Sangiovese 70–100%, other non-aromatic reds up to 30%.
  - Ageing «almeno tre anni, di cui almeno sei mesi in bottiglia», counted from 1 November of the vintage.
  - The whole comune of Torgiano.
- **Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG:** DM 5 Nov 1992.
- Sources: https://www.disciplinare.it/torgiano-rosso-riserva-docg.html ; https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/torgiano-rosso-riserva-docg/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Lungarotti

### 8. Orvieto DOC grapes, date, two regions
**Corrected (grape wording).**
- **Grapes (art. 2).** «Trebbiano Toscano (Procanico) e Grechetto minimo 60%», counted *together*, with other white varieties approved for Umbria and the province of Viterbo up to 40%. Verdello, Drupeggio and Malvasia are not named in art. 2; they belong to that permitted 40%. "Mostly Trebbiano" is not required: the 60% can be any mix of the two.
- **Date.** DOC by DPR **7 Aug 1971**, last modified 2011.
- **Two regions: confirmed.**
  - Umbria: 13 comuni in the province of Terni (Orvieto, Allerona, Baschi, Ficulle, Porano and others).
  - Lazio: 5 comuni in the province of Viterbo (Castiglione in Teverina, Civitella d'Agliano, Graffignano, Lubriano, Bagnoregio).
  - There is a Classico subzone.
- **Styles.** secco, abboccato, amabile, dolce, superiore, vendemmia tardiva, muffa nobile. Muffa Nobile grapes need ≥16% potential natural alcohol.
- Source: https://www.regione.umbria.it/documents/18/1934957/DOC+Orvieto.pdf/9e9b25c9-be9d-46b5-bfd8-57ecca2dfbac

### 9. Trebbiano Spoletino
**Confirmed in substance; "field maples" needs softening.**
- **Distinct variety.** Genetically «distinto sia dal Trebbiano Toscano sia dal Trebbiano Abruzzese». In the national register since 1970. About 200 ha. https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/trebbiano-spoletino/
- **How it was grown.** Traditionally as *vite maritata*, trained up **elms or maples**, between Montefalco, Spoleto and Trevi. https://vinodabere.it/trebbiano-spoletino-un-vitigno-riscoperto-da-cui-si-ricavano-vini-di-carattere/
- **The rescue.**
  - Clonal work in the 1980s; the real revival in the last 15–20 years.
  - Research by Tabarrini and the now-defunct Novelli.
  - **Collerisana (Spoleto) bottled it first, in 1992.**
  - Antonelli made massal selections from old married vines from 2007 and replanted Vigna Tonda in 2016 [producer].
  - Source: https://www.lucianopignataro.it/a/la-storia-del-trebbiano-spoletino-raccontata-nel-trebium-e-nel-vigna-tonda-di-antonelli-san-marco/282152/
- **Spoleto DOC:** DM 27 June 2011 (G.U. 13 July 2011). https://www.winemag.it/doc-spoleto-in-gazzetta-ufficiale-trebbiano-spoletino-futuro/

### 10. How the caves made Orvieto sweet; number of caves
**Corrected mechanism: slow cold fermentation in the caves. Noble rot formed in the vineyards, not in the grottoes.**
- **The cellars (disciplinare art. 9).**
  - It describes the Etruscan-origin cellar «a tre piani»: grapes crushed at ground level, must running down clay pipes to ferment below, wine moved lower still to mature.
  - It says this «garantiva ... un prodotto amabile, frizzante».
  - For Vendemmia Tardiva the same text still allows «una lenta fermentazione che si attenua nei mesi freddi».
- **Why it stayed sweet.** Consortium and AIS: the cold of the grottoes made fermentations so slow that they often stopped, leaving residual sugar, i.e. abboccato.
- **Noble rot.**
  - It develops **on the vine**, where autumn fogs and afternoon wind alternate.
  - Ministry inspector **Giorgio Garavini**, delimiting the zone in **1931**, noted *muffa nobile* and that abboccato was the most prized and widespread type.
  - en.wikipedia's line that botrytis settled on grapes *stored in the grottoes after harvest* is unsourced. I would not use it.
- **Caves: confirmed.** «più di 1200 cavità artificiali» under the town, censused by speleologists from the late 1970s. These are cellars, wells, cisterns and quarries, not all wine cellars. https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotterranei_di_Orvieto
- Other sources: https://www.orvietodoc.it/storiaorvietodoc/ ; https://www.aisumbria.it/articolo/loro-etrusco-di-orvieto

### 11. "The wine the popes drank when they took refuge here"
**Overstated.** The epithet «vino dei papi» is traditional, but I found no document of a pope's taste for Orvieto *white*.
- **Popes resident at Orvieto:** Urban IV (from 1262), Gregory X (1272–73), Martin IV (1281–84), Nicholas IV (1290–91), Boniface VIII (1297). **Clement VII** took refuge there after the Sack of Rome, 1527–28. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orvieto_and_the_popes
- **Sante Lancerio**, cellarer to **Paul III**, praises a **red** from Sucano (Sugano), two miles from Orvieto: «Sucano è un castelletto distante da Orvieto due miglia, e dopo il vino Monterano non ha pari bevanda per vino rosso ... Di questo vino S.S. beveva volentieri, massime quando era in Orvieto.» https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugano
- **Gregory XVI deathbed legend:** not found.
- **Side find (Montefalco, not Orvieto).** On **31 Oct 1544** Montefalco's accounts record a gift of wine to Paul III: «pro vino greco condonato s.d.n. bononenos eptem». Source: the regional register sheet at item 21.

### 12. Signorelli "said to have been paid in wine"
**Confirmed that wine was part of the pay, not the whole of it.**
- **Contract of 5 April 1499.** 1911 Britannica, drawing on the cathedral archive: «to complete the ceiling for 200 ducats, and to paint the walls for 600, along with lodging, and in every month two measures of wine and two quarters of corn.» https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Signorelli,_Luca
- **Second contract.** The walls were allotted in late April 1500 for **575 ducats**. Finished by 1502, paid until 1504. https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappella_di_San_Brizio
- **Annual quantity.** AIS Umbria: the Opera supplied «dodici some di vino (circa mille litri)» a year. (This fits two *some* a month if work was not continuous; the sources don't reconcile it.) https://www.aisumbria.it/articolo/loro-etrusco-di-orvieto
- **Pinturicchio.** His 1496 contract is reported as «sei quartenghi di grano ... e il vino necessario», and elsewhere as «vin d'Orvieto quantum libuerit». Secondary sources only; Treccani DBI does not give the terms. Treat as **legend-adjacent**.
- **Beware.** Italofile's "contract with Pope Urban IV" is impossible (Urban IV died in 1264).

### 13. "<5% off-dry or sweet"; market turned dry in the 1970s; consortium; noble-rot revival
- **<5%: repeated but not primary-sourced.** en.wikipedia Orvieto DOC: «less than five percent of total production», no citation. The consortium publishes no split. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orvieto_DOC
- **"Dry in the 1970s": not verifiable.** The consortium dates the shift from semi-sweet to dry vaguely, starting in the 19th century. Say "in the second half of the twentieth century".
- **Consortium: confirmed.**
  - Founded **1958** as «Consorzio del Vino Tipico di Orvieto».
  - Renamed Consorzio Tutela in **1971**.
  - Superiore introduced 1997; Rosso Orvietano DOC 1998.
  - https://www.orvietodoc.it/consorzio-orvietodoc/
- **Revival.**
  - Claudio Barbi of **Decugnano dei Barbi** made **Pourriture Noble in 1981**, «the first noble rot produced in Italy» [producer]. Growers had until then picked early to avoid botrytis. https://www.decugnanodeibarbi.com/en/our-story
  - **Antinori's Muffato della Sala**: first vintage **1987** (Sauvignon 50, Grechetto 30, Drupeggio 20) [producer]. It is **Umbria IGT, not Orvieto DOC**. https://www.antinori.it/en/vino/muffato-della-sala-en/
  - The DOC itself now has a *Muffa Nobile* type.

### 14. Torgiano and Lungarotti
**Confirmed, with dates.**
- **Place.** Torgiano stands on a hill where the **Chiascio meets the Tiber**; the name is traditionally *turris amnium*. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torgiano
- **Giorgio Lungarotti (1910–1999).**
  - Moved from sharecropping to direct management in the late 1940s and early 1950s, and planted specialised vineyards.
  - **First wines, Rubesco and Torre di Giano, from the 1962 harvest.** The name Rubesco came from his wife Maria Grazia Marchetti (*rubescere*).
  - **Rubesco Riserva Vigna Monticchio: first vintage 1964.**
  - Sources [producer]: https://lungarotti.it/eng/rubesco62/ ; https://www.intravino.com/primo-piano/rubesco-riserva-vigna-monticchio-di-lungarotti-tutta-la-storia-dal-1964-a-oggi/
- **"Bottled when almost everyone sold in bulk": supported.** Intravino and Civiltà del bere describe Umbria then as a bulk-wine region. There are no counts. Soft producer/press consensus.
- **"Inland Sangiovese could age for decades": supported but a judgement.** Intravino's vertical found the 1975 and 1977 Monticchio still alive in the 2020s.

### 15. MUVIT and MOO
**Confirmed.**
- **MUVIT.** Opened **23 April 1974** by Giorgio Lungarotti and Maria Grazia Marchetti, in the agricultural wing of the **17th-century Palazzo Graziani-Baglioni**. 20 rooms, more than 3,000 objects.
- **MOO** (olive and oil museum) opened **2000**, in an old oil mill. The Lungarotti Foundation dates from 1987.
- **Outside verdict.** "The best wine museum in Italy" is attributed to the **New York Times** by WineNews and by Lungarotti. **I could not locate the NYT article itself.** Quote it as "called by the New York Times…" only with that caveat. The Drinks Business put it in a world top ten.
- Sources: https://winenews.it/en/the-museo-del-vino-of-torgiano-turns-50-between-events-and-photographic-exhibitions_524338/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_del_vino_(Torgiano) ; https://cultura.gov.it/luogo/museo-dell-olivo-e-dell-olio

### 16. Gamay del Trasimeno = Grenache; DOC dates
**Confirmed.**
- **Identity.** «gli studi genetici condotti negli anni Duemila hanno dimostrato che il Gamay del Trasimeno è in realtà identico alla Grenache» (quattrocalici). The consortium: same family as Cannonau, Granaccia, Tai Rosso. The University of Perugia was involved, but I found no named paper.
- **The name.** Probably from the French-style *alberello* training, which recalled Beaujolais Gamay. Documented locally from the late 1800s.
- **Arrival: legends, two versions.** Sardinian shepherds in the mid-1800s (it.wikipedia), or Eleonora Alarcón y Mendoza in the 1400s (Winemag).
- **Colli del Trasimeno DOC:** DPR **13 Jan 1972**. Now "Colli del Trasimeno o Trasimeno".
- **Colli Altotiberini DOC:** DPR **22 Jan 1980**. San Giustino, Citerna, Città di Castello, Monte Santa Maria Tiberina, Montone, Umbertide, Gubbio, Perugia.
- Sources: https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/gamay-del-trasimeno/ ; https://www.trasimenodoc.it/trasimeno-gamay/ ; https://www.winemag.it/gamay-del-trasimeno-grenache-dumbria-caccia-di-identita/ ; https://www.assovini.it/italia/umbria/item/520-colli-del-trasimeno-doc ; https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colli_Altotiberini

### 17. Olive belt Assisi–Spoleto; Umbria DOP
**Confirmed; "terraces older than the vineyards" not verifiable.**
- **FAO GIAHS, July 2018.** "Olive groves of the slopes between Assisi and Spoleto", **the first Italian GIAHS site**. 9,000 ha across six comuni: Assisi, Spello, Foligno, Trevi, Campello sul Clitunno, Spoleto. https://www.fao.org/giahs/giahs-around-the-world/italy-assisi-spoleto-olive-groves/mediterranean-diet-agricultural-heritage-as-smooth-as-oil/en
- **National register of historic rural landscapes, January 2018.** The register gives 6,142 ha and about 1.24 million trees. Length is given as "60 km" (WineNews) or "over 40 km" (fasciaolivata.it). https://www.umbriagricoltura.it/paesaggi-rurali-storici-la-fascia-olivata-assisi-spoleto-nel-registro-nazionale/
- **Umbria DOP oil (1997).** The first DOP covering a whole region. Five subzones: Colli Assisi-Spoleto, Colli Martani, Colli Amerini, Colli del Trasimeno, Colli Orvietani.
- **Cultivars.** In Colli Assisi-Spoleto **Moraiolo is at least 60%**, with Leccino and Frantoio up to 30%. https://www.quattrocalici.it/denominazioni/umbria-dop/
- **Age of the terraces.** Only vague claims ("two millennia", «ulivi secolari»). No dated source.

### 18. Umbrian Sangiovese deeper and more tannic than Tuscan
**Not verifiable.** No study or authoritative source found; only blogs, and those mostly about Montefalco Rosso, where Sagrantino in the blend explains the colour. Drop it, or confine it to Montefalco Rosso.

## B. Reading 2, "Sagrantino: Italy's most tannic red"

### 19. Most tannic grape in Italy, "above Nebbiolo, above Tannat"
**Confirmed with a caveat: "highest measured in the studies", not a formal national ranking.**
- **Arapitsas et al., *Molecules* 2021**, 133 commercial reds:
  - Sagrantino epicatechin-phloroglucinol «928 mg/L (±303), which was more than double in respect to the second wine group, Cabernet Sauvignon (437 ± 95 mg/L)».
  - Highest procyanidin B1 (62 mg/L).
  - Highest mean degree of polymerisation (6.9), close to Tannat (6.4).
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7922431/
- **Fondazione Mach (Mattivi) for Caprai.** Sagrantino polyphenol values were «marcatamente più elevati rispetto a quelli di tutte le altre varietà presenti nella banca dati dell'Istituto Agrario di San Michele all'Adige». Colour intensity was among the highest. Caprai 2007 book [producer-published, scientific content]: https://www.arnaldocaprai.it/files/it/impresa-saperi-territorio/Impresa-Saperi-Territorio-Arnaldo-Caprai.pdf
- **Belfrage MW** (World of Fine Wine): «the highest level of polyphenols of any quality grape in the world». https://worldoffinewine.com/news-features/sagrantino-montefalco-italian-red-wine-umbria

### 20. Thick skins, small berries, high pip-to-pulp ratio
**Partly confirmed; pip ratio not verifiable.**
- Bunch small, 120–180 g, compact.
- Berry «medio-piccolo, sferoidale, buccia ... blu-nero, pruinosa» (regional register).
- Quattrocalici: skin «particolarmente spessa»; 2–3 seeds per berry, medium to small.
- Caprai/Mattivi: tannins are extracted «essenzialmente dalla buccia», almost completely within five days. The tannin is a skin story, not a seed story.
- No source measures a high seed-to-pulp ratio. Say "small berries, thick skins".

### 21. Name; Franciscans from Asia Minor; other origins; DNA
- **Name from *sacer*: legend (traditional etymology).** The regional register sheet says the name recalls the passito drunk at religious feasts. The disciplinare: «vino sacro perché vino della festa religiosa».
- **Franciscans from Asia Minor: legend.**
  - Recorded as local belief by Dr Fronzi in 1915: «forse da uno dei numerosi seguaci di San Francesco».
  - The current disciplinare repeats it cautiously: «più probabilmente è stato portato attorno al XIV–XV secolo ... dai frati francescani di ritorno dai loro viaggi di predicazione in Asia Minore».
- **Other stories.**
  - Byzantine monks from Greece (quattrocalici).
  - Pliny's *Itriola* grape of Bevagna and Montefalco. The disciplinare says it is probably *not* Sagrantino.
  - A link with Swiss "Heiden" brought by Saracens: «non hanno trovato nessun riscontro».
  - No Spanish-origin source found.
- **DNA: not verifiable.** No published parentage found. Sources call it without known relatives: «non ha omologhi in Italia e nel mondo» (Caprai 2007). Quattrocalici's "records from about 1100" is not backed.
- Sources: register sheet https://biodiversita.umbria.parco3a.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Scheda-Iscrizione-Registro-Regionale-vite-Sagrantino.pdf ; https://www.quattrocalici.it/vitigni/sagrantino/

### 22. Earliest documents
**Confirmed; many dated sources exist.**

All quotations below are from the Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG disciplinare, art. 9, and the regional register sheet.
- **1088.** Oldest document in the area dealing with vineyards.
- **19 Oct 1315.** A local document lists every vineyard task: «pastinare, plantare, ... potare, zappare, palare, ligare ... vendemmiare, uvam pistare». Cited by S. Nessi; quoted in Caprai 2007.
- **1424.** Last revision of the Montefalco statutes. Book IV includes «della pena di dà danno nella vigna», «della pena di chi porta i pali dalle vigne altrui», «della pena di chi vende l'uve senza licenzia».
- **1540.** The council fixes the harvest date: «quod nulla persona possit vendemiare usque ad x dies mensis sectembris».
- **1549.** Earliest mention of the grape. An order for Sagrantino must by «l'ebreo Guglielmo, mercante di Trevi e di sua moglie Stella». Source: A. Toaff, *Il vino e la carne*, Bologna 1989, p. 97.
- **30 April 1575.** Foligno notarial contract: «quattuor pergulis sagrantini».
- **August 1598.** The Assisi jurist **Bartolomeo Nuti** writes: «Un altro modo di fare il vino rosso è in Foligno. Se metta in una botte ... sagrantino, o, uva negra sgranata ...». Sagrantino was used to "govern" red wines for colour and flavour. Some web pages call him "Giovan Maria Nuti"; the disciplinare says Bartolomeo.
- **Conflict to know about.** The older regional register sheet (2014) claims the name «non compare mai nei documenti antichi» before the late 19th century. The 1549, 1575 and 1598 finds (Metelli, Guarino, *Archivi in Valle Umbra* 2002) overturn that.
- DOCG disciplinare PDF: https://www.consorziomontefalco.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Disciplinare-Montefalco-Sagrantino-DOCG.pdf

### 23. Passito for feast days; drying
**Confirmed for Christmas and Easter and family occasions. Weddings and attics not found.**
- **Disciplinare art. 9.**
  - «non c'era contadino che trascurasse di far crescere nella vigna il prezioso vitigno ... da far passire sui graticci di canne (le camorganne) fino a Natale per poi sgranarli manualmente e premerli in torchi di ridotte dimensioni, lasciando invecchiare il dolce nettare per almeno due anni.»
  - It «consacrava la tavola in occasione del Natale e della Pasqua».
  - The passito «non aveva mercato». It was kept for home feasts.
- **Caprai 2007:** «un vino rosso passito bevuto in loco, come da tradizione secolare, la mattina di Pasqua».
- **Lamb pairing:** press repetition only.
- **Vines on house walls.** Sagrantino pergolas («piérgole») climbed house walls in Montefalco; old vines were kept in the monasteries of Santa Chiara and San Leonardo.

### 24. "By the 1960s a few dozen hectares"
**Corrected.**
- «all'inizio degli anni '70 ... era praticamente scomparso: ne erano rimasti **meno di dieci ettari**» (Caprai 2007); Belfrage agrees.
- **66 ha of DOCG vineyard in 1992**; about 390 ha today.
- Sources: https://worldoffinewine.com/news-features/sagrantino-montefalco-italian-red-wine-umbria ; Caprai PDF (item 19)

### 25. Caprai, and who bottled dry Sagrantino first
- **Arnaldo Caprai: corrected details.**
  - A cashmere and textile manufacturer. In **1971** he bought «una tenuta agricola di pochi ettari»; Wine Spectator says 108 acres, 10 of them vines.
  - He **died 4 January 2026, aged 92.**
  - **Marco took over management in 1987** (company book; Wine Spectator says he joined in 1986).
  - The university partner was **Leonardo Valenti of the University of Milan**: first visit 1989, collaboration from 1990, clonal selection from 1990. **Not Attilio Scienza.** Enologist Attilio Pagli from 1991.
  - Three clones were registered in 2003.
- **"25 Anni".** First vintage **1993**, released **1996** for the estate's 25th anniversary. It won Tre Bicchieri in 1996, reportedly the first 100% Sagrantino to do so; the 1996 release makes the 25-year arithmetic work.
- **Ownership now.** On **1 April 2026 Angelini Wines & Estates bought control**; Marco keeps 25% and stays president. https://www.umbria24.it/economia/la-arnaldo-caprai-non-sara-piu-dei-caprai-la-compra-angelini-wineestates/ ; https://www.winespectator.com/articles/arnaldo-caprai-obituary
- **First dry Sagrantino.**
  - The earliest record is the «Sagrantino Asciutto» entered by **Senator Rolandi Ricci, owner of Cantina Scacciadiavoli**, at the **Mostra Regionale di Vini ed Olii, Montefalco, 13–20 Sept 1925** (disciplinare art. 9).
  - Commercial dry wine came «solo a partire dai primi anni del 1970».
  - The cooperative enopolio that became **Terre de' Trinci** keeps dry bottles from **1972, 1973, 1975** and claims to be first [producer]. The regional sheet says the cooperative made the first dry version «verso la fine degli anni settanta».
  - Lodovico Mattoni is also named as a 1970s pioneer.
  - Adanti and Antonelli were bottling in the 1970s (WineWithSeth, weak). Antonelli moved to bottling in 1979 [producer].
  - **Caprai was not first but is credited with the breakthrough.**

### 28. DOCG ageing and passito rules
**Corrected.** Current text, last amended by DM 3 July 2023.
- **Ageing, secco and passito:**
  - «almeno trentatre mesi, a decorrere dal 1° dicembre dell'anno di produzione delle uve, di cui - per la sola tipologia Secco - almeno dodici mesi in botti di rovere di qualsiasi dimensione».
  - **Plus** «un ulteriore periodo di affinamento in bottiglia di almeno quattro mesi».
  - So "37 months" is the effective minimum (33 + 4), but it should be stated as 33 months including 12 in oak, then 4 in bottle. Passito: the same 33 + 4, **no wood requirement: confirmed**.
- **Passito drying: corrected.** The disciplinare sets **no minimum drying time**. It allows natural drying plus humidity control and cooling, and forbids heating: «E' comunque vietato il ricorso al riscaldamento». "At least two months on racks" is the consortium's description of practice, not a rule.
- **Passito numbers:**
  - Grapes after drying: ≥17% potential alcohol.
  - Wine: ≥18% total alcohol, residual sugar 80–180 g/l.
- **Secco:** ≥13% (13.5% with "Vigna").
- **Vineyards:** max 8 t/ha, new plantings ≥4,000 vines/ha, hill sites only.
- Source: https://www.consorziomontefalco.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Disciplinare-Montefalco-Sagrantino-DOCG.pdf

### 29. Montefalco Rosso DOC rules
**Current (from the 2016 harvest):**
- **Sangiovese 60–80%, Sagrantino 10–25%**, other red grapes from the regional list up to 30%. The consortium writes "15–30%", which cannot add up at the maximums; treat it as "up to".
- Previous rule: Sangiovese 60–70%, Sagrantino 10–15%.
- The 1979 original: Sangiovese 60–75%, Sagrantino 5–10%, Trebbiano Toscano 15–20% (a white grape in the red).
- **Ageing** (2014 text, check that it is unchanged): Rosso 18 months from 1 Nov; Riserva 30 months, 12 in wood.

## Wanted stories found (sourced, usable to open or close a chapter)

1. **The painter's wine ration (Orvieto).** On 5 April 1499 Signorelli signed to finish the San Brizio vault for 200 ducats, the walls for 600, «along with lodging, and in every month two measures of wine and two quarters of corn». AIS Umbria reckons twelve *some* of wine a year, about 1,000 litres. (Item 12)
2. **The cellar with three floors.** The Orvieto disciplinare describes the Etruscan-origin tufa cellar: crush on top, ferment below, mature lowest. The cold stalled fermentation and left the wine «amabile, frizzante». (Item 10)
3. **The pope's red from Sucano.** Sante Lancerio: «Di questo vino S.S. beveva volentieri, massime quando era in Orvieto». Paul III preferred a *red* from two miles outside Orvieto. (Item 11)
4. **Garavini's 1931 survey.** A ministry inspector draws Orvieto's boundary and records noble rot and abboccato as the prized style. Fifty years later (1981) Claudio Barbi bottles Pourriture Noble from rot that growers had always picked early to avoid. (Items 10, 13)
5. **The 1540 harvest ban.** Montefalco's council: «quod nulla persona possit vendemiare usque ad x dies mensis sectembris». The 1424 statutes already punished anyone who damaged a vineyard or stole its stakes. (Item 22)
6. **Guglielmo and Stella, 1549.** The first written "Sagrantino" is an order for its must by a Jewish merchant of Trevi and his wife. (Item 22)
7. **Four pergolas, 1575, and a recipe, 1598.** A Foligno lease reserves «quattuor pergulis sagrantini» to the landlord. Bartolomeo Nuti notes Sagrantino thrown into a cask to strengthen red wine. (Item 22)
8. **Racks of reeds until Christmas.** Every peasant kept a few Sagrantino vines. The grapes dried on *camorganne* until Christmas, were destemmed by hand, pressed in small presses and aged two years, for Christmas and Easter at home. Caprai: drunk «la mattina di Pasqua». (Item 23)
9. **The senator's dry wine, 1925.** At the Montefalco regional wine show (13–20 Sept 1925) Senator Rolandi Ricci of Scacciadiavoli entered a «Sagrantino Asciutto», half a century before dry Sagrantino reached the market. (Item 25)
10. **Fewer than ten hectares.** Early 1970s: under 10 ha. 1992: 66 ha. Today about 390 ha. A textile man buys a few hectares in 1971; his son counts the surviving vines in 1989; the "25 Anni" of 1993 wins Tre Bicchieri in 1996. And in 2026, weeks after Arnaldo's death, the family sells control. (Items 24, 25)
11. **The 1315 vineyard calendar.** A Montefalco document lists the year's work in a chain of verbs, «pastinare, plantare, ... potare, zappare, palare, ligare ... vendemmiare, uvam pistare». A possible spoken close. (Item 22)
12. **Two museums for two crops at Torgiano.** Wine museum 1974, oil museum 2000, both founded by a couple who had turned sharecropped hills into bottled wine from 1962. (Items 14, 15)

## Errors in the old text, at a glance
- "Wettest" and "greenest": unsupported (Toscana is more wooded). "Cuore verde" is a 1970s slogan, not Carducci.
- Orvieto grapes: a joint minimum of 60% Trebbiano + Grechetto, not "mostly Trebbiano"; Verdello, Drupeggio and Malvasia are not named.
- Botrytis "settling on grapes laid in the grottoes": wrong. The caves slowed fermentation; noble rot came from vineyard fogs.
- "The wine the popes drank": undocumented for Orvieto white; Lancerio's papal favourite near Orvieto was a red.
- Signorelli was paid in ducats plus lodging, grain and wine, not "in wine".
- "<5% sweet" and "market turned dry in the 1970s": no primary source.
- Muffato della Sala is Umbria IGT (first vintage 1987); the true first botrytis wine was Decugnano's 1981.
- The Umbria-vs-Tuscany Sangiovese comparison: unsourced.
- "Above Nebbiolo, above Tannat": supported by Arapitsas 2021, but "most tannic in Italy" is a study result, not an official ranking.
- The Franciscan-from-Asia-Minor origin is legend; no DNA parentage is published.
- "A few dozen hectares in the 1960s" → fewer than 10 ha in the early 1970s.
- Caprai's partner was Leonardo Valenti (University of Milan), not Scienza. Marco took over in 1987. Dry Sagrantino predates Caprai: 1925 Scacciadiavoli, 1970s cooperative and Mattoni.
- Ageing is 33 months (12 in oak for secco only) plus 4 in bottle. There is no two-month drying minimum in the rules. Passito needs ≥18% alcohol and 80–180 g/l sugar.
- Montefalco Rosso since 2016 is Sangiovese 60–80% and Sagrantino 10–25%.
